## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**Large-Load Queue Nearly Doubles as CenterPoint Pegs 14 GW of Demand Batch-Zero Eligible**

- ERCOT's large-load interconnection queue held 466,497 MW of requests as of June 2026, nearly double the 238,629 MW logged in March 2026 (ercotqueue.com tracker, July 11) — evidence the new Batch Zero process hasn't slowed the rush to file, even as qualification deadlines tightened.
- CenterPoint Energy officials said Tuesday (Utility Dive, July 28) that roughly 14 GW of the company's service-territory large-load requests are likely Batch Zero-eligible, and the utility now expects 50% load growth by the end of 2029, driven largely by data centers.
- The Texas Tribune reported Wednesday (July 29) that PUCT Chairman Thomas Gleeson sent Gov. Abbott a letter asking state lawmakers to expand ERCOT/PUCT authority over data centers ahead of the 2027 session — including direct emergency communication with large loads and mandatory registration; the story was still running via wire pickup in DFW-area outlets like the Denton Record-Chronicle this morning (July 30).
- A separate Denton Record-Chronicle grid dispatch published this morning (July 30) noted ERCOT is meeting record summer demand "for now," citing new renewable capacity — including a 180 MW solar farm from 174 Power Global — as part of what's kept the system stable so far this summer.

**Today's Signal:** The queue data confirms Batch Zero hasn't cooled demand — it's nearly doubled since March — which is exactly the dynamic driving PUCT's push for more legal authority over these loads.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Meta and BlackRock Unveil $14 Billion, 1-Gigawatt El Paso Campus**

- Meta and BlackRock announced Tuesday (July 28) a new strategic venture to build and operate a 1-gigawatt, roughly $14 billion AI data center campus in El Paso; BlackRock-managed funds will hold an 80% stake, with Meta retaining 20%, and a portion of BlackRock's investment financed through about $12.5 billion in debt (Reuters, Meta Investor Relations).
- The 1,000-acre campus is one of the largest single Texas data center commitments announced to date and continues Meta's pattern of using off-balance-sheet financing partners for AI infrastructure buildout.
- Closer to DFW, Community Impact reported Tuesday (July 29) that Westlake officials announced construction of a new data center near Keller and Southlake, to be sited south of the town's existing pump station at 13590 Denton Highway (US 377) — placing a hyperscale-adjacent project directly inside the core DFW metro rather than the exurban ring.
- In Young County, just west of the Wise/Parker county line, the Olney Enterprise reported this morning (July 30) that Stream Data Centers' purchase of Plug Power's 66-acre Graham site — a deal worth up to $76.5 million, first reported last week — is moving closer to finalization as "Project Saltworks," an 867-acre proposed campus.

**Today's Signal:** Meta's El Paso deal shows hyperscalers still favor West Texas for the biggest single commitments, but Westlake's in-town Keller/Southlake project is a reminder that DFW's inner suburbs, not just its exurban counties, are now squarely in play.

## 🌿 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**NRG's Withdrawn Hood County Gas Plant Still Shadows New TCEQ Filings Near DFW**

- NRG's withdrawal of its air permit application for the proposed 1,200-megawatt, gas-fired Tolar Power Center in Hood County (first reported in June by the Fort Worth Star-Telegram and Oil & Gas Watch) remains the most recent confirmed DFW-adjacent permit action; no new Hood, Parker, Wise, Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, or Tarrant county gas-plant or data-center air permit filing was confirmed as newly dated today.
- TCEQ's Title V current-applications tracker shows a data refresh as of July 26, 2026, but no newly posted DFW-area, data-center-linked entries were visible in today's check.
- Public Citizen published a "Texas Data Center Policy Guide" this week explaining the two-track system operators use statewide — New Source Review permits for new construction and Title V operating permits for major sources — context for why critics say smaller, NSR-only filings for on-site gas plants draw less public scrutiny than a single major-source Title V permit would.
- Statewide baseline, per Environmental Integrity Project/Texas Tribune reporting: at least 15 Texas data centers are building their own behind-the-meter gas plants, with combined emissions estimated above 130 million tons of greenhouse gases — the pollution backdrop against which any new DFW-area filing would be judged.

**Today's Signal:** No new DFW-specific TCEQ filing surfaced today — but the NSR-vs.-Title V mechanics Public Citizen just laid out are the same ones critics say let operators avoid the public notice that would otherwise have flagged a filing like NRG's now-withdrawn Tolar permit sooner.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Westlake and Young County Show Two Faces of the Same Land Rush**

- Westlake's newly announced data center near Keller/Southlake (Section 2) puts a project inside Tarrant/Denton counties' built-up core, a contrast to the exurban pattern seen in Hood, Parker, and Wise counties.
- In Fort Worth, the fight continues over the Zoning Commission's July 8 vote (7-4) rejecting the city's proposed data-center ordinance; five council members are now pushing for a temporary moratorium, with a City Council work session update scheduled for August 4 and a possible council vote to follow.
- The Young County/Graham "Project Saltworks" land deal (Section 2) — Plug Power's abandoned hydrogen site repurposed for an 867-acre data center campus — is a brownfield-to-data-center pivot, confirmed today as "moving closer to reality" in local coverage.
- NBC 5 DFW reported this week that opposition groups are running a statewide "week of action," including rallies, committee testimony, and webinars focused on data centers' water use and grid impact — a sign organized opposition is scaling up its tactics beyond individual county hearings.

**Today's Signal:** Fort Worth's Aug. 4 work session and the Young County deal's progress are worth tracking together — one is a city trying to slow approvals down, the other is a county-level deal moving forward regardless.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**No New DFW-Specific Deal Today — Abilene's PowerPlay AI Shows the Model Still Spreading Statewide**

- No new dedicated gas-plant, gas-plus-battery, or behind-the-meter generation announcement tied specifically to a DFW-area campus was confirmed as newly dated today.
- The most recent statewide example remains PowerPlay AI's plan (Pipeline and Gas Journal, July 20) for a 400-megawatt, behind-the-meter data center near Abilene backed by dedicated natural gas infrastructure — outside DFW, but the same design pattern (skip the interconnection queue, build your own gas supply) under scrutiny in the NRG Tolar withdrawal and the statewide behind-the-meter count.
- CenterPoint's disclosure that 14 GW of its large loads are likely Batch Zero-eligible (Section 1) is itself a signal about the alternative: operators willing to wait in ERCOT's queue rather than build on-site generation are betting the new batch process moves faster than the old study process did.
- Regulatory backdrop unchanged: PUCT/ERCOT's pending large-load disclosure rules (Section 1) would require big power users to report curtailment and backup-generation capability, making behind-the-meter deals more visible once finalized.

**Today's Signal:** With no new DFW power-first deal today, the open question is whether operators here follow Abilene's model (build your own gas) or CenterPoint's bet on Batch Zero — Hood County's NRG withdrawal suggests at least one DFW-area operator just chose to step back from the gas-plant route.
