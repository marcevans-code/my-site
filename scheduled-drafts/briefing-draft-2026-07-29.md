## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**PUCT Tells Abbott It Wants More Teeth: Direct Emergency Contact With Data Centers, Mandatory Registration**

- The Texas Tribune reported today (July 29) that PUCT Chairman Thomas Gleeson sent Gov. Greg Abbott a letter this month outlining legislative asks for the 2027 session: authority for ERCOT to communicate directly with large-load customers (not just their utility) during grid emergencies, a requirement that data centers and other large energy consumers register with the state, and extending the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act — which currently covers only power plants and transmission companies — to data centers with foreign-ownership ties.
- Gleeson's letter responds to a directive Abbott issued last month ordering PUCT and ERCOT to review their authority over data centers and ensure the facilities pay their own infrastructure costs rather than raising residents' bills.
- UT-Austin energy researcher Joshua Rhodes told the Tribune data centers will likely be "a central focus of the next legislative session," pointing to how unresolved the question of who pays for new transmission and generation capacity remains.
- Context: ERCOT's Batch Zero large-load interconnection process — covering more than 438,000 MW of queued demand, an estimated 90% of it data centers — closed its initial qualification window July 10–24; the next hard date on the calendar is an August 31 deficiency-cure deadline.

**Today's Signal:** Gleeson's letter is the clearest sign yet that Texas regulators view their current authority as insufficient — expect the 2027 legislative session to be shaped directly by asks like mandatory data-center registration and direct emergency communication.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Galaxy Digital Buys 500 Acres Outside Waco for a Second Texas Campus**

- Data Center Dynamics reported today (July 29) that Galaxy Digital has acquired roughly 500 acres in McGregor (McLennan County), buying the land from the city of McGregor for $7.5 million. The site, known as "Project Merlin," will offer an initial 75MW of grid-connected capacity starting in 2028, with room to grow into a "multi-hundred-megawatt" campus by 2030.
- Galaxy plans to build its own private substation on-site, fund any local transmission upgrades itself, and use closed-loop cooling to limit water use. It would be the company's second Texas campus alongside its 1,500-acre Helios site in Dickens County, where CoreWeave leases the full approved 800MW under a 15-year deal.
- CEO Mike Novogratz framed the purchase as proof of a "disciplined, multi-campus" strategy, citing Galaxy's Dickens County record of "funding local infrastructure" as a selling point to McGregor officials.
- Not a DFW project, but McGregor is the latest sign hyperscale and crypto-adjacent operators are targeting mid-size Texas cities with available land and lighter approval processes — the same dynamic drawing interest to Ellis, Hood, and Parker counties.

**Today's Signal:** Galaxy's pitch to McGregor — "we'll build and fund our own substation" — is the same commitment state regulators are now trying to make mandatory statewide (see Section 1); operators are already selling it voluntarily as a community-relations move.

## 🌿 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**Vantage/VoltaGrid Face Clean Air Act Notice of Intent to Sue Over Alleged "Permit-Splitting" at Two San Antonio Campuses**

- Texas Public Radio's continuing coverage (published July 28, still the top environmental story in Texas data-center circles today) details a July 22 notice of intent to sue filed by the Environmental Integrity Project, Sierra Club, and Public Citizen against Vantage Data Centers and power provider VoltaGrid. The groups allege the companies improperly divided a single data center and its behind-the-meter gas plants and 150+ diesel backup generators into smaller pollution sources specifically to avoid a major-source Clean Air Act permit — and the public notice and comment period that would come with it.
- The two campuses (14270 Omicron Drive and 5207 Rogers Road) sit in Bexar County, which already violates the federal ozone health standard; cited pollutants include particulate matter, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, and formaldehyde.
- The Clean Air Act's 60-day notice period runs to roughly September 20 before a citizen suit could be filed. A TCEQ public hearing on Vantage's proposed federal operating permit for the Omicron Drive site is set for 7 p.m. Aug. 17 in San Antonio. Vantage said it "take[s] environmental responsibilities seriously" and is reviewing the allegations.
- Separately, TCEQ's monthly Commissioners' Agenda Meeting — the formal proceeding where commissioners act on pending permit applications statewide — is on the calendar for tomorrow, July 30; no DFW-area, data-center-linked gas-plant item was confirmed on that agenda as of today's check.

**Today's Signal:** This case is in San Antonio, not DFW, but the "permit-splitting" allegation is a playbook worth watching for Ellis, Hood, Parker, and Wise county filings too — any operator proposing several smaller on-site generators instead of one larger plant should draw the same scrutiny TCEQ and these groups are now applying to Vantage.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Data Center Opposition in Hood County and Beyond Increasingly Framed in Religious and Moral Terms**

- The Texas Tribune published a feature today (July 29) on how religious language has become central to data-center opposition statewide, anchored in Hood County: Granbury-area resident Matt Long has told the county's development commission and City Council that trading "2,100 acres of natural beauty for godless technological complexes" is a spiritual test, citing apocalyptic scripture (Daniel 2, Revelation 13) at packed prayer meetings and public hearings.
- The piece cites a June Texas Politics Project poll finding 71% of Texas Democrats and 44% of Texas Republicans oppose data centers (only 14% of Republicans strongly support them), plus a national Gallup survey putting opposition at 75% of Democrats and 63% of Republicans — evidence the fight increasingly crosses party lines.
- Hood County remains the backdrop for an active moratorium fight over eight proposed data centers and up to nine associated gas plants; the county's existing "sanctuary for the unborn" ordinance has become a talking point for opponents who argue the county isn't equally protective of "the born."
- Fort Worth's City Council still has an Aug. 11 vote scheduled on whether to begin the formal, multi-month process of enacting a data-center development moratorium, following the Zoning Commission's 7-4 rejection of the city's proposed data-center ordinance on July 8.

**Today's Signal:** With opposition polling above 60% among both parties nationally, expect more bipartisan, values-based framing — religion, water, property rights — to keep showing up at county commissioners' courts across DFW's exurban ring well ahead of the 2027 legislative session.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**San Antonio Lawsuit Threat Puts the "Split-Permit" Behind-the-Meter Model on Trial**

- The Vantage/VoltaGrid notice of intent to sue (Section 3) is as much a power-first story as an environmental one: both San Antonio campuses run on-site, behind-the-meter natural gas plants plus more than 150 diesel backup generators, built specifically so the facilities can skip ERCOT's interconnection queue — the same power-first logic behind proposed gas plants in Hood, Parker, and Wise counties.
- Galaxy Digital's McGregor announcement (Section 2) shows the voluntary version of the same trend: the company is committing to build and fund its own substation and transmission upgrades rather than rely solely on ERCOT interconnection timelines.
- No new dedicated gas-plant or gas-plus-battery announcement specific to a DFW-area campus surfaced in today's research; the standing statewide baseline remains the Environmental Integrity Project's count of at least 15 Texas data centers building their own behind-the-meter gas plants.
- Regulatory backdrop: PUCT/ERCOT's pending large-load rules (Section 1) would require big power users to disclose curtailment and backup-generation capability — a disclosure regime that would make deals like Vantage/VoltaGrid's more visible going forward.

**Today's Signal:** The Vantage/VoltaGrid case is the first real legal test of whether splitting a data center's on-site generation into permit-exempt pieces will hold up — a ruling against the companies would raise the compliance cost of the power-first model statewide, DFW included.
