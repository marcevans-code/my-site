# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 6, 2026

(Friday, November 6, 2026)

**Top Story:** Keller settled its fight over the 1.1-million-square-foot Circle T data center in Westlake on Thursday. The developer will pay about $1.9 million and accept enforceable noise limits, and residents will drop their lawsuit. That evening, Texas's incoming governor told CNN the state needs "to stop what we are doing with data centers" until it has laws to protect residents.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🤝 Keller Settles Over Westlake's Circle T Data Center: Noise Limits, a Berm and About $1.9 Million

- The Keller City Council unanimously approved settlement terms Thursday, Nov. 5, with Circle T Owner LP and the Town of Westlake over a 1.1-million-square-foot data center planned along Highway 377 in Westlake, on Keller's border next to the Marshall Ridge neighborhood. The Lakes at Marshall Ridge HOA agreed to end its lawsuit as part of the deal (CBS Texas).
- Keller gets daytime and nighttime noise limits at residential property lines that it can enforce, an independent baseline noise study, and quarterly noise monitoring for the first three years of operation. Circle T also agreed to a 10-foot screening berm, replacement of dead or badly damaged screening trees, and a grading and drainage plan.
- Diesel backup generators must meet EPA Tier 4 emissions standards, and testing is limited to daytime hours.
- The developer will pay $1.5 million for noise monitoring, environmental mitigation and city programs, $300,000 to Keller Fire Rescue for training, equipment and emergency response, and $76,000 toward Keller's legal and expert costs (CBS Texas). "Keller's first responsibility has been, and will always be, to protect our residents," Mayor Ross McMullin said.
- Residents had said they didn't get proper notice of the project and raised concerns about health, quality of life and property values. Keller had sought a temporary injunction to stop further development, which expired in late September, Fox 4 reported. The settlement doesn't stop the project, and Westlake's Town Council still has to approve it at an upcoming meeting. Keller said it would also work with the incoming governor on statewide data center rules (Fox 4).

**Today's Signal:** This is what a neighbor can win when the zoning vote happened in the next town over: limits on noise and generator testing and a check, but not a halt. Settlements like this one may be the clearest guide to what a statewide noise and notice law could require.

### 2. 🗣️ Hinojosa on CNN: "Stop What We Are Doing With Data Centers" Until Texas Has Laws

- In a CNN interview Thursday, Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa said data centers "have moved into mostly rural communities, and there are no rules and laws to protect Texans." She added: "We will have laws to protect Texans, and we need to stop what we are doing with data centers until we have those protections" (Reuters via U.S. News).
- She also said she doesn't oppose the industry outright. "It is not that I hate data centers. I hate the way they are taking advantage of Texas, and that stops when I'm governor," she said, adding that residents should "have a seat at the table to decide if and how these data centers come into their communities" (USA Today). She said Texas can work with Elon Musk's companies, "but it has to be on our terms."
- At her first news conference Thursday, at UT's LBJ School, Hinojosa named her transition team: Joe Madden, a former chief of staff to Harris County Judge Lina Hidalgo, as executive director; former Republican state Rep. Ernest Bailes; Ryan Pollock, executive director of the Texas State Building and Construction Trades Council; and senior adviser Tamala Barksdale (Texas Tribune, Community Impact). Her data center platform includes a requirement for skilled, licensed union labor. She said Abbott had pledged to "fully cooperate" with the transition. She takes office Jan. 19.
- The Tribune also reported Friday that Hinojosa's agency appointments must be confirmed by two-thirds of the Texas Senate, which Republicans still control. Rice University's John Diamond told CBS Austin that the data center sales tax exemption also can't be undone without the Legislature: "The governor can't get rid of that on her own."
- The same day, OpenAI announced new data center commitments: paying for all the power and infrastructure its sites use, a water plan for each site, largely ending nondisclosure agreements with public officials, and a "local compact" with residents near each facility (ABC News). Several match items on Hinojosa's list, including the NDA ban and proof that residents won't pay more.

**Today's Signal:** Hinojosa spelled out her plan Thursday: a pause until the Legislature writes rules, with residents getting a say in projects. But the Tribune's two-thirds detail matters. Thursday's edition said she could reshape TCEQ and the PUC through appointments, and those appointments now look like they'll need Republican votes too.

### 3. 🔌 Oncor's Queue Grows to 795 Requests, With 293 GW of Data Centers

- Oncor reported third-quarter results Thursday, Nov. 5. Its active transmission queue for large commercial and industrial customers held 795 requests at the end of September. About 293 GW of that is data centers, and about 19 GW is other industrial load, including about 4 GW of crypto mining (Oncor 8-K).
- Where Oncor is the transmission provider, Batch Zero has classified about 24 GW as base load, 10 GW of which is already energized, and 21 GW as studied load. Those classifications depend on ERCOT's audit, expected in December. Batch Zero projects aren't in Oncor's current base capital plan, and it doesn't expect a final transmission plan for them until fall 2027.
- Oncor plans about $9.0 billion of capital spending in 2026, the most in its history, and had spent about $6.3 billion through September. It expects its 2027–2031 base plan to be about 20% larger and to finalize it in the first quarter of 2027.
- The release doesn't mention the election. CEO Allen Nye said Oncor looks forward "to working with lawmakers, regulators, landowners, and other stakeholders across Texas."

**Today's Signal:** At 293 GW, Oncor's data center queue is many times anything the North Texas grid could serve soon. Only the 45 GW Batch Zero has sorted so far is close to real, and even that depends on December's audit. Whatever rules come out of January's session will be written for a queue that is mostly speculative.

### 4. ⚖️ The PUC Moves All Five Batch Zero Complaints Forward and Proposes the Standard Large-Load Contract

- After Thursday's open meeting, the PUC issued orders requesting lists of issues in all five Batch Zero complaints against ERCOT. They were filed by Medina County Power NR I (Docket 60356), Rowan Stampede (60396), Mesquite H2 (60410), ACS DC Hicks Field Road (60425) and Skybox Datacenters (60431), whose complaint Skybox said had been left off the agenda (PUC Interchange).
- The Skybox order says the company alleges ERCOT wrongly reclassified its Wichita Falls data center from "base load" to "studied load." Skybox and ERCOT must file their lists of issues by Nov. 19, and staff and other parties may also file. The commission may then adopt a preliminary order setting out what the case will cover (Docket 60431, Item 17).
- The PUC also formally proposed its Standardized Large Load Interconnection Agreement in Project 59999 on Thursday. It would be the standard contract for every large load connecting in ERCOT. Under it, large loads would have to disclose their water and energy use and submit to audits when the PUC or ERCOT requires one. Their transmission capacity would be reallocated if a phased energization runs more than 24 months late. All parties and their affiliates would have to comply with the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act (Project 59999, Item 4).
- Comments and any requests for a public hearing are due Dec. 14. The agreement could still change to match the final transmission cost-recovery rule in Project 58000.

**Today's Signal:** Under the proposed agreement, every large load in ERCOT would have to disclose its water and energy use. That's the gap ERCOT's survey exposed on Wednesday, when a large share of projects couldn't answer the water and cooling questions. The current commissioners can lock that requirement in before Jan. 19, with no new law needed.

### 5. 📉 Microsoft Passes on More Nscale Capacity in Texas, and Analysts Argue Over Hut 8's Selloff

- Microsoft declined an option that would have more than doubled its existing compute contract with Nscale at a Texas data center, Reuters reported Thursday, following the Financial Times. A source said Microsoft decided several months ago, before Nscale's September IPO filing, that the existing Texas contract isn't affected, and that Nscale is talking with other customers about the remaining capacity. Reuters didn't name the site (via Central Oregon Daily).
- Hut 8 reported third-quarter results Wednesday. It said it had agreed to buy two energized West Texas sites from affiliates of Poolin Technology through a Chapter 11 sale, for up to $180 million. The sites have up to 230 MW of utility capacity at full energization, still subject to final power approvals, and the deal could close in the fourth quarter (Hut 8 8-K).
- The stock fell 23% Wednesday and kept falling Thursday, as investors weighed the election's effect on Hut 8's Beacon Point campus in Texas. Cantor Fitzgerald said management told it Beacon Point Phase 1 has no outstanding permits, and Phase 2 needs only a notice of intent for site work. Cantor expects permits to resume between mid-December and mid-January. Needham said it's "modestly less confident" the Dec. 10 audit and the PUC's Dec. 17 sign-off will go as scheduled. All three firms, including Rosenblatt, kept their ratings but cut their price targets (Benzinga).
- Core Scientific reported third-quarter results Thursday, with colocation revenue up nearly 60% from the second quarter, to $215.4 million, and 485 MW billing as of Sept. 30. The release didn't mention Texas permitting (Core Scientific 8-K).
- Cleanview data cited by Bloomberg (via Energy Connects) shows about 40% of pending U.S. data center capacity is in states whose new governors promised to stop or pause construction. GLJ Research's Gordon Johnson said Texas projects without power or environmental permits will "probably go to Louisiana, Oklahoma or nowhere."

**Today's Signal:** The analysts now fall into two camps. One says Texas projects with permits in hand will be fine. The other says even the December dates are now in doubt. Microsoft's Nscale decision came before the election, but it shows that a Texas site with power and a signed tenant can still be left looking for customers to fill the rest.

### 6. 🐄 The Incoming Agriculture Commissioner Wants His Own Data Center Moratorium, and Snopes Checks His Calf Story

- Agriculture Commissioner-elect Clayton Tucker, a Democrat, says he'll bring a statewide moratorium proposal on new data centers to the Legislature in January. "I want a full moratorium," he told KETK, and said the state shouldn't rely on companies' own reports about their electricity and water use.
- On Friday, Snopes published a fact-check of a story Tucker has told since at least May: that every calf born on a Texas ranch has been stillborn since a data center was built next door. Snopes left the claim unrated because it couldn't be verified. In one August telling, Tucker placed the ranch in Fannin County, north of Dallas, and on an Aug. 24 podcast he said the facility was a battery storage plant rather than a data center.
- The Texas Animal Health Commission told Snopes it has received no reports or complaints of cattle stillbirths near data centers or battery storage sites. Snopes said Tucker's own Oct. 17 and Oct. 25 posts acknowledged that "no veterinarian has confirmed the stillbirths" and that no study has linked data center noise to livestock deaths.

**Today's Signal:** The noise complaints near data centers are real, as the Keller settlement shows. But a statewide official whose best-known example can't be traced to a specific ranch hands the industry an easy rebuttal before his bill is even filed.

No new TCEQ news releases since Oct. 21, and no data center or gas plant air permit actions turned up in the eight tracked counties. No new behind-the-meter generation deals were announced in Texas. ERCOT's news releases still stop at Sept. 21, no outlet reported what commissioners discussed at Thursday's PUC meeting, and Bastrop's council action on the Cedar Creek gas plant is still unreported.
