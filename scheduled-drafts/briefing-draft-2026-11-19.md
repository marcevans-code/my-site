# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 19, 2026

(Thursday, November 19, 2026)

**Top Story:** The companies behind two gas-powered San Antonio data centers told a federal judge they followed every TCEQ rule, and that the environmental groups suing them have no constitutional right to bring the case at all.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ Vantage and VoltaGrid Fight a Shutdown of Their San Antonio Gas Plants and Call the Clean Air Act Suit Unconstitutional

- Monday, Nov. 16: Three Vantage Data Centers affiliates and VoltaGrid jointly moved to dismiss the Clean Air Act suit Public Citizen and the Sierra Club filed in September over Vantage's two San Antonio campuses. They argue the law's citizen-suit provision doesn't let groups sue on behalf of their members, and that if it does, it violates Article II and the private nondelegation doctrine, at least as applied here (*Public Citizen v. Vantage Data Centers*, W.D. Tex. No. 5:26-cv-06032, Dkt. 41).
- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Vantage filed its opposition to the groups' amended request for a preliminary injunction. It says TCEQ authorized VoltaGrid's Omicron Drive gas plant in October 2024 and its Rogers Road plant in November 2025, and amended the Omicron registration in July 2026, while Vantage's diesel backup generators run under permit-by-rule registrations filed in February 2024. Vantage argues TCEQ treated the plants and the data centers as separate sources (Dkt. 47).
- The filing describes the sites' status: Vantage's TX11 building has run on about 50 MW from VoltaGrid's Omicron plant since October 2025, and TX12 is under construction. At Rogers, TX21 runs on CPS Energy bridge power (about 24 MW of IT load) plus temporary VoltaGrid trailer engines, TX22 is under construction, and VoltaGrid's Rogers plant isn't operating yet. Vantage puts operating customer load at about 86 MW (Dkt. 47).
- Vantage says it invested "billions of dollars" relying on those authorizations, that more than 1,000 people (about 750 at Omicron and 500 at Rogers) would be put out of work, and that a shutdown "would chill the very investment that permitting is meant to channel." It also argues the groups waited about 31 months after construction began to send notice, and asks that any injunction require a bond. Dollar figures are redacted in the public copy (Dkt. 47). VoltaGrid filed a separate opposition (Dkt. 46).
- In a discovery brief the same day, the defendants called VoltaGrid's trailer-mounted units "temporary nonroad engines governed by a separate federal regime," outside the stationary-source rules the groups rely on (Dkt. 44).
- Magistrate Judge Elizabeth Chestney set an initial pretrial conference for Jan. 11, 2027, and noted that neither the injunction request nor the motion to dismiss has been set for a hearing (Dkt. 48, signed Nov. 17). On Wednesday, Nov. 18, U.S. District Judge Jason Pulliam certified the constitutional challenge to the U.S. attorney general, who can intervene within 60 days (Dkt. 49). The groups' reply on the injunction is due Nov. 23 (Dkt. 34).

**Today's Signal:** Vantage's defense is that it did what TCEQ told it to do, the same week TCEQ restarted data center permitting. The groups' case is that TCEQ's minor-source standard permit was the wrong permit for plants this size. If the constitutional argument wins, it reaches past this case: Public Citizen, which also filed last week's Starbase construction complaint with TCEQ, and groups like it would lose their federal court route, leaving enforcement to TCEQ and the federal government.

### 2. 🧮 ChronoScale and STN Pair Up on a 350 MW Odessa Campus Backed by a Frontier AI Lab Contract Worth Up to $7.5 Billion

- Wednesday, Nov. 18: ChronoScale (Nasdaq: CHRN), Applied Digital's Dallas-based cloud spin-off, agreed to a 50/50 development partnership with STN on STN's Odessa campus. Each side contributes $390 million in value, subject to antitrust (HSR) clearance (ChronoScale 8-K, Ex. 99.1; Data Center Dynamics, Georgia Butler).
- The campus is planned for 350 MW. The first 65 MW phase is ordered for the first half of 2027, and ChronoScale holds a right of first offer on the other 285 MW. The customer, an unnamed "leading frontier AI lab," signed a contract worth up to $7.5 billion over six years, about $20.8 million a year per MW blended across phases. Part of Phase 1 is backed by credit support from an investment-grade technology company (ChronoScale 8-K).
- ChronoScale also lined up up to $750 million: $250 million in equity and up to $500 million in convertible preferred stock, with investors including the AI lab itself, Nutanix and Empery Digital (ChronoScale 8-K; DCD). The 8-K reports a $153 million common stock sale at $8 a share and a warrant for up to 8.1 million shares to an undisclosed GPU services customer (TradingView summary of the 8-K).
- STN's release says the campus will run on on-site generation and battery storage. It names no utility, generation technology or capacity (STN release via National Law Review).

**Today's Signal:** A week ago STN described this campus as 400 MW to start and up to 1.6 GW eventually. Now the contracted plan is 350 MW, with a buyer and financing behind it. What STN still hasn't said is how the on-site power will be generated. If it's gas, it needs a TCEQ air permit, and permits are moving again.

### 3. 🗺️ Southern Dallas County Residents Fear State Rules Will Pile More Data Centers Where They Already Cluster

- Wednesday, Nov. 18: NBC 5 Investigates reported from Joppa, a Freedman's town in southern Dallas, where resident Miriam Matthews lives about a quarter mile from a small data center she says hums all night: "It's noise pollution." Community leader Emmanuel Davis said Dallas has no data center zoning category, so they're allowed by right on most industrial and mixed-use land (NBC 5, Scott Friedman, Eva Parks and Edward Ayala).
- About 10 miles south, Wilmer, Lancaster and Red Oak host hyperscale campuses. Red Oak has nine data centers operating and more than a dozen planned. "I'm surrounded by them at this point," said resident Sergio Chapa. Mayor Mark Stanfill said the city is developing responsibly, and local officials cited tax revenue for roads and lower taxes (NBC 5).
- Beth Garza, ERCOT's former independent market monitor, said it's too early to know whether ERCOT's reviews will increase clustering, but said the community impact process could shrink the Batch Zero list because projects that didn't answer ERCOT's information requests would drop out. ERCOT didn't directly answer whether its studies could create clusters (NBC 5). ERCOT told the PUC on Nov. 5 that 93% of companies had responded (FOX 7 Austin, Sierra Rains).
- Abre' Conner of the NAACP's environmental and climate justice center said places with existing industrial buildout become "sacrifice zones." Five Dallas council members want city rules, and a council committee is expected to take up the issue soon (NBC 5).

**Today's Signal:** If the projects that survive ERCOT's review are the ones that already have power and fiber, they're likely to be in the corridors south of Dallas that already have both. Joppa's problem comes before any state rule: under Dallas zoning, a data center on industrial land needs no hearing, so neighbors get no vote.

### 4. 🏗️ Data Center Construction Starts Fell 28% Last Quarter, but Crusoe's 450 MW Abilene Build Helped Drive October's Rebound

- Tuesday, Nov. 17 (reported Wednesday): Cleanview's building-level data showed U.S. developers started 8.4 GW of data center capacity in the third quarter across 65 buildings, down 28% from a record 11.7 GW in the second quarter but 55% above the same quarter last year. It was the first quarter-over-quarter drop since early 2025 (Blockspace via Yahoo Finance; Data Center Dynamics).
- October alone saw 4.6 GW of starts across 33 buildings. Three campuses made up about 35% of that, including 450 MW at Crusoe's Abilene campus for Microsoft (Blockspace via Yahoo Finance).
- Cleanview said governors in nine states have promised or imposed pauses on new data centers, and those states hold 41% of planned U.S. capacity. Founder Michael Thomas said developers are still starting projects "at twice last year's pace" and that if November and December match October, the fourth quarter would set a record of about 14 GW (Blockspace via Yahoo Finance).

**Today's Signal:** Texas's pauses hit grid connections and permits for projects still waiting. They didn't stop construction on sites that already had what they needed, which is why Abilene could break ground on 450 MW in October. The next test is how many projects waiting on permits can break ground before Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office in January.

### 5. 🔍 Another Company Scouts a Site Outside Hillsboro After Provident Walked Away

- Tuesday, Nov. 17 (reported Wednesday): Hillsboro city staff told the City Council that another company is exploring a data center on a specific property outside city limits in Hill County. The company and the property weren't named (KXXV).
- Officials said the project as now envisioned would be "much smaller" than Provident's proposal, which fell through, and stressed it's separate. They said it "should not yet be considered a firm development" and that most inquiries never get past due diligence (KXXV).
- Because the site is outside city limits and the company hasn't asked Hillsboro for anything, the city said the project isn't currently subject to city regulation (KXXV).

**Today's Signal:** Provident's deal needed the city's land, which gave Hillsboro leverage. A site outside city limits doesn't. Texas counties have no general zoning power, so unless the developer needs city water, sewer or annexation, residents may have little say over whether it gets built.
