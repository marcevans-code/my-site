## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**ERCOT Confirms It Will Miss the Aug. 7 Batch Zero Deadline, Seeks a Do-Over**

- ERCOT has told transmission service providers it will not meet the Aug. 7, 2026 deadline to notify them which large loads cleared Batch Zero classification, and plans to ask the PUCT for a "good cause" exception to push the date back (Troutman Pepper Locke, Aug. 4; Butler Snow, Aug. 4).
- The slippage follows directly from Monday's (Aug. 3) audit order from Gov. Greg Abbott, which prompted ERCOT to pause the Batch Zero transmission planning study it had been running under PUCT Docket 59142 since June.
- Reuters and other outlets (POWER Magazine, Houston Public Media, TechCrunch) confirmed this week that the freeze applies across the roughly 1,800-project, 474-gigawatt interconnection queue — figures regulators have repeated in nationwide coverage of the story.
- No new PUCT filing detailing exactly how ERCOT's "good cause" exception request will be handled had posted as of this morning.

> **Today's Signal:** Even the mechanics of implementing Monday's freeze are already sliding — ERCOT can't hit its own paperwork deadline, let alone complete Abbott's open-ended audit.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Fort Worth Pulls Its Own Presentation as Staff Scramble to Read Abbott's Audit**

- Fort Worth city staff canceled a planned Tuesday council presentation on data-center zoning regulations and a possible moratorium process, telling council members that Assistant City Manager Jesica McEachern is still assessing how Monday's statewide audit order affects locally proposed projects (Fort Worth Report, Aug. 4).
- McEachern told council, "if a project can't get approval to connect to the grid, we don't know really then what that timing looks like to move forward" — a direct acknowledgment that Abbott's freeze is already reshaping timelines for projects already in the local approval pipeline.
- No new hyperscaler land purchase, air-permit filing, or construction start tied to Meta, AWS, Google, or Microsoft was confirmed as newly dated today; the most recent confirmed activity remains Meta and BlackRock's $14 billion, 1-gigawatt joint venture announced July 28 for a campus in El Paso.
- The Data Center Coalition's Monday statement calling the audit an opportunity to "showcase the good actors in the data center industry" remains the industry's only on-record response as of this morning; no individual member company has issued its own statement.

> **Today's Signal:** Corporate activity has gone quiet by comparison — right now builders and cities alike are waiting to see what Abbott's audit actually requires before committing to next steps.

**No New DFW-Area Filing Today — the Water-Use Survey Abbott Cited Is the Live Issue**

- No new Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood County TCEQ draft-permit or public-notice filing tied to a data center or gas plant was confirmed as newly dated today.

- Coverage recirculating this week continues to note at least 15 Texas data centers are pursuing their own behind-the-meter gas plants, part of a broader set of 32 proposed gas-fired data-center plants statewide that researchers project could emit roughly 287 million tons of greenhouse gases annually (The Texas Tribune; KERA News).

> **Today's Signal:** TCEQ hasn't posted anything new today, but its long-ignored water-use survey is now doing double duty as the paperwork trail for Abbott's statewide audit.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Westlake Approves Its Data Center Over Hours of Objection; Denton Moves the Other Way**

- The Town of Westlake's Planning and Zoning Commission approved an 87-acre, 1-million-square-foot data center along Highway 377 near the Keller city line late Tuesday, after a packed public meeting and an executive session; the project was approved with added conditions on sound walls, landscaping, and cooling equipment (NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth, Aug. 4).
- The approved Westlake campus is projected to use about 54 million gallons of water annually — a figure town leaders compared to existing corporate campuses in defending the project against resident objections over noise, water, and quality of life.
- In Denton, the City Council instead voted Aug. 4 to pursue a two-track approach: advancing both a temporary 3-to-6-month moratorium on new data centers and a longer-term regulatory framework (Denton Record-Chronicle; Dallas Business Journal, Aug. 4-5).
- Fort Worth, meanwhile, pulled its own Tuesday zoning presentation to first determine how Abbott's statewide audit affects the Aug. 11 council vote on a possible moratorium — leaving that city's process on hold while Westlake and Denton both moved forward with decisions of their own (Fort Worth Report, Aug. 4).

> **Today's Signal:** Three DFW-area governments took three different paths on the same day — Westlake approved, Denton leaned toward pausing, and Fort Worth paused its own process to figure out what Abbott's order even means for it.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**Texas Leads the Nation in Behind-the-Meter Capacity as Transmission Delays Bite**

- A Reuters Events whitepaper cited today shows Texas leading all states with more than 20.6 gigawatts of planned behind-the-meter generation capacity for data centers, ahead of New Mexico (9.2 GW), Pennsylvania (7.5 GW), and Utah (6.0 GW) (pv magazine USA, Aug. 5).
- Nationally, developers were planning roughly 56 gigawatts of on-site generation as of early 2026 — about 30% of all planned data-center capacity — as multi-year transmission buildouts (six to fifteen years, per the report) push hyperscalers toward gas, solar, storage, and Virtual Power Plants instead of waiting on grid connections.
- Grid operators elsewhere are moving to formalize the trend: the Southwest Power Pool's new "HILLGA" framework offers interconnection agreements in under 90 days to large loads that bring their own generation — a model ERCOT hasn't adopted, but one that Texas's audit-driven grid freeze (Section 1) makes more attractive by the day.
- Separately, Ars Technica reported this week that some Texas data-center operators have used local permitting loopholes to install gas turbines and backup diesel generators on-site without the scrutiny a full grid-connected project would face — the kind of activity Abbott's audit is meant to catch, though the audit applies only to grid-connected projects, not self-generating ones.

> **Today's Signal:** With ERCOT's queue frozen for an unknown duration, today's data confirms Texas developers already have the strongest incentive in the country to skip the grid entirely and self-generate.
