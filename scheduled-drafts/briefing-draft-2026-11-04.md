# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 4, 2026

(Wednesday, November 4, 2026)

**Top Story:** Gina Hinojosa unseated Gov. Greg Abbott after campaigning on a statewide data center moratorium. She'll be the first Democrat to govern Texas since Ann Richards. Democrats also won lieutenant governor, attorney general and a Railroad Commission seat, but Republicans kept the Texas Senate, and that is where the moratorium would have to pass.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🗳️ Gina Hinojosa Wins the Texas Governor's Race After Promising a Data Center Moratorium

- The Associated Press called the race for Hinojosa at 10:17 p.m. Central on Tuesday, Nov. 3. Abbott had already conceded around 10 p.m., telling supporters "mathematically we are not going to be able to win this election." Bisnow reported Hinojosa had more than 4.4 million votes, about 53%, to Abbott's roughly 3.8 million, with 80% of counties reporting.
- Hinojosa, a 52-year-old state representative from Austin, will be the first Democrat elected governor since Ann Richards in 1990 and the state's first Latina governor. Bisnow said she was outspent about 4 to 1.
- Her campaign's data center page calls for "an immediate moratorium on data centers to last until the legislature can meet to put in place real protections." It also calls for ending the 20-year tax breaks for hyperscale data centers created by HB 2712, signed in 2015. Fast Company reported she also wants to ban nondisclosure agreements that keep communities in the dark and to make companies prove their projects won't raise household water or electric bills.
- She called Abbott's own move an "election year pause." On Sept. 21, he directed TCEQ to "halt all permits sought by data centers until the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) completes its audit." Abbott is governor until January, so the order stands for now. The Texas Tribune's Oct. 30 explainer noted that any future governor can rescind past executive orders.
- The same explainer said a statewide moratorium would need the Legislature, where Republicans were expected to keep both chambers. Fast Company added that repealing the sales tax exemption or giving counties zoning power would also take legislation, and that a statewide moratorium could face legal challenges. The comptroller projects the exemption will cost $3.2 billion in revenue over two years.
- Fast Company cited a Jefferies estimate that a moratorium could put 13.3 GW of announced Texas projects at risk. The Tribune reported that anger over data centers helped her campaign, and that she blamed Abbott for a Big Tech takeover of rural land, resources and schools.

**Today's Signal:** For now, nothing changes on paper. Abbott stays governor until January, and his permit pause stays in effect until the ERCOT audit is done. What changed overnight is who decides what replaces it. Hinojosa has said a temporary pause isn't enough and wants protections written into law. That means a Republican Senate has to agree.

### 2. 🏛️ Democrats Flip Lieutenant Governor, Attorney General and the Railroad Commission. Republicans Keep the Texas Senate.

- The AP projected at 2 a.m. Wednesday that Democrat Vikki Goodwin had beaten Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, KXAN reported. She is the first Democrat to hold the office since Bob Bullock. The lieutenant governor presides over the Senate and sets its agenda for the session starting in January. Click2Houston reported Patrick conceded, saying the "result was fair and square" (headline only).
- At 1:57 a.m., the AP projected that state Sen. Nathan Johnson of Dallas had won the attorney general's race, making him the first Democrat in the office in 27 years. At 2:36 a.m., the AP projected state Rep. Jon Rosenthal the winner for Railroad Commission, the first Democrat elected there since 1990. The commission regulates the oil and gas production and pipelines behind the gas plants many Texas data centers are now planning.
- At 4:24 a.m., the AP projected that Republicans keep control of the Texas Senate. The Tribune's Oct. 30 explainer warned that a Republican majority could change Senate rules to strip the lieutenant governor's powers. In early returns, Republican Don Huffines led the comptroller's race (KUT, 8:15 p.m.). The comptroller administers the data center sales tax exemption.
- James Talarico beat Ken Paxton for U.S. Senate, with the AP calling it at 10:03 p.m. Talarico has called for tighter data center rules, saying construction "shouldn't contribute to the state's affordability crisis" (Bisnow).
- Locally, Taylor voters passed Prop F, which requires the city to publish a project's details, including the company's name, before council takes final action on anything it discussed in closed session (KXAN). Nationally, Stateline counted 33 local measures restricting data centers that had passed or were leading late Tuesday, and only one measure supporting construction had won, in North Dakota. An NBC exit poll found 56% of voters called AI and data centers a "crucial or major factor" in their vote for Congress.

**Today's Signal:** The lieutenant governor's job has been the most reliable way to kill a bill in Texas, and starting in January it belongs to a Democrat. But Republicans still control the Senate, which can rewrite its own rules, and both its majority and the attorney general can decide how hard data center rules get pushed or enforced.

### 3. 📉 Texas-Exposed AI Stocks Drop Overnight, Then Recover Most of the Loss by Morning

- Shares of AI infrastructure companies with Texas exposure fell in overnight trading after the result, Seeking Alpha reported at 3:09 a.m. Eastern Wednesday. CoreWeave fell 7%, and Nebius and Oracle fell 4% each. Seeking Alpha's list of companies with Texas data center projects included Galaxy Digital, Cipher Digital, Riot Platforms, Hut 8, Core Scientific, Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle.
- By premarket trading, the moves were mixed, CNBC reported. CoreWeave was down 0.8%, Iren 1.9% and Oracle 1.3%, while Nebius was up 2.9%. SpaceX fell 4.6% and Tesla 4.4%. CNBC said Musk put $224 million into Texas races this cycle. SpaceX is building data center capacity in Texas, among other places.
- Wells Fargo puts 27% of planned U.S. data center gigawatts in Texas. Its chief equity strategist, Ohsung Kwon, had called a Democratic win there a potential "left-tail event" for the AI trade. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. called Texas the night's "biggest worry" for tech investors.
- Horizon Investments CIO Scott Ladner said before the race was called that if Abbott lost, Texas's "fertile ground is largely barren" for new data centers. In an early-morning post on X, Musk wrote, "Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now."

**Today's Signal:** The overnight drop mostly reversed by morning, which suggests investors are pricing a slower, more political Texas, not a closed one. The real test comes later, when developers decide whether to keep spending on Texas sites while the permit pause holds and the session plays out.

### 4. ⚖️ ERCOT Asks the PUC to Set a Nov. 20 Deadline for Batch Zero Complaints. Developers Ask for Full Hearings.

- At 12:06 p.m. Tuesday, Nov. 3, ERCOT filed the same proposal in all five pending Batch Zero complaint dockets: Medina County Power (60356), Rowan Stampede (60396), Mesquite H2 (60410), ACS DC Hicks Field Road (60425) and Skybox Datacenters (60431). It asks the PUC to open a generic docket for issues common to all the complaints and to require any complaint over a large load's classification to be filed by Nov. 20, 2026.
- ERCOT said it received 32 disputes after its Sept. 3 conditional classifications, denied 25, and expects "as many as 12 to 20 more" developers to file complaints. It also wants tight limits on discovery and suggested capping each party's final hearing time at "one or two hours."
- The filing warns that unless all Batch Zero complaints are resolved by the end of March 2027, the entire Batch Zero interconnection study "will likely experience material delays," affecting "all Large Loads in Batch Zero," not just the companies that complained.
- At 4:43 p.m., the five developers filed jointly against consolidation and against ERCOT's proposed limits on discovery. They asked for time for oral argument at Thursday's open meeting and for a hearing before the full commission on each complaint. They said the reclassification process "lacked transparency" and that ERCOT left some of them out of its meetings with transmission providers.
- In a separate order Tuesday, the administrative law judge denied Medina County Power's request to keep discovery moving. The case stays paused until Nov. 10, as do the other complaints, so the commission can take up the process Thursday.

**Today's Signal:** ERCOT's argument is that speed protects everyone in the queue. The developers' argument is that they can't challenge a decision when they haven't seen the data behind it. Thursday's meeting will show which matters more to commissioners, two days after an election that put data centers at the center of Texas politics.

### 5. 🤝 Data Center Industry Launches a Texas Lobbying Group Ahead of the 2027 Session

- Dallas Innovates reported Tuesday, Nov. 3, that an industry coalition launched the Austin-based Texas Digital Infrastructure Alliance on Monday, Nov. 2, to advocate for data centers and other digital infrastructure.
- The co-executive directors are Marvin Singleton, co-founder and managing partner of Dallas-based Throughline Global Advisors, and Darren Grubb, a Throughline principal and former chief communications officer at the Dallas Regional Chamber. Austin policy adviser Megan Mauro is also part of the team.
- Grubb said the alliance won't publish its members. They include companies in data centers, telecom, fiber, cloud computing, utilities, real estate and construction. It launched a digital ad campaign with a 60-second video on Oct. 28.
- The group says it will focus on electricity, water, workforce and tax policy, using Texas-specific research. Its statement says digital businesses choose Texas "for speed to power, tax structure." Lawmakers are its main audience when the session opens in January.

**Today's Signal:** The industry built its lobbying group before the election, and now it will be working against a governor who wants to end its tax break. Because the alliance won't say who its members are, legislators considering a ban on nondisclosure agreements will be hearing from a group that is itself keeping its backers private.

No new TCEQ news releases since Oct. 21, and no new data center or gas plant air permit actions turned up in the eight tracked counties. No new zoning filings were reported in the DFW counties, and no new behind-the-meter generation deals were announced in Texas. ERCOT's news releases still stop at Sept. 21.
