## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🔍 Lufkin Emails Show TCEQ Pointing Amp Z's Power Plant Just Under a Federal Review Threshold

- The Lufkin Daily News reported Saturday that Verdant, the company affiliated with the Amp Z data center campus on State Highway 103 East, filed for a roughly 600-MW, 12-turbine gas plant at the former Abitibi paper mill on Dec. 9, 2024. Its particulate estimate rounded to 250 tons a year, which would have triggered federal Prevention of Significant Deterioration review.
- On Dec. 23, 2024, TCEQ reviewer Briannah Spooner asked company representative Chad Nerren whether the company could reevaluate PM emissions to come in under 249.99 tons when rounded to two decimals. The next day Nerren proposed cutting one combined operating hour a year, from 83,931 to 83,930. That moved the estimate from 249.9976 to 249.9947 tons.
- TCEQ canceled the mill's old PSD permit on Jan. 13, 2025, and approved the turbines' standard permit registration two days later, valid to Jan. 15, 2035. The paper found the approval and technical review still list 83,931 hours, the higher figure the company had offered to cut. A TCEQ spokesman would say only that all permits "are enforceable and must be complied with as issued."
- Verdant's March 2026 wastewater-permit renewal, for up to 5 million gallons a day discharged toward the Angelina River and Sam Rayburn Reservoir, includes no water balance, because the facility is "idled until operations resume." Lufkin's city manager said the 500-gallon-a-day estimate given to residents is not a limit the city plans to enforce.

**Today's Signal:** This is the same major-versus-minor-source question at the center of Public Citizen and the Sierra Club's Sept. 21 lawsuit against Vantage and VoltaGrid in San Antonio. Here the paper trail shows the regulator suggesting the fix. Amp Z's air authorization predates the freeze, so the TCEQ pause doesn't touch it. The emails could still matter when TCEQ sends its Oct. 19 update to the governor.

### 2. 📢 Amazon Drops NDAs and Pledges $1 Billion While Wharton County Keeps Asking About Water and Power

- In a blog post Friday covered over the weekend by TechCrunch, Wired and the AP, AWS CEO Matt Garman said Amazon no longer uses nondisclosure agreements with the government agencies it works with on data center projects. He pledged more than $1 billion over five years for communities near its sites and warned that more than 100 local moratoriums being considered nationwide could make the U.S. write "its own losing ticket."
- TechCrunch noted that a planned Amazon data center in Texas is permitted to emit 33 million tons of CO2 a year, more than any U.S. power plant. Garman's response was that backup generators "almost never run," about 10 hours a year.
- The Houston Chronicle's latest look at the Boling campus says the Coastal Bend Groundwater Conservation District's Sept. 8 permits allow up to 78.2 million gallons over about two and a half years. Amazon says it will use about 7.1 million gallons a year. It still hasn't applied to LCRA for the roughly 500 acre-feet a year of surface water it has said cooling could need.
- The Chronicle also reports Amazon now says each of the 13 buildings could draw about 60 MW, which could mean about 780 MW for the campus. Amazon wouldn't confirm a total, citing ERCOT's ongoing review. A Center Square piece Sunday headlined the project as "approved," but it described the same earlier water permits, not a new decision.

**Today's Signal:** Ending NDAs answers the transparency complaint Texas opponents raise most often. But in Wharton County, the open questions are the surface-water permit and the power total, and Amazon says ERCOT's audit is the reason it won't answer the second one yet.

### 3. 🏗️ Skybox Files a $550 Million Fourth Building in Hutto as State Rules Tighten

- Skybox and Prologis filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation for Skybox Hutto 4, a single-story, 846,300-square-foot building at 3399 County Road 132 in Williamson County, Hoodline reported Monday, following Data Center Dynamics. Construction is listed from January 2027 to October 2028.
- Hutto 4 would be the largest building yet on the PowerCampus, which is planned for 600 MW of IT capacity across 3.88 million square feet. Oncor and LCRA bought about 70 nearby acres for two dedicated 300-MW substations.
- The filing comes after the PUC's Sept. 18 rule requiring loads of 75 MW or more to post $50,000 per MW in security before ERCOT interconnection studies, and after Abbott's Sept. 21 TCEQ permit freeze. Hoodline notes it's unclear whether either will apply to this building.

**Today's Signal:** Like Crusoe's Jayton filings last week, this is a TDLR building registration, so it moves forward during the freeze. Whether the building gets built likely depends on the PUC's new security rule and on when ERCOT can deliver power to the site.

### 4. 🧮 Fort Worth and the TribFest Stage Run the Tax Math on Data Centers

- The Fort Worth Report published Sunday Fort Worth's estimate that its four data centers produced $83 million in gross property tax over five years. In 2024, they paid as much as 8,378 average-valued homes. By fiscal 2031, the city projects matching data center tax revenue would take about 22,718 homes.
- Assistant City Manager Jesica McEachern said that revenue isn't the "decision point." Council member Elizabeth Beck called offering incentives to data centers "a ridiculous policy." Railroad Commissioner Wayne Christian, who doesn't regulate data centers, said Texas shouldn't "turn down billions" over problems it can fix through regulation.
- The Real Deal's Sunday TribFest recap quoted Texas Taxpayers and Research Association president Jennifer Rabb, who drew boos for saying a data center brings 14 times more property tax than the next most productive use of a plot of land.
- In Dallas, a Friday Dallas Observer report described Joppa residents at a Thursday press conference demanding a say after a small crypto-linked facility was built by right near their homes. The five-member memo seeking a council vote on a Dallas moratorium has been sent to the Economic Development Committee. That committee's meeting next week was canceled.

**Today's Signal:** Fort Worth's own figures make the case for data centers better than the industry's coalition does, and the city still voted toward a 90-day pause. Dallas hasn't voted on its own pause yet, and by-right zoning means small facilities like the one in Joppa need no hearing at all.

### 5. 🗳️ Hinojosa Tells Abbott to Call a Special Session on Data Centers

- In a joint Dallas Morning News and NBC 5 interview that aired Sunday, recorded in Murphy in Collin County, Democratic nominee Gina Hinojosa said Abbott should call lawmakers back to Austin to pass stronger data center rules "to protect people."
- She blamed Abbott's tax-break policy for data centers "descending upon Texas," pointing to the 20-year state sales-tax exemption he signed in 2015. She also defended her 2017 vote letting contractor jobs count toward data center incentive job requirements.
- The DMN notes early voting starts in about two weeks. Data centers are now an issue in the governor's race and in down-ballot campaigns, including House District 71 in the Big Country, where Democrat Diana Luna is campaigning on it.

**Today's Signal:** Abbott's response so far has been the permit freeze and audits, not legislation, so a special-session demand gives Hinojosa a clear contrast. The 2027 session starts in January either way.

### 6. 🌿 Microsoft Pitches Native Landscaping as San Antonio Sets an Oct. 15 Zoning Vote

- Tom's Hardware, following The Verge, reported Sunday on Microsoft's "biomimicry" program of wetlands, prairie buffers and native gardens, now standard for its new U.S. sites. In San Antonio, Microsoft says it is investing in more than 400 acres of woodland and prairie restoration.
- District 6 Councilmember Ric Galvan, who has more than a dozen data centers in his district, recalled a 2022 approval allowing Microsoft to remove 2,642 trees from a 33-acre tract. The council declined his proposed moratorium and is scheduled to vote on zoning rules Oct. 15.
- Public Citizen's Adrian Shelley called the program "lipstick on a pig," pointing to Microsoft's 20-year deal for power from Chevron's planned 2.67-GW gas plant at the Kilby site in Reeves County.

**Today's Signal:** Landscaping can address noise and appearance, but it doesn't address the air permits now at the center of the Texas fight. That leaves San Antonio's Oct. 15 zoning vote as the next concrete decision on Microsoft's sites there.

ERCOT posted no new large-load or readiness-test releases over the weekend. No new TCEQ notices surfaced for data center or gas projects in Collin, Ellis, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker or Hood counties.
