## 1. ⚡ ERCOT & Grid Signals
**Record Demand Meets a Looming Batch Zero Deadline**

- Texas set an all-time peak demand record of 91,308 MW on July 22 at 5 p.m., breaking the prior record of 85,508 MW from August 2023; solar hit its own output record during the same stretch, exceeding 34,700 MW and at times supplying over 45% of generation (KERA News, July 30).
- ERCOT's July 2026 long-term forecast now projects statewide demand could reach roughly 175,000 MW by 2032, driven largely by data centers (KERA News, July 30; E&E News).
- Under the Batch Zero process (effective July 11 after June 18 PUCT approval), ERCOT must notify transmission/distribution service providers of which large loads qualify as Batch Zero-eligible by August 7 — a deadline now one week out.
- ERCOT's public large-load interconnection queue is estimated at roughly 438,000 MW of proposed demand, with about 90% attributed to data centers (kilowattlogic.com); separately, about 9,062 MW of load has been approved to energize over the past 12 months.
- **Today's Signal:** The grid just broke its all-time demand record on July 22, and ERCOT still owes utilities a Batch Zero eligibility notice by August 7 — the queue-triage system is being built and stress-tested at the same time.

## 2. 🏗️ Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**A $10 Billion Fort Worth Project Adds to a Crowded Field**

- Black Mountain has advanced plans for a $10 billion data center on 450+ acres in southeast Fort Worth (Lon Stephenson Road/Anglin Drive area, near Forest Hill, Kennetdale and Everman), anchored by a proposed 980-megawatt dedicated substation; Fort Worth's zoning commission revised allowed building height (55 ft to 70 ft) while cutting the project's footprint in half back in April (Fort Worth Report).
- Alphabet/Google closed a $4.75 billion acquisition of Intersect Power, adding Texas Panhandle sites in Gray County (near Pampa) and Roberts County (near Miami), plus additional in-development Texas projects.
- AWS's roughly 2,700-acre, four-building campus in Wharton County (about $300 million per building) is slated to begin construction August 1 — tomorrow — and follows the company's ~1,300-acre Cedar Creek-area acquisition in Bastrop County in May (Data Center Knowledge).
- Reporting this week (Forbes, July 28) put Meta's BlackRock-financed El Paso campus commitment as high as $10 billion for roughly 1 GW of capacity, with BlackRock holding an 80% stake — figures that continue to be reported somewhat inconsistently across outlets and are worth tracking as the deal's structure firms up.
- **Today's Signal:** With AWS's Wharton County construction starting tomorrow and Black Mountain's $10B Fort Worth project still advancing, DFW and its exurban ring keep adding large, simultaneous builds rather than one dominant project.

## 3. 🌿 Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**No New DFW County Filing Today — But the Statewide Numbers Keep Growing**

- No new Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood County TCEQ draft-permit or public-notice filing tied to a data center or gas plant was confirmed as newly dated today — a genuine gap, not an omission.
- Texas Tribune's July 9 analysis of state permit filings found at least 32 gas-fired plants (100+ MW each) planned in Texas to directly supply data centers — more than any other state — with potential emissions above 287 million tons of greenhouse gases per year, plus over 14,000 tons of fine particulate matter, 20,000 tons of NOx, and 8,000 tons of VOCs at max capacity.
- Floodlight News reporting documents that several AI-linked gas plants are using a "standard permit" track historically reserved for facilities like dry cleaners — a pathway that avoids the fuller individualized public-notice and comment process a major-source permit would require.
- TCEQ's Region 4 office (covering Collin, Cooke, Dallas, Denton, Ellis, Erath, Fannin, Grayson, Hood, Hunt, Johnson, Kaufman, Navarro, Palo Pinto, Parker, Rockwall, Somervell, Tarrant, and Wise counties) remains the jurisdiction to watch for any future DFW-area filing.
- **Today's Signal:** No new DFW-specific filing surfaced today, but the "dry-cleaner permit" loophole reporting explains why a filing could clear with minimal public notice when one does land.

## 4. 🏛️ Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Fort Worth's August 11 Vote Looms as Johnson County Politics Catches Up**

- Fort Worth's staff-drafted data-center ordinance — 250-ft setbacks from residential property, 72-hour ambient noise studies, closed-loop cooling standards, and a crypto-mining ban — is scheduled for a City Council vote on August 11, following the Zoning Commission's July 8 denial (7-4) of an earlier draft.
- A majority of Fort Worth council members (Beck, Flores, Hall, Martinez, Nettles) are separately pushing for a temporary moratorium on new data-center development once Council returns from summer break.
- In Johnson County, a Johnson County Post article (July 15) shows data centers have become a live issue in the county chair race, following a May 11 Commissioners Court public meeting on water, energy, noise, and health impacts; County Judge Christopher Boedeker said no specific project is currently proposed for unincorporated county land, but residents rank the issue second only to property taxes.
- Hood County — where developers have proposed eight data centers spanning more than 7,600 acres, ranking the county 6th statewide for planned data centers and 3rd per square mile — continues to see organized resident pushback, driven in part by the county's lack of local zoning authority to block projects (Texas Tribune, June 2).
- **Today's Signal:** Fort Worth's August 11 ordinance vote and Johnson County's chair-race politics show the same fight playing out on two different clocks — one heading to a formal vote in under two weeks, the other still building toward one.

## 5. 🔌 Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**West Texas Sets the Statewide Pace While DFW Watches From the Sidelines**

- No new dedicated gas-plant, gas-plus-battery, or behind-the-meter generation deal tied specifically to a DFW-area campus was confirmed as newly dated today.
- Project Kilby, near Pecos in West Texas, pairs a Microsoft-run AI campus with roughly 2.67 GW of co-located gas generation developed by Chevron's Energy Forge One and Engine No. 1, targeting first power in 2028 and about 2 GW of eventual data-center capacity.
- Cummins announced (June 16) an agreement to supply natural-gas generator sets for Circe Energy's behind-the-meter microgrids powering AI/HPC data centers in West Texas, including a 1,950-acre Permian Basin campus starting at 10 MW this year and scaling toward 1.1 GW by 2030 as an off-grid "prime power" microgrid.
- Texas leads the nation with an estimated 38 GW of behind-the-meter gas generation in development for data centers, out of roughly 101 GW announced nationally — a strategy directly tied to ERCOT's queue backlog (now near 438,000 MW).
- **Today's Signal:** The behind-the-meter model keeps scaling statewide (38 GW and counting), but the DFW-specific example remains Wise County's Red Post Energy complex from earlier this year — no new DFW power-first deal has landed since.
