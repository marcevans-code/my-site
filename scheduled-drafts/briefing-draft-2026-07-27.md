## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**Batch Zero's Deadlines Are Behind Us — Attention Shifts to Whether the Queue Actually Thins Out**

- Both halves of ERCOT's Batch Zero filing window have now closed — applicant-side submissions closed July 10, utility-side technical packages closed July 24 — meaning the nine-month interconnection study clock is now running for the first cohort of large loads to move through the state's new process.
- A third-party queue tracker (ERCOT Queue Dashboard) shows the pending large-load book still enormous: 87 projects between 500–1,000 MW and roughly 232,500 MW of proposed large load statewide as of this month, alongside separate figures citing more than 445 GW in the process overall.
- ERCOT's own news-release page shows no new release since the June 18 PUC approval of the Batch Zero process — no ERCOT-issued update today.
- The next hard date on the calendar is the Batch Zero deficiency-cure deadline of August 31, when incomplete filings from this round get resolved or dropped.

**Today's Signal:** With both Batch Zero filing deadlines now in the rearview mirror, the real test starts now — worth watching whether ERCOT actually clears cases out of the queue on schedule, or whether the 400+ GW backlog proves the nine-month clock was optimistic.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity

**Big Digital Energy Closes on a Hood County Power-Ready Site Built for a 300 MW Behind-the-Meter Campus**

- Big Digital Energy (Nasdaq: BGDE) and 10NetZero completed acquisition of a roughly 50-acre power-ready site in Hood County — less than 40 miles from DFW — on July 20. The site has 17 MW operational today, with phased grid expansion targeting 111 MW pending ERCOT validation, plus on-site gas infrastructure enabling a behind-the-meter buildout up to 300 MW for a planned AI/HPC campus. Northland Capital Markets advised on the deal.
- A weekly industry roundup (week of July 10) reported new filings outside Dallas: a 100,000-square-foot data center proposal and a second filing from Nexus DC, plus two data-center project filings in Haskell, Texas from Housebound Group LLC, an affiliate reportedly linked to Google, filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.
- Wistron opened a $700 million AI-hardware manufacturing facility in Fort Worth this week, producing Nvidia GB300 Grace Blackwell systems and creating roughly 800 jobs — not a data center itself, but directly tied to the DFW region's build-out of AI infrastructure supply chains.
- Fort Worth's Zoning Commission rejected the city's proposed data-center zoning ordinance 7-4 on July 8; that decision heads to City Council August 4, with a final vote set for August 11.

**Today's Signal:** Big Digital Energy's Hood County deal is a small-footprint but textbook example of the "power-first" playbook — buying land specifically for its existing gas infrastructure and grid headroom rather than the other way around — worth watching whether the site's 300 MW behind-the-meter ambitions draw the same TCEQ and county scrutiny as the nearby Comanche Circle campus.

## 🌿 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)

**Public Opposition to the Hood County NRG Tolar Gas Plant Tops 11,000 Comments**

- TCEQ has now received more than 11,000 public comments opposing the NRG Tolar gas-plant permit application (No. 181591) in Hood County, which is co-located with the Comanche Circle data-center campus — per an updated Sierra Club Greater Fort Worth action-alert tally as of July 18.
- The Public Utility Commission of Texas has separately logged roughly 150 protest comments on Oncor's related "Dinosaur–Longshore" 765kV transmission line proposal through Somervell County, which would help serve the same campus.
- A Texas Tribune/Floodlight investigation (July 9) identified at least 15 data-center-tied gas plants forming a statewide "shadow grid," nine of which could collectively emit more than 130 million tons of greenhouse gases per year; it documented use of "permit by rule" minor-permit loopholes — including by the Stargate/Crusoe project in Abilene — to avoid full public review.
- A separate July 16 Texas Tribune report flagged a proposed federal EPA rule that would shift more air-permitting authority to states, potentially narrowing the public-comment window Texans currently get on data-center-linked gas plants.
- Direct automated review of TCEQ's own NSR and Title V pending-applications databases (indexed as updated July 19 and July 24 respectively) did not surface readable, county-specific filing data for Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood counties today — a manual database check is recommended to confirm whether any new draft permits posted this week.

**Today's Signal:** Eleven thousand public comments on a single gas-plant permit is a striking number for TCEQ's usually quiet process — worth watching whether that volume of opposition pushes the Tolar permit toward a contested-case hearing rather than a routine approval.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments

**Cedar Creek Reservoir Coalition Takes Its Fight to Austin Today After Diode Ventures Withdraws**

- Diode Ventures, a Kansas-based developer, withdrew its proposed data center near Cedar Creek Reservoir in Henderson County after Gov. Greg Abbott publicly said data centers must be "responsible stewards" of water and natural resources; the project would have drawn up to 5 million gallons of lake water per day by its fifth year.
- The "Save Cedar Creek Lake" coalition, which organized against the project, said it plans to bring its fight to a state committee meeting in Austin today, July 27 — pressing for the 2027-session legislation Abbott has floated requiring data centers to cover their own interconnection costs, use closed-loop water systems, and forfeit sales-tax exemptions.
- Fort Worth's rejected zoning ordinance (7-4 vote, July 8) remains parked for a City Council vote August 11; five council members are pushing a competing moratorium proposal.
- Denton City Council is expected to take up a proposed AI-data-center moratorium in August; a community town hall opposing local data centers and a proposed AI drone factory was held July 16 at Emily Fowler Central Library, hosted by DSA Denton and the Revolutionary Front.
- Hood County commissioners have twice rejected (most recently by a 3-2 vote) a Development Commission recommendation for a one-year moratorium on large data-center projects like Comanche Circle; that fight remains a live backdrop to the Tolar permit opposition noted above.

**Today's Signal:** The Diode Ventures withdrawal is the clearest sign yet that public water-use objections — not just zoning fights — can kill a Texas data-center project outright; worth watching whether today's Austin committee meeting turns that local win into statewide legislative traction ahead of 2027.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation

**Hood County Deal Underscores the "Buy for the Gas Line, Not Just the Land" Pattern**

- Big Digital Energy/10NetZero's newly closed Hood County site (Section 2) explicitly cites existing natural-gas pipeline infrastructure as the reason a behind-the-meter buildout to 300 MW is feasible there, layered on top of a separate 111 MW of ERCOT-validated grid capacity — a hybrid, power-first structure rather than a pure grid-interconnection play.
- The broader "shadow grid" pattern documented by Texas Tribune/Floodlight (Section 3) includes examples like Stargate Abilene, already permitted for 10 turbines and 62 diesel generators and reportedly seeking approval for 41 more turbines and 18 more generators — built up incrementally through minor-permit filings that individually stay under full-review thresholds.
- Standing context from a July 2 KERA-covered study: Texas accounts for 32 of 74 proposed gas plants tracked nationally that are tied to AI data centers, with potential combined emissions as high as 287 million tons of greenhouse gases annually if all are built.
- No new PUC of Texas docket specific to data-center interconnection surfaced beyond the already-covered Batch Zero process; no distinct new large-load PUCT filing was found dated this week.

**Today's Signal:** The incremental, "small first, big later" minor-permit pattern behind projects like Stargate Abilene is becoming the template rather than the exception — worth tracking whether the Hood County site follows the same phased-permitting path as its buildout moves from 111 MW toward the full 300 MW behind-the-meter target.
