# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through September 5, 2026)*

**Top Story:** A Red Oak homeowner's lawsuit accusing her city of an unlawful data-center rezoning became today's sharpest legal test yet for Texans fighting these projects, landing the same week Black Mountain's request for enough Parker County groundwater to supply tens of thousands of homes triggered a new mandatory year-long study.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ Red Oak Residents Sue Their Own City Over a Data-Center Rezoning

- Two Red Oak residents who live within 200 feet of an 830-acre tract filed suit this week in Ellis County against the city, its mayor and five council members, alleging Red Oak failed to follow mandatory voting, notice and procedural requirements when it rezoned the land from agricultural to a "high-tech industrial park" planned development back in May, according to the Dallas Observer.
- Resident Jazmin Villegas said she learned the data center's terms only after the rezoning, calling the project "hidden behind an NDA"; her 20.5-acre property, lined with "No Data Centers" signs, hosted a Sept. 3 press conference where Democratic gubernatorial candidate Gina Hinojosa joined residents and called on Gov. Greg Abbott to convene a special legislative session on data-center protections.
- A new Texas Politics Project poll from the University of Texas at Austin, cited in the same report, found only 30% of 1,200 surveyed Texas voters support data-center construction in their local communities, against 57% opposed.
- Hinojosa said Abbott's statewide moratorium on new grid interconnections, in place since early August, is a "campaign move" she expects him to lift once the November election is decided.

**Today's Signal:** Red Oak's lawsuit turns a familiar complaint — no notice, an NDA-shielded deal — into an actual court filing, and a statewide poll showing 57% opposition gives it a political backdrop no city council can easily wave away before November.

### 2. 💧 Black Mountain's Next Texas Site Wants 138 Million Gallons of Groundwater a Year

- Black Mountain — the Fort Worth-based developer whose $10 billion southeast Fort Worth campus remains stuck on a tabled City Council rezoning vote — has told the Upper Trinity Groundwater Conservation District it intends to request permits for 138 million gallons of groundwater annually, or just under 400,000 gallons a day, General Manager Doug Shaw confirmed at the district's Aug. 27 board meeting.
- The request is tied to Black Mountain's roughly 2,083-acre purchase at Pearson Ranch, near Azle Highway north of Weatherford in unincorporated Parker County; under UTGCD rules linking water allocations to acreage owned, that holding alone puts the company within its rights to seek that volume, though no formal permit application has been filed yet.
- Under a subsidiary called Fort Worth Power Core LLC, Black Mountain has already secured a TCEQ air-emissions permit to install five natural-gas backup turbines at 501 Pearson Ranch Road — the same "bridge power" approach data centers elsewhere in Texas use to run before a full ERCOT interconnection comes through — giving the unincorporated county site an industrial footprint residents say is larger than the city limits of neighboring Aledo.
- Because the request clears a 50-million-gallon-a-year threshold UTGCD adopted in February, Black Mountain now faces a mandatory, public groundwater-impact study before any permit is issued — a process expected to delay the project at least a year — while advocacy group Texans United Against Data Centers argues the site's location in the Lake Weatherford watershed could give the City of Weatherford its own drinking-water-protection claim over how much the company draws.
- Parker County Commissioners Court, facing standing-room-only crowds over the project, has passed a resolution demanding tighter state-level regulation of data-center utilities, even though the court has limited authority over development on unincorporated land.

**Today's Signal:** Reporting this year has tied Black Mountain to at least four Texas sites — Fort Worth, Parker County, Bowie County and San Antonio — all running a similar buy-land-then-file-for-turbines playbook, and Parker County's new 50-million-gallon study rule is the first guardrail in this briefing's coverage area built specifically to slow it down.

### 3. 🏗️ A New 54-Megawatt Data Center Lands in the North Dallas Corridor

- Northampton Capital Partners and Provident Data Centers announced a joint venture this week to develop a turnkey data center with 54 megawatts of critical capacity in the North Dallas Corridor, with the project expected online in late 2027.
- The announcement, first reported Sept. 3, is still being picked up across trade press today; it marks the venture's first disclosed project together after Northampton separately partnered with Olympus Power on generation projects earlier this year.
- Neither company has yet disclosed a specific site address, cost, or anchor tenant for the facility.

**Today's Signal:** This is a routine capacity announcement rather than a fight — no rezoning battle, no water permit, no lawsuit attached — which is itself notable against a week when two other DFW-area projects hit real legal and regulatory friction.

Quiet today on new ERCOT interconnection signals and on TCEQ air-permit filings beyond Black Mountain's already-secured Pearson Ranch turbine permit.
