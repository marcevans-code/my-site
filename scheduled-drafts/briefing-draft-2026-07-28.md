## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**CenterPoint's Q2 Earnings Give the First Real Numbers on Batch Zero — 17 GW Submitted, 14 GW Expected to Qualify**

- CenterPoint Energy's Q2 2026 earnings release (July 28) provided the first utility-side numbers from ERCOT's Batch Zero process: the company submitted more than 17 GW of large-load projects, with roughly 14 GW expected to qualify as base load or studied load once ERCOT completes its review.
- CenterPoint also raised its 10-year capital plan to $66.7 billion (from $65.5 billion), citing accelerating electricity demand tied to data centers, and reiterated full-year 2026 guidance.
- A third-party tracker (ERCOT Queue Dashboard / ercotqueue.com) put the statewide large-load interconnection queue at 466,497 MW of requests as of June 2026 — nearly double the 238,629 MW logged in March. ERCOT's own figures from June filings cite a somewhat lower 438,000–453,000 MW range, a reminder that queue totals vary by source and methodology.
- No new release appeared on ERCOT's own News and Publications page today. The next hard date on the calendar remains the Batch Zero deficiency-cure deadline of August 31.

**Today's Signal:** CenterPoint is the first major transmission utility to disclose its own Batch Zero submission numbers, giving the clearest gauge yet of how much of that 400+ GW statewide queue is real, utility-backed load moving through the nine-month study clock.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Meta Hands BlackRock 80% of a New $14 Billion El Paso Campus in a Debt-Heavy Financing Structure**

- Meta and BlackRock announced a joint venture today (July 28) to develop and operate a $14 billion, 1-gigawatt data center campus in El Paso, already under construction. BlackRock-managed funds take an 80% ownership stake financed partly through $12.5 billion in debt, while Meta retains 20%, contributes land and in-progress construction assets worth about $2.3 billion, and will lease back compute capacity rather than own the campus outright. Operations are expected to begin in 2028.
- The Wall Street Journal reported the same day that Meta priced a $12.5 billion bond offering tied to the project at a higher interest rate than a similar deal a year ago — one data point in what BofA Global Research says is a $270 billion pace of AI-related bond issuance so far this year, nearly double all of 2025.
- The Texas Tribune reported (July 27) that audits are lagging on the state's sales-tax exemption for data center equipment: 138 facilities have now qualified statewide (59 in 2026 alone), representing an estimated $3.3 billion in tax savings, but only 20 have been audited and six of those were found out of compliance — prompting a Texas Senate Finance Committee hearing this week.
- Fort Worth's City Council remains on track to vote August 11 on whether to pursue a formal data-center development moratorium, a process city staff describe as lengthy even if approved.

**Today's Signal:** Financing structures like the Meta/BlackRock venture — hyperscalers leasing capacity from asset-manager-owned campuses instead of building on their own balance sheets — are becoming the template as investors scrutinize AI capex, a trend directly relevant to how future DFW-area projects get financed and by whom.

## 🌿 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**Correcting the Record: the NRG Tolar Permit at the Center of Hood County's Fight Was Actually Withdrawn Back in June**

- Records checked today confirm NRG Energy withdrew its air permit application (No. 181591) for the 1,200 MW–2 GW Tolar Power Center in Hood County on June 18, 2026, after mounting public opposition — including the 11,000+ comment tally referenced in recent coverage. That withdrawal happened over a month before the comment volume was last cited, so the permit is not currently pending before TCEQ; no resubmission has been confirmed as of today.
- A Texas Tribune report (July 16) flagged a proposed federal EPA rule that would shift more air-permitting authority to states, which could narrow the public-comment window Texans get on data-center-linked gas plants going forward.
- The Environmental Integrity Project's July report remains the most-cited statewide figure: 32 of 74 proposed gas plants tracked nationally and tied to AI data centers are in Texas, with potential combined emissions as high as 287 million tons of greenhouse gases annually if all are built.
- Direct attempts to reach TCEQ's NSR and Title V pending-applications pages today returned broken/moved-page errors rather than live filing data — a manual database check is again recommended to confirm whether any new draft permits posted this week for Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood counties.

**Today's Signal:** The Tolar withdrawal is a reminder that TCEQ public-comment pressure campaigns can and do work before a permit reaches a vote — worth watching whether NRG or another operator files a revised application for the same Hood County site now that the Comanche Circle concept plan itself has already been approved.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Red Oak Residents Renew Opposition as Lawmakers Question a $3.3 Billion Tax-Break Program**

- Red Oak (Ellis County) residents renewed public opposition to data-center development this week, as covered by NBC 5 DFW, timed to a Texas Senate Finance Committee hearing (July 27) reviewing the state's data-center sales-tax exemption program.
- That hearing is one stop in a multi-day "week of action" by a coalition of more than a dozen advocacy groups pressing Gov. Abbott and state lawmakers for stronger data-center oversight ahead of the 2027 legislative session.
- Fort Worth's City Council vote on pursuing a formal moratorium remains set for August 11, following the Zoning Commission's 7-4 rejection of the city's proposed data-center zoning ordinance on July 8.
- Hood County's Comanche Circle concept plan — 2,101 acres approved without conditions on June 9 — remains the backdrop for the county's ongoing moratorium fight and the now-withdrawn Tolar gas-plant permit next door.

**Today's Signal:** The Red Oak pushback and the Senate Finance Committee's scrutiny of a $3.3 billion tax-break program are converging on the same argument — that data centers are getting public subsidies and infrastructure priority without commensurate local oversight — a frame likely to shape both county zoning fights and 2027 legislative proposals.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**No New On-Site Generation Deal Today, But Regulators Are Writing the Rules Those Deals Will Have to Follow**

- No dated announcement of a new behind-the-meter gas plant, gas+battery hybrid, or dedicated on-site generation deal for a Texas AI campus surfaced today. The most recent confirmed example remains Big Digital Energy/10NetZero's Hood County site (closed July 20, covered in prior briefings).
- Data Center Knowledge reported (July 27) that PUCT and ERCOT are developing a Senate Bill 6 reliability program that would compensate large loads for reducing demand ahead of grid emergencies, while requiring those facilities to disclose their ability to curtail operations or rely on backup/on-site generation — a rule set that will directly govern how power-first campuses like Comanche Circle and the Hood County site operate.
- The same PUCT filing confirmed a pending rulemaking requiring large-load customers to post financial security before entering ERCOT planning studies and to pay direct interconnection costs, expected to be finalized by December 2026.
- The Environmental Integrity Project's "shadow grid" figures (Section 3) — at least 15 data centers building their own behind-the-meter gas plants, nine of them capable of emitting 130+ million tons of greenhouse gases combined — remain the standing statewide baseline for this trend.

**Today's Signal:** With no new on-site generation deal today, the more consequential development is regulatory — PUCT's forthcoming curtailment-disclosure and financial-security rules will determine whether power-first, behind-the-meter campuses can keep operating with minimal public visibility into their backup generation.
