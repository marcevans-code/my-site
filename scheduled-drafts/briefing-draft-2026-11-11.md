# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 11, 2026

(Covering Tuesday, November 10 – Wednesday, November 11, 2026)

**Top Story:** Fort Worth held the first of two required hearings on a 90-day data center moratorium Tuesday night, hours after its own advisory commission handed the council 19 recommendations, including a 350-foot minimum setback from homes and a "zero-water" cooling standard.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🏙️ Fort Worth Hears From Residents on a Data Center Moratorium and Gets a 19-Point Rulebook From Its Own Commission

- Tuesday, Nov. 10: The city's 11-member Data Center & Infrastructure Commission presented a 156-page report to the City Council with 19 recommendations covering water and natural resources, land use, economic impact and electricity. TCU environmental director Michael Slattery, who led the group, called data centers the most contested land-use issue in the city (Fort Worth Report).
- The recommendations include a "zero-water" requirement for closed-loop, nonevaporative cooling with limited adiabatic cooling, public reporting of water use, a technical fact sheet and verifiable grid-relief commitments from developers, a minimum 350-foot setback from homes that grows with building height, and a framework to enforce noise limits. Slattery said the setback drew the biggest disagreement among commissioners, and council member Jeanette Martinez said she'd like it larger (Fort Worth Report).
- That evening, the council held the first of two state-required public hearings on the 90-day moratorium it started in August. All but one of about two dozen speakers backed it. If three-fourths of the council approves it, it would take effect Feb. 23 and end May 24 unless extended. Projects already approved and permitted, such as Black Mountain's $10 billion project in southeast Fort Worth, could keep building (Fort Worth Report).
- Two Tarrant County-area lawmakers have filed bills since filing opened Monday: Rep. Salman Bhojani, D-Euless, would require data centers to register with affected local governments and disclose their water supplier and water and power use, and Rep.-elect Armin Mizani, R-Keller, would require city approval inside city limits and county approval in unincorporated areas. Commissioner Matt Krause's resolution to end Tarrant County abatements for data centers is expected to come to a vote Nov. 17 (Fort Worth Report).
- Statewide, Sen. Joan Huffman said in a statement Tuesday that the data center sales tax exemption she wants repealed with SB 293 "is not sustainable" (KRIS 6).

**Today's Signal:** The commission didn't say where data centers may go. It set conditions they'd have to meet anywhere, the "certainty and predictability" Slattery said developers asked for. That gives the council a draft ordinance to work on during the 90 days, which only matters if a Legislature that's now debating county and city approval powers leaves cities room to use it.

### 2. 🚀 SpaceX's Starbase Site Was Carved Out of Brownsville Before the City's Data Center Moratorium

- Tuesday, Nov. 10: The Texas Tribune reported that the 444.45 acres near the Port of Brownsville where SpaceX plans its $24 billion gas plant and "super-compute facility" were de-annexed by Brownsville earlier this year at SpaceX's request. SpaceX offered $220 million for city water and wastewater projects in return, and a company representative pressed the city to approve quickly or lose the money.
- Brownsville's own 90-day data center moratorium, covering the city and its extraterritorial jurisdiction, took effect last week. The Tribune said the project likely would have needed a different site if the land had stayed in the city, and that online speculation SpaceX was avoiding the moratorium "appears to have been correct all along." Starbase annexed the land and posted the project Monday.
- Brownsville said it stands by the de-annexation based on the "information and circumstances presented at the time" and will judge future requests independently under state law. The South Texas Environmental Justice Network announced a protest over air pollution, water use and energy bills. SpaceX didn't answer the Tribune's questions (Texas Tribune; KPRC Click2Houston).

**Today's Signal:** The land left Brownsville's jurisdiction before the moratorium, and the gas plant means the project doesn't need ERCOT's audited queue. That leaves Starbase's Dec. 9 abatement hearing and TCEQ's air permit as the only public decision points, and both sit with bodies close to SpaceX or under Abbott's pause.

### 3. 📈 CoreWeave Tells Analysts Most of Its Texas Power Is Online or Fully Permitted

- Tuesday, Nov. 10: On CoreWeave's third-quarter call, CEO Michael Intrator said that "within Texas specifically, the overwhelming majority of our power is either online or fully permitted" and under construction, and that the company doesn't "see material risks to our 2027 deliveries based on ongoing construction at fully permitted sites" (Investing.com transcript; Motley Fool transcript via AOL).
- Asked about its letter to Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa, Intrator said it was meant to establish a relationship and tell her "we are nonpartisan." He said the company does "not anticipate any impact from the seismic regulatory shift that has occurred" (Investing.com transcript).
- CoreWeave reported $3.7 billion in revenue, up 168%, a record $133 billion backlog and 4.6 GW of contracted power. It raised 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0 billion–$13.3 billion, and shares rose 5.7% after hours to $51.51 (Investing.com transcript; GuruFocus via Yahoo Finance).

**Today's Signal:** "Overwhelming majority" is the company's own count, and it didn't name the Texas capacity that isn't permitted yet. Those sites are the ones a Hinojosa freeze or a new PUC permit requirement would catch.

### 4. 🔥 AZIO Says Its Live Oak County Campus Will Run Entirely Off the ERCOT Grid

- Tuesday, Nov. 10: AZIO AI Holdings (Nasdaq: AZIO) said Atlas One, the first campus in its Project Atlas program in Live Oak County, is designed to run on on-site natural gas generation with no ERCOT large-load interconnection request pending (AZIO via GlobeNewswire).
- The company has a signed 10 MW hosting agreement with Power Champion at $150 per kilowatt per month and plans an 11 MW first phase. Nothing is energized yet, and each phase's generation needs its own air authorization before construction (AZIO).
- COO Elgin Tracy said "behind-the-meter is not a loophole," pointing to the state's requirements for site control, financial commitment and real data (AZIO).

**Today's Signal:** At 11 MW, Atlas One is small, but it shows the same strategy SpaceX is using at a much larger scale: build your own power and avoid the audited ERCOT queue. Both still need TCEQ air authorization, which makes TCEQ the main check on off-grid projects.

### 5. 🗂️ Consumer Advocates Weigh In on the Panhandle Tariff Case and the Standard Interconnection Agreement

- Tuesday, Nov. 10: The Office of Public Utility Counsel filed its proposed list of issues in Docket 60332, Southwestern Public Service Co.'s large-load customer tariff case, responding to Monday's PUC order. SPS filed another set of answers to Texas Industrial Energy Consumers' first questions (PUC Interchange).
- OPUC also filed a notice of participation in Project 59999, the rulemaking on a standard large-load interconnection agreement. The Texas Register acknowledged that the proposal will run in its Nov. 20 issue (PUC Interchange).

**Today's Signal:** OPUC is now a party in both the SPS tariff case and the statewide agreement rulemaking. Residential ratepayers will have a formal voice in setting the rules for data center hookups, both inside and outside ERCOT.

ERCOT's only new release, on Nov. 10, covered its upgraded public grid-alert system, not large loads. TCEQ still has no news releases after Oct. 21, and no air permit actions or zoning votes turned up in the other seven tracked DFW counties. There was also no word yet on Cottle County's Project Matter abatement hearing.
