# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use
*(Latest updates through August 12, 2026)*

## 🔌 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals

**The Compliance List Triples Overnight**

- Gov. Abbott announced Tuesday evening, Aug. 11, that EdgeConneX, Stream Data Centers, and Oracle have agreed to comply with his Aug. 3 data-center standards, pushing the roster of publicly committed operators to nine in just over a week. EdgeConneX's commitment covers roughly 1,400 acres across three planned campuses in Bastrop County along FM 535 (Office of the Texas Governor, Aug. 11).
- ERCOT's suspension of Batch Zero classification notices, and its request for a good-cause exception, remain queued for the PUCT's Aug. 20 open meeting; no new classification activity has posted since the Aug. 3 pause.

**Today's Signal:** Nine operators have now signed onto Abbott's standards in nine days — a compliance list growing almost daily while the actual interconnection queue it applies to hasn't moved an inch since Aug. 3.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity

**A Houston Land Developer Rebrands Around the Boom**

- Houston-based W Land Development announced Aug. 11 that it is pivoting from residential land development into data-center site preparation — acquiring parcels, handling permitting and utility coordination, and marketing "power-ready" sites directly to operators (Data Center Dynamics, Aug. 11).

**Today's Signal:** The Texas land-speculation wave around data centers is maturing into its own service industry, with developers now packaging "power-ready" land as a product rather than waiting for operators to find it themselves.

## 🌎 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)

**A West Texas Permit Draws a Legislator's Intervention**

- Westline TX Holdings LLC, tied to Beacon Data Centers, filed a TCEQ air-permit application on Aug. 10 for the "Westline Energy Infrastructure Campus" near Dove Creek in Tom Green County — outside the core DFW county list, but projecting 1,177.2 tons/year of carbon monoxide, 419.3 tons/year of NOx, and 117.2 tons/year of sulfur dioxide from on-site gas reciprocating engines.
- The same day, state Rep. Drew Darby (R-San Angelo) formally asked TCEQ to hold a public hearing on the permits and sent Beacon a nine-question public letter demanding disclosure on emissions, water use, and local jobs.
- No new draft permit, public-notice filing, or comment deadline surfaced today in the core DFW counties of Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood.

**Today's Signal:** The permit fights are spreading well beyond DFW's eight-county core, and it's a sitting state legislator, not just neighbors, now demanding TCEQ hold a hearing.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments

**Fort Worth's "Moratorium" Turns Out to Be a 2027 Story**

- Fort Worth's City Council unanimously voted Aug. 11 to start the legal process for a 90-day data-center construction moratorium — but Texas law requires two public hearings and two council votes with mandatory gaps between them, so the pause itself wouldn't take effect until Feb. 16, 2027 at the earliest, with hearings set for Nov. 10 and Jan. 5 (Fort Worth Report, Aug. 12).
- The same meeting, council passed new siting rules 10-1 requiring any data-center application filed after Aug. 11 to show proof of PUCT/ERCOT grid-connection approval, and created a new Data Center & Infrastructure Committee to study noise, water, and land-use impacts. Neither measure applies retroactively to projects already filed, including Black Mountain's.
- Separately on Aug. 11, local governments — organizing through the Texas Association of Counties and reported out of Grayson County — began lobbying the Legislature for zoning authority over data centers, a power Texas counties currently don't have at all (KXII, Aug. 11).
- A Texas Tribune investigation published Aug. 10 followed a Grayson County landowner near Bells who spent months tracking survey crews to uncover a Black Mountain-linked data-center and power-plant proposal near his property, part of a broader pattern of NDAs and shell-company filings — including Meta-linked entities "Winner LLC" and "Wurldwide LLC" — obscuring land deals; at least 34 Texas counties have now asked the state for more disclosure.

**Today's Signal:** "Moratorium" is the headline, but the fine print is a process that won't bind anyone until 2027 — while counties with zero zoning authority over data centers are only now asking the Legislature to give them any.

## ⚡ 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation

**A Grid-Jurisdiction Gap Lets One Project Keep Building Through the Audit**

- Reporting flagged that Abbott's Aug. 3 audit directive only reaches projects connected to ERCOT — parts of East and North Texas instead sit on the SPP or MISO grids, outside ERCOT's authority entirely. Black Mountain Power LLC's roughly 600-acre site near Leary/Red Lick in Bowie County was continuing land-clearing and construction as of Aug. 7, specifically because it falls outside ERCOT's jurisdiction (Texas Scorecard, Aug. 11).

**Today's Signal:** The audit's boundary is drawn by grid interconnect, not geography — and at least one project is building straight through the pause because it simply isn't plugged into the grid the state is auditing.
