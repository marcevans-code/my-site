## 1. ⚡ ERCOT & Grid Signals
**Abbott Freezes New Grid Connections, Ordering a Full Audit of the Interconnection Queue**

- Gov. Greg Abbott on Monday (Aug. 3) ordered the Public Utility Commission of Texas and ERCOT to conduct a "comprehensive verification and audit" of every data center advancing through ERCOT's interconnection queue, directing regulators to deny grid connection to any project that fails to comply with disclosure requirements on tax breaks, power and water use, community-impact mitigation, and ownership (The Texas Tribune, Aug. 3).
- ERCOT is currently tracking more than 1,800 projects in the interconnection queue totaling over 474 gigawatts — more than five times the grid's peak-demand record — with roughly 90% of that request volume coming from data centers, according to figures cited in the governor's directive (The Texas Tribune; ERCOT).
- In direct response, ERCOT confirmed Monday it is pausing the "Batch Zero" transmission planning study already underway — the same process the PUCT approved in June (Docket 59142) to screen the first cohort of large-load projects — while it works out how Abbott's order interacts with that ongoing rulemaking (The Texas Tribune; Data Center Knowledge).
- The order builds on Abbott's June directive requiring data centers to fully fund their own grid-infrastructure costs and a July 17 letter in which PUCT Chair Thomas Gleeson asked lawmakers for expanded authority to regulate the industry — this week's audit escalates that fight from a legislative request to an immediate operational freeze.
- **Today's Signal:** For the first time, Texas has actually stopped new data-center grid connections rather than just studying the backlog — the open question is whether Batch Zero projects already mid-review get swept into the audit or grandfathered through.

## 2. 🏗️ Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Industry Calls Abbott's Audit an Opportunity, Critics Call It Political Theater**

- The Data Center Coalition, the trade group representing Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft and other hyperscalers, said Monday it was "hopeful" the audit "can showcase the good actors in the data center industry rather than delaying them unnecessarily," and urged regulators to move quickly (The Texas Tribune, Aug. 3, quoting spokesman Dan Diorio).
- Opponents said the directive stops short of a real moratorium: Texas Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller called it "all hat and no cattle," and state Rep. Gina Hinojosa, an Austin Democrat challenging Abbott, said a "pause" with no fixed end date isn't credible (The Texas Tribune, Aug. 3).
- The Tribune's own count puts at least 335 data centers already operating statewide and at least 248 more planned, with hundreds of additional projects at earlier stages sitting in the ERCOT power queue — Texas is the No. 2 data-center market nationally behind Virginia and is on pace to take the top spot (The Texas Tribune, Aug. 3).
- Abbott's freeze applies only to projects seeking a traditional ERCOT grid connection; developers building their own on-site power — or building outside ERCOT's footprint entirely, like the Meta/BlackRock campus in El Paso — are unaffected, a distinction that could accelerate the shift toward the behind-the-meter model covered in Section 5.
- **Today's Signal:** Abbott's audit sorts the industry into two camps overnight — grid-connected projects now face a compliance gate with no fixed timeline, while self-generating projects get a competitive edge simply by not needing ERCOT's blessing.

## 3. 🌿 Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**No New DFW Filing Today, But Abbott's Audit Just Made TCEQ's Water Data Central to the Fight**

- No new Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood County TCEQ draft-permit or public-notice filing tied to a data center or gas plant was confirmed as newly dated today.
- Abbott's audit directive explicitly pulls TCEQ into the process, requiring developers to disclose water use, cooling-system design, and water sourcing — data points TCEQ and the Texas Water Development Board already collect through a joint PUCT survey that only 28 of 377 notified companies had answered as of a June legislative hearing (The Texas Tribune, Aug. 3).
- That gap is why Abbott cited the water survey's "pretty pathetic" response rate — a description used by state Rep. Brad Buckley, R-Salado, in June — as one justification for the broader audit (The Texas Tribune, Aug. 3).
- Elsewhere in Texas, a TCEQ public hearing is now set for Aug. 17 on a Vantage Data Centers facility accused of splitting a single gas-and-diesel power plant into smaller permit applications to avoid full-scale environmental review — the same "minor source" permitting pattern flagged in earlier Tribune/Floodlight reporting on the industry (News 4 San Antonio).
- **Today's Signal:** TCEQ's own water-use survey — largely ignored by the industry until now — has effectively become the paperwork Abbott is demanding data centers finally produce.

## 4. 🏛️ Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Fort Worth and Westlake Both Take Up Data Centers Today, From Opposite Directions**

- Fort Worth City Council holds its work session this afternoon to review staff's revised data-center regulations — covering land use, neighborhood compatibility, water use, noise, and incentive rules — ahead of an Aug. 11 vote on whether to begin the state-required legal process for a temporary moratorium on new development; if approved, a final council vote wouldn't come until December (NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth; City of Fort Worth).
- That timeline follows the Fort Worth Zoning Commission's 7-4 vote recommending denial of the original ordinance, with commissioners saying they wanted more time to weigh public feedback rather than opposing stronger rules outright (NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth).
- In Westlake, the town's Planning and Zoning Commission is scheduled to review a proposed data center today after Keller residents packed a Sunday community meeting with Mayor Ross McMullin to object — the site would sit about 506 feet from some Keller backyards, and because it's entirely within Westlake's city limits, Keller officials say they have no legal authority to block it (FOX 4 Dallas-Fort Worth, Aug. 2).
- Westlake officials have said the project will meet the town's noise standards and generate significant tax revenue; Keller's mayor said he wishes Westlake had given his city advance notice and plans to push for changes to setbacks, siting, and noise conditions instead (FOX 4 Dallas-Fort Worth).
- **Today's Signal:** Fort Worth is trying to slow data centers down through its own ordinance, while Westlake shows the limits of that approach — a project sited a few hundred feet over a city line leaves the neighboring city's residents with no vote and no veto.

## 5. 🔌 Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**No New DFW Deal Today — Abilene's Behind-the-Meter JV Is the One to Watch Statewide**

- No new dedicated gas-plant, gas-plus-battery, or behind-the-meter generation deal tied specifically to a DFW-area campus was confirmed as newly dated today.
- The most recent statewide example remains newly formed PowerPlay AI's July 30 announcement of a 400-megawatt behind-the-meter gas-fired data center campus near Abilene, developed as a joint venture with neocloud operator Sharon AI; the partners say they're now securing gas-supply agreements and an independent power producer, targeting power delivery by 2028 (Data Center Dynamics, July 30).
- Abbott's new audit directive (Section 1) applies only to projects seeking ERCOT grid connections, not to self-generating campuses like the Abilene project — a distinction likely to make the behind-the-meter route more attractive to developers trying to avoid the audit entirely.
- In Denton, the city's two operating on-site facilities, Core Scientific and Qumulus AI, both run closed-loop cooling systems the companies say require no ongoing water withdrawal — a model city officials point to as a counterpoint to the gas-fired behind-the-meter builds further west (Community Impact).
- **Today's Signal:** With ERCOT-connected projects now facing an open-ended state audit, expect more developers to pitch power-first, self-generating campuses like Abilene's — the audit doesn't touch them.
