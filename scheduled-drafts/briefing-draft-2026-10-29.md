## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ ERCOT Asks the PUC to Throw Out the First Batch Zero Appeal With Prejudice

- ERCOT filed its response in Docket 60356 at 3:33 p.m. Wednesday, Oct. 28, the deadline set in the case's original schedule. It says Medina County Power NR I "has not satisfied the requirement to have a qualifying study to be classified as Base Load" and asks the commission to "deny the requested relief and dismiss the Complaint with prejudice." The public version runs 100 pages; most exhibits are filed confidential or highly sensitive.
- ERCOT's core argument: Medina's utility, South Texas Electric Cooperative, left three large loads "electrically affected by the Medina project" out of the legacy study, so under the Planning Guide the study isn't "complete and valid" and the project had to go into Studied Load. ERCOT says it didn't "invalidate" the study, and that the rules give it *sole discretion* to decide validity without STEC's agreement.
- The filing spells out what's at stake. Base Load is "essentially guaranteed inclusion in Batch Zero" at full requested capacity, while Studied Load "may be allocated less than its requested amount of transmission capacity."
- It also warns that every classification so far is only conditional. ERCOT and PUC staff are still running a request-for-information review under Abbott's Sept. 3 directive, and a project now marked Base Load can still drop to Studied Load if it fails other tests, such as site control, a notice to proceed on interconnection facilities, or ordering long-lead equipment on time.
- Medina answered the same day. It served its first requests for information on ERCOT, and at 9 p.m. filed an appeal and request for clarification of Order No. 3, the Oct. 27 order pausing its case. Medina argues that, unlike the other three complainants, it had an agreed schedule with ERCOT, and that delay costs it its only real chance to get into Batch Zero as Base Load.
- No new Batch Zero complaints appeared in the PUC's Oct. 28 electric filings.

**Today's Signal:** This response is a template for every Batch Zero appeal. Skybox's complaint also turns on a utility study that missed neighboring loads, and ERCOT's answer is that the rules, not the utility's diligence, decide the outcome. The footnote may matter more than the argument: even developers who won Base Load status don't get to keep it until the RFI review is done.

### 2. 🏦 Texas's Pause Strands an Anthropic-Linked Bond Sale on Bank Balance Sheets

- Banks have shelved plans to sell bonds against a roughly $15 billion loan for Nexus Data Centers' campus in Hubbard, near Waco, which Anthropic leases, Bloomberg reported Wednesday, Oct. 28. Write-ups by Briefs Finance and Finimize give the details; Bloomberg's own story is paywalled.
- The loan is a $14.2 billion one-year term facility plus a $1.4 billion five-year revolver, arranged by Citigroup, Intesa Sanpaolo, Mizuho, Morgan Stanley, MUFG, Royal Bank of Canada and Banco Santander, with Morgan Stanley leading the lender group.
- Lenders had planned a $5 billion to $7 billion high-yield bond sale for September. That sale may now be spread out over the next year, keeping the debt on bank books longer. The reason given is Texas's freeze on new project approvals while the state reviews grid risks.
- The bridge loan has already been funded, and some agencies have assigned ratings in the mid-BB range, according to people familiar with the matter. Anthropic, Google, Citi, Santander and MUFG declined to comment.

**Today's Signal:** The pause has moved from the ERCOT queue to Wall Street. The construction money is already out the door, so for now the risk sits with seven banks instead of bond buyers. The longer the audit runs, the longer that risk stays with them.

### 3. 🛑 Denton's Moratorium Clears Its Last Hearing, and the Council Floats Making It Six Months

- Denton City Council held its second and final required public hearing and the first reading of a data center moratorium Tuesday night, Oct. 27. It took no vote, and adoption could come as early as Dec. 1. Adoption needs a 75% supermajority, Community Impact reported.
- The ordinance as drafted would halt new commercial data center applications for 90 days in the city, Denton Municipal Electric's service area and the city's water and sewer territory. Mayor Pro Tem Nick Stevens suggested the state maximum of 180 days, which would run through May 31, 2027, the last day of the legislative session. Council Member George Ferrie agreed. A longer pause would take a separate vote.
- More than two dozen people spoke in favor and none against, WFAA reported. The city says its two existing data centers have already generated $3 million in sales tax even though neither is fully online.
- Already-approved projects from Qumulus AI and Core Scientific are exempt. Denton's new rules requiring a special permit for any data center took effect Oct. 27. Under state law, a city that adopts a moratorium can't enact another one for two years.

**Today's Signal:** Timing the moratorium to end with the session is the point. Denton is betting the Legislature writes statewide rules before the city's one moratorium for two years runs out.

### 4. 📏 Arlington Drafts Its First Data Center Rulebook: 300 Feet From Homes, No Evaporative Cooling

- Arlington City Council saw proposed data center ordinances for the first time at a Tuesday, Oct. 27 meeting, the Fort Worth Report reported. The rules cover three types: stand-alone enterprise facilities, edge "equipment box" units, and data centers inside multitenant buildings.
- Stand-alone data centers would be allowed only in light industrial and industrial manufacturing districts with a specific use permit, or in a planned development that names the project. They'd have to sit at least 300 feet from homes, stay under 75 feet tall, skip evaporative cooling, and meet day and night noise limits, with testing before and after construction.
- Edge units would be capped at 200 square feet and 12 feet tall. Data centers inside other buildings would be allowed in any nonresidential zone if tied to the building's main use, with no evaporative cooling and no outdoor backup generators such as diesel units. Battery storage would be allowed, depending on size.
- Council Member Brittney Garcia-Dumas said she wants data centers "as far away" from residents as possible. Land development assistant director Kenneth Bloom said "This is a work in progress." Staff expect enterprise facilities on 5 to 10 acres, like homebuilder D.R. Horton's center off I-30, to be the most likely type.

**Today's Signal:** Arlington doesn't expect a hyperscale campus, and it's writing rules anyway. A ban on evaporative cooling is a water rule written into zoning, and if it holds up, other DFW suburbs without the land for big campuses will likely copy it.

### 5. 🏗️ DataBank's CEO Calls the Audit "Not That Onerous" and Keeps Building in Red Oak

- DataBank CEO Raul Martynek told DCD, in an interview published Thursday, Oct. 29, that the Dallas-based developer "weren't really expecting" Abbott's pause but will press ahead in Texas, which holds 40% of its 1.3 GW installed or under construction.
- That includes a fourth building at its campus in Red Oak, south of Dallas in Ellis County, which would add 60 MW.
- Martynek said the audit will "separate wheat from the chaff" among speculative land-and-queue plays, and that ERCOT's new disclosure requirements are "really not that onerous." He opposes a full moratorium but supports national AI regulation.
- DCD cited BloombergNEF estimates that 20% of planned U.S. data center capacity is at risk from Abbott's actions, and analyst estimates that the audit covers about 49.8 GW and could cost more than $8 billion by the first quarter of 2027.

**Today's Signal:** Established operators with permitted, powered sites see the audit as a filter that removes speculative competitors, not as a threat. The Red Oak expansion is a reminder that Ellis County's growth comes partly from expanding existing campuses, not only from new projects.

### 6. 📞 Five Days Out, Data Centers Show Up in AI Robocalls, a Rally Pledge and a Court Fight

- A pro-Paxton super PAC that took $10 million from Elon Musk, Lone Star Liberty, is running conversational AI robocalls that claim James Talarico "took $11 million from data center special interests," CNN reported Thursday, Oct. 29. The caller, "Jennifer," identifies itself as a "virtual staffer." The PAC says the figure comes from a Daily Caller report that counted Reid Hoffman's $10 million to a pro-Talarico super PAC. Nomorobo first detected the calls Oct. 23, mostly to landlines. Talarico's campaign said Musk is "firing up his data centers to create the only volunteers willing to spread lies."
- At an El Paso rally Wednesday, Oct. 28, Gina Hinojosa promised "a moratorium on any agreements" with data centers until new laws give communities "a seat at the table," and said sales tax breaks for data centers cost the state more than $1 billion a year, El Paso Matters reported.
- Hinojosa's open-records fight over Abbott's communications with Musk, which covers communications about data center development among other topics, CBS Austin reported, is on hold. A Travis County judge ordered access Monday, Oct. 26, and the 15th Court of Appeals stayed that order Tuesday. A hearing is set for Nov. 2.
- In Montgomery County, voters KPRC interviewed Oct. 28 were split on data centers, from good-paying jobs to "I rather have water than artificial intelligence."

**Today's Signal:** A super PAC is using AI calls to attack a candidate over data center money, and the governor's race is in court over emails that include data center talks. Whoever wins, data centers will be one of the first issues the next Legislature takes up.
