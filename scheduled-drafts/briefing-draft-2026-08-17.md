⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use
Monday, August 17, 2026

## 1. 🔌 ERCOT & Grid Signals — Audit's Real Scope Comes Into Focus

- At a special Public Utility Commission meeting Friday, ERCOT and PUCT officials disclosed for the first time that Gov. Abbott's audit order will cover roughly 250 to 300 projects — nearly all data centers — representing about 200 gigawatts of future demand, more than twice ERCOT's own peak-demand record set last month, according to testimony reported by The Texas Tribune.
- ERCOT general counsel Chad Seely said the verification work is being moved "to the front of the line" ahead of the Batch Zero interconnection study rather than run alongside it, meaning every affected project now faces the audit's added standards before any energization decision.
- PUC Project 59142's docket shows the deadline fight sharpening: ERCOT filed Aug. 10 for "good cause" exceptions to Batch Zero timelines, and by Aug. 16 the docket had drawn dueling public comments, including one from a Texas resident demanding stricter audit standards and another from data-center-linked filer Pallara, Inc. weighing in on ERCOT's request for more time.
- The same Friday meeting saw state Rep. Brad Buckley and more than 20 landowners press PUCT to deny certificate-of-convenience-and-necessity applications for the 765-kilovolt transmission lines meant to relieve grid congestion from the data-center buildout, arguing the approval process should wait for the Legislature to revise it.

**Today's Signal:** The emergency PUCT meeting turned Abbott's three-week-old audit order into hard numbers — 250 to 300 projects, 200 GW — while showing the same regulatory logjam now extends past interconnections to the transmission lines meant to carry all that power.

## 2. 🏗️ Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity — Permian Permits Land as Compliance List Grows

- New Era Energy & Digital said today it has secured construction permits for Texas Critical Data Centers, its Permian Basin project near Odessa, while its Phase 2 power partner has separately filed a standard TCEQ air-permit application to support roughly 550 megawatts of additional capacity there.
- State officials said Friday that at least 43 companies constructing data centers in Texas have now issued public comments supporting Abbott's power, water and community-impact standards, building on the group that already includes Meta and OpenAI, which signed on Aug. 11.

**Today's Signal:** With ERCOT's queue effectively frozen, developers are working around the bottleneck two ways at once — Permian Basin operators keep collecting permits for projects that plan to stay off the grid, while hyperscalers keep racking up signatures on Abbott's compliance pledge to speed everyone else back into it.

## 3. 🌎 Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ) — Hearing Night in San Antonio, Quiet in DFW Counties

- A TCEQ public hearing on Vantage Data Centers' Omicron Drive campus in San Antonio — which pairs on-site gas generation with more than 150 diesel backup generators — was set for 7 p.m. tonight per Texas Public Radio's reporting, though environmental advocates said today the agency appears to have called it off without public explanation; TCEQ had not confirmed a cancellation as of this morning.
- No new air-permit filings or public-notice postings turned up today in the eight DFW-area counties this briefing tracks (Collin, Ellis, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, Hood); the most recent activity there remains NRG's withdrawal of its air-permit application for a gas-fired plant near Glen Rose in Hood County, reported Aug. 12.

**Today's Signal:** Tonight's confusion over the San Antonio hearing is a preview of the friction DFW counties should expect once more of their own air-permit fights reach the public-comment stage.

## 4. 🏛️ Land-Use & Zoning Developments — Keller Lawyers Up, Westlake Votes Tonight

- Keller's city council voted unanimously overnight into today to formally oppose the data center proposed for neighboring Westlake and to retain outside legal counsel, after more than two dozen residents spoke out at a meeting that ran past midnight.
- Westlake's town council is scheduled to vote today on the development agreement for the project — now described in filings as a 94-acre, 1.1-million-square-foot facility roughly 506 feet from Keller backyards — despite the neighboring city's objections.
- In Collin County, the small town of Lucas has begun mobilizing against a project developers have not explicitly billed as a data center, after residents grew suspicious of a "commercial" rezoning request, the Dallas Morning News reported this week.

**Today's Signal:** The Westlake-Keller dispute has moved from public comment to legal threats in under a week, and Lucas's preemptive pushback shows other DFW suburbs are now scrutinizing "commercial" zoning requests before developers even say the words "data center."

## 5. ⚡ Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation — Pecos Plant Anchors the Statewide Tally

- Amazon's confirmed 7.65-gigawatt behind-the-meter gas plant for its Pecos County AI campus remains the largest single power-first commitment tracked statewide, letting the company bypass ERCOT's frozen interconnection queue entirely by generating its own power on-site.
- A Forbes analysis of Cleanview data published last month put total behind-the-meter gas capacity now in development for Texas data centers at roughly 90 GW, a scale advocates say is drawing power projects toward the state's less-stringent standard-permit process, which doesn't require the public notice a full ERCOT interconnection would trigger.
- In Hood County, the on-site-generation model hit a setback this month when NRG withdrew its air-permit application for the 2-gigawatt gas turbine meant to power the proposed Comanche Circle data center near Glen Rose — though the county's underlying approval for the data-center concept remains valid for two more years.

**Today's Signal:** Amazon's Pecos plant shows the power-first model working as designed — a total grid bypass — while Hood County shows its weak point: without ERCOT review forcing the issue, these projects still need a working power plant of their own, and that permit can fall through.
