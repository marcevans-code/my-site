Friday, August 14, 2026

## 1. 🔌 ERCOT & Grid Signals — Analysts Put a Price Tag on the Freeze

- BloombergNEF analysis reported this week estimates Gov. Abbott's Aug. 3 audit directive could delay as much as 49.8 GW of data-center load and cost developers up to $15 billion, with the firm calculating that roughly one-fifth of the entire U.S. data-center pipeline now sits inside Texas's paused interconnection queue.
- ERCOT's Batch Zero Large Load classification notices, originally due Aug. 7, remain suspended; the grid operator plans to ask the Public Utility Commission for a formal schedule exception at the PUCT's Aug. 20 open meeting rather than resume notifications unilaterally.
- The underlying numbers driving the standoff haven't moved: ERCOT's interconnection queue still sits near 474 GW of requested capacity, with data centers accounting for roughly 90% of that load, per the governor's own directive letter to PUCT and ERCOT.

**Today's Signal:** Wall Street analysts are now attaching hard dollar figures to Abbott's grid pause, turning what began as a political directive into a line item investors are pricing into Texas data-center deals.

## 2. 🏗️ Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity — Compliance Letters Pile Up as AWS Breaks Ground

- AWS has filed plans for a roughly 2,700-acre, four-building campus outside Houston in Wharton County, with each $300 million building slated to start construction Aug. 1 and the first three set to open by January 2027.
- Beyond the initial round of signatories, filings and statements this week show QTS Data Centers, Skybox, Digital Realty, Mara, Oracle, EdgeConneX and Fort Worth-based Stream Data Centers have also submitted written commitments to follow Gov. Abbott's power, water and community-impact standards.
- Google's letter to the governor touted a $40 billion Texas infrastructure and jobs investment, while OpenAI pledged to fund its own grid infrastructure and minimize water use — part of a broader industry push to defuse the political fight before it reaches Austin.

**Today's Signal:** With more than a dozen operators now on record agreeing to Abbott's rules, the compliance letter has become the industry's default response to the state audit — even as ground-up construction keeps moving in counties like Wharton without interruption.

## 4. 🏛️ Land-Use & Zoning Developments — Westlake Pushes Back: "We Told You in 2024"

- Westlake officials issued a formal public response defending the 87.9-acre data center campus its Planning and Zoning Commission approved Aug. 4, arguing the project sits in an industrial-zoned corridor and would boost town revenue by roughly 14%.
- The town's response points to a December 2024 meeting at which Keller's own leadership reportedly voiced support for the project, disputing Keller's more recent characterization of being blindsided — a discrepancy WFAA highlighted this week as the two cities trade public statements.
- Keller has not filed suit yet; Mayor Ross McMullin says he'll place formal legal action on a future council agenda if Westlake doesn't pause the project, leaving the dispute in a standoff rather than in court for now.
- Separately, Fort Worth's new 11-member Data Center & Infrastructure Commission — created alongside the council's Aug. 11 moratorium-process vote — is scheduled to deliver its first findings at a Nov. 3 work session.

**Today's Signal:** Westlake's rebuttal reframes the Keller fight as a dispute over who knew what and when, not just over land use — a preview of the paper trail other DFW suburbs will need if more of these fights go public.

## 5. ⚡ Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation — Amazon Details the Mitigation Plan Behind Its 7.65 GW Gas Plant

- Amazon confirmed it will buy power directly from Pacifico Energy's GW Ranch gas plant in Pecos County — 35 turbines totaling 7.65 GW, permitted for up to 33 million metric tons of CO2 per year, roughly 5% of Canada's total annual greenhouse gas emissions.
- To offset scrutiny, Amazon says the campus will add up to 750 MW of on-site solar and 1.8 GW of battery storage, and will rely on non-potable water for cooling rather than drawing on local supplies.
- Timeline details are now public: first power is targeted for Q1 2027, one gigawatt in service by 2028, and five-plus gigawatts by 2031.

**Today's Signal:** Amazon is the first hyperscaler to pair a Texas gas-mega-plant announcement with a specific solar-and-battery mitigation package — a template other operators facing the same emissions math will likely be pushed to copy.
