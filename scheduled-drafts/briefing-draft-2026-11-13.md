# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 13, 2026

(Covering Thursday, November 12 – Friday, November 13, 2026)

**Top Story:** Westlake's Town Council approved the development agreement for PowerHouse Data Centers' 1.1-million-square-foot campus on the Keller line Thursday night, a week after a $1.876 million settlement ended the neighbors' lawsuit.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🔇 Westlake Approves the PowerHouse Data Center Deal, With Keller's Noise Limits Attached

- Thursday, Nov. 12: Westlake's Town Council approved the development agreement for PowerHouse Data Centers' planned campus of four buildings totaling 1.1 million square feet on about 88 acres at the northeast corner of U.S. 377 and Westport Parkway. Keller says the site is 506 feet from its residents' backyards; Westlake has said its nearest home is about two-thirds of a mile away (WFAA; CBS News Texas).
- The vote follows the Nov. 5 settlement among the developer, the city of Keller and the Lakes at Marshall Ridge HOA. The developer owes $1.876 million within 45 days: $1.5 million for a noise mitigation fund, $300,000 for fire protection and $76,000 for Keller's legal and consulting costs (WFAA; CBS News Texas).
- The settlement caps noise at 49 decibels from 7 p.m. to 7 a.m. and 56 decibels during the day at residential property lines, requires a baseline noise study before opening, raises the berm from 8 to 10 feet, extends sound monitoring from one year to three, and limits Tier 4 diesel generator testing to 10 a.m.–4 p.m. (WFAA; CBS News Texas).
- The council also approved having property owner Circle T Owner LP pay Westlake's attorneys' and public relations fees from the lawsuit, and a new mutual aid agreement with Keller. A staff memo noted Westlake "has not agreed to allow another municipality to enforce" its ordinances, though CBS reports Keller gets contractual enforcement rights over some requirements through the settlement (WFAA; CBS News Texas).
- Mayor Kim Greaves: "We hate being called a bad neighbor, and hopefully this puts it behind us." The town expects about $5 million a year for the first 10 years, about a 14% revenue increase. The developer told planning commissioners in August the campus would run entirely on Oncor power and average about 165,000 gallons of water a day (WFAA).

**Today's Signal:** Keller didn't stop the project. It turned a lawsuit into a list of decibel caps, generator hours and a $1.5 million noise fund it can enforce outside its own city limits. Every city that borders a data center site in Texas now has a template for what a neighbor can get when it has no zoning power.

### 2. 💵 Fermi Lands a 600 MW Hyperscaler Deal for Project Matador, but No Lease Yet, and TensorWave Is Out

- Thursday, Nov. 12: Fermi (Nasdaq: FRMI) said an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler agreed to 600 MW of power at Project Matador, its campus near Amarillo on Texas Tech University System land, with delivery starting in 2027. Fermi is targeting late in the second quarter or early in the third (DCD; Startup Fortune).
- The customer is paying $200 million up front, which management expected by Nov. 13, plus $15 million under an early construction and site development agreement. The two are finalizing a $90 million structural steel and building materials deal. No lease has been signed, and Fermi wants crews mobilized by Dec. 1 (DCD; Startup Fortune).
- Fermi and TensorWave terminated the data center lease they signed in August, along with AMD's related credit support, leaving Matador without a signed tenant (DCD).
- CEO Lee McIntire: "Hyperscalers want sites that are ready with permitted power." Fermi says it holds a 6 GW TCEQ air permit with 5 GW more filed, and had five gas turbines and 4.6 miles of gas line on site as of Oct. 31 (DCD; Investing.com; Startup Fortune).
- Third-quarter net loss was $141.6 million with no revenue, cash was $315.5 million (about $286 million unrestricted) and net debt was $958.4 million. Shares, which hit $1.55 earlier this month, reached $2.15 Thursday against a $30 IPO price (Startup Fortune; DCD).

**Today's Signal:** McIntire's pitch is the air permit, not the land. Fermi got its 6 GW TCEQ authorization before Abbott's pause, which a new project can't easily get in Texas while the pause holds. The $200 million is a deposit on that permit, and the missing lease is the part to watch.

### 3. 🚀 SpaceX Puts $74 Billion Behind Starbase and Arizona, and Its Texas Gas Plant Stays Just Under the Federal NOx Line

- Thursday, Nov. 12: SpaceX announced plans to invest more than $74 billion in two AI supercomputing campuses: more than $24 billion at Starbase in the Rio Grande Valley and about $50 billion in Cochise County, Ariz. It said the Texas site will generate its own power and that it will spend $220 million to expand Brownsville's water supply (The Hill).
- "These investments will expand local tax bases, generate substantial new revenue for schools and public services," the company said (The Hill).
- SpaceX's Sept. 8 filing with TCEQ, under the standard permit for electric generating units, lists 35 temporary turbines (about 748 MW), with eight GE Vernova 7E turbines (about 708 MW) as an alternative. The registration was still pending as of Nov. 12 (Tech Times).
- The filing proposes a nitrogen oxide limit of 248.86 tons a year, 1.14 tons under the 250-ton federal threshold that would trigger major-source review. Startup and shutdown emissions aren't counted, and up to 50 such events per turbine per year are allowed. The 956 Report estimated roughly 458 tons if all 35 turbines ran at full output for a year (Tech Times).
- Starbase's commission takes up the tax abatement and development agreement Dec. 9 at 7 p.m.; that vote doesn't decide the TCEQ registration (Tech Times).

**Today's Signal:** A cap set 1.14 tons below the federal trigger is a design choice, not a coincidence. It keeps a 700-plus MW plant in the standard-permit lane, the same lane Abbott's pause may or may not cover, and that question will land on a TCEQ answering to a new governor.

### 4. 🌳 Forest Hill and Weston Gardens Neighbors Say Nobody Asked Them About Black Mountain

- Thursday, Nov. 12: WFAA reported on residents near Black Mountain Fort Worth, a proposed data center behind the historic Weston Gardens nursery between Forest Hill and Everman, near a power substation, as Fort Worth weighs a 90-day moratorium.
- "Why is it so close to us? Why are they putting it next to a historic garden center? Next to two schools?" said Amon Ibrahim, a landscape designer at Weston Gardens since 2014. Forest Hill resident Andrew Leos said his family learned of the project from a letter in the mail and is considering moving 45 minutes away (WFAA).
- Councilman Chris Nettles said Fort Worth can be a model for the country: "We have five data centers in the city of Fort Worth. They have not caused us any problems." He said the city will work with the Legislature and the new governor to protect residents (WFAA).

**Today's Signal:** Forest Hill residents live across a road from the site but can't vote in Fort Worth. That's the Keller problem again, and Westlake just showed how it gets resolved when the neighboring city has no leverage but a lawsuit.

### 5. 🚫 Kyle Moves Toward a San Marcos-Style Ban, and Laredo Activists Want the Same

- Thursday, Nov. 12: Kyle Councilwoman Claudia Zapata, who has announced she's stepping down, is introducing an ordinance to prohibit data centers citywide, using the same approach San Marcos adopted in June: define the use and don't permit it in any zoning district. It's expected to be discussed, and could be voted on, at the council's Tuesday, Nov. 17 meeting (KVUE).
- Zapata said she knows of no data center proposals in Kyle and wants to act before one arrives: "The last thing we need for our residents... are these high-intensity data processing facilities" (KVUE).
- In Laredo, KGNS reported reaction to Sen. Judith Zaffirini's SB 36, which would bar cities and counties from making or renewing certain development agreements with data centers and block certain property tax breaks, and SB 35, which would have ERCOT share confidential large-load information with the Texas Water Development Board. Mayor Victor Treviño called them "a step in the right direction." Rick Treviño of Fronteras Sin Data Centers said residents want "the right to say no to these outright" (KGNS).

**Today's Signal:** Kyle would be banning something nobody has proposed yet, which is the point: a preemptive ban is easier to pass than one aimed at a project with a landowner and a lawyer. If it passes Tuesday, the I-35 corridor between Austin and San Antonio has two cities that have said no in advance.

### 6. 🏛️ What Hinojosa Can Do on Day One, and What Has to Wait for the Senate

- Thursday, Nov. 12: KUT's Leigh Walden reported that Houston Advanced Research Center CEO John Hall expects Gov.-elect Hinojosa to extend Abbott's data center pause and possibly strengthen it, which he said is largely within a governor's authority because the pause began as an executive order. Ending tax breaks and giving communities a say would likely need bipartisan votes in a Republican Senate (KUT; Texas Public Radio).
- Because TCEQ and PUC commissioners serve six-year terms, Hinojosa may name a TCEQ commissioner as early as January, but her first PUC appointment may not come until September 2027 (KUT).
- Former UT Energy Institute deputy director Michael Webber said projects already underway will likely continue and that unwinding tax breaks negotiated under Abbott could be unprecedented and, in his view, possibly illegal (KUT).

**Today's Signal:** The PUC timeline is the overlooked one. The agency writing Texas's large-load interconnection and cost rules will be made up entirely of commissioners appointed before Hinojosa until at least September 2027, so the parts of her agenda that run through the PUC will need the Legislature to write them into law.

### 7. 🧱 STN Plans 400 MW in Odessa as Ector County Takes Up Project Landman

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: STN One said its Project Landman campus in Odessa and Ector County, under construction since May, is the first to combine its land, power, factory-built data center and AI computing businesses. Its first 2 MW modular unit was commissioned July 31, and the campus is planned for 400 MW initially and up to 1.6 GW (Construction Review).
- STN says the site will pair on-site generation and battery storage with a utility connection but hasn't named the generation technology, capacity or utility. It says cooling is closed-loop with no process water discharge (Construction Review).
- Thursday, Nov. 12: Ector County's commissioners court agenda included a Project Landman presentation and authorization for county officials to sign related documents. No outcome was reported (Construction Review).

**Today's Signal:** Ector County now has two campuses, Landman and New Era's TCDC, that say they'll bring their own power. Neither has said what fuel or how much on-site generation its next phase will use, and that's a TCEQ question the county can't answer for them.

### 8. ☀️ Sabey Signs a 10-Year Solar Deal in Pecos County

- This week: Sabey Data Centers, which runs a site in Austin, signed a 10-year virtual power purchase agreement for 43.5 MW from Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners' Greasewood Solar Farm in Girvin, Pecos County, operating since 2021. It's expected to generate about 130 GWh a year (DCD, Nov. 12).

**Today's Signal:** A virtual PPA with an existing solar farm adds no new megawatts to ERCOT. It's a paper match for Sabey's 2029 net-zero goal, not a new source of supply.

No new Texas Tribune data center story after Nov. 10, no ERCOT release after Nov. 10, and TCEQ hasn't posted a release since Oct. 21. The PUC's large-load dockets were quiet apart from a confidential SPS exhibit, and nothing turned up on air permits in the eight tracked DFW counties.
