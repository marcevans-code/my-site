# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

**(Latest updates through August 26, 2026)**

**Top Story:** BloombergNEF pegs the real cost of Gov. Abbott's data-center grid audit at up to $15 billion and 49.8 gigawatts of delayed load, as ERCOT's CEO tells lawmakers the frozen Batch Zero review won't even launch until the end of the year.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 💸 BloombergNEF Puts a Price Tag on the Audit: $15 Billion and Counting

- BloombergNEF estimates Gov. Greg Abbott's grid-connection audit could delay 49.8 gigawatts of data-center load and cost projects up to $15 billion combined, turning what began as a regulatory pause into a capital-markets story, Capacity Media reported today.
- Testifying before a Texas House committee last week — recapped today by Yahoo/The Cool Down — ERCOT CEO Pablo Vegas said the Batch Zero large-load review "won't launch in September" and now likely won't start "until the end of the year," later than the Dec. 10 verification deadline the PUC had previously set.
- More than 40 companies, including Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Amazon Web Services, have publicly backed the audit rather than fight it; Data Center Coalition EVP Dan Diorio called it a chance to "showcase the good actors" while pushing regulators to move quickly, and Texas operator QTS welcomed it on similar terms.
- The Texas Water Development Board is now leaning on that same ERCOT interconnection data to estimate data-center water demand, since the state's official water plan still doesn't account for it — a gap Democratic gubernatorial candidate Gina Hinojosa is citing as proof the audit doesn't go far enough, calling today for a special session.
- In Ellis County alone, ERCOT hearing testimony noted three Red Oak data-center campuses already operating or under construction could add $11 billion to the city's tax base, with another $30 billion in projects still being pursued — the kind of local stakes fueling both sides of the delay.

**Today's Signal:** Texas built its national pitch on speed; BNEF's $15 billion estimate is the first hard number putting a price on trading that speed for scrutiny, and it lands the same week lenders elsewhere have started pricing community opposition into data-center financing.

### 2. ⚡ Landowner Notification Failures Pile Up Against Oncor's 765-kV Lines

- State Rep. Ken King (R-Canadian) wrote to ERCOT and PUCT on Aug. 24 saying Oncor left 1,400 landowners out of public meetings on the Bell County East-to-Big Hill 765-kV line until after the application was filed, leaving them roughly three weeks to intervene versus the ten months given to landowners notified on schedule.
- Administrative law judges have already recommended PUCT deny the Bell County East-to-Big Hill application on those notification grounds; a second ALJ panel separately found Oncor failed to notify more than 1,600 landowners on the Dinosaur Switch-to-Longshore Switch 765-kV line.
- Both lines are part of the roughly 1,200-mile, three-segment STEP Permian project meant to move power from East Texas to the gas-rich Permian Basin; PUCT is scheduled to take up all three segments on Aug. 28.
- State Reps. Shelby Slawson and Brad Buckley publicly backed King's letter, with Slawson echoing Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick's call to deny every pending 765-kV application until lawmakers can overhaul the process in the 2027 session; Oncor disputes the notification findings, saying it has "consistently engaged" with landowners and state officials.

**Today's Signal:** King's letter ties the transmission-line notice failures directly to the data-center fight, inviting ERCOT and PUCT to write "legislative guardrails" giving counties more say — the same ask now driving Fort Worth's moratorium push and Hinojosa's campaign platform.

### 3. 🏙️ Fort Worth Becomes the Next Data-Center Flashpoint on the Campaign Trail

- Democratic gubernatorial nominee Gina Hinojosa drew more than 300 people to a Fort Worth rally Tuesday night, calling Tarrant County "ground zero" for the November election and pledging to pass a statewide data-center moratorium if elected.
- Hinojosa cited Texas Tribune reporting that data-center tax exemptions could cost the state more than $3.2 billion in lost sales tax revenue, and said she'd end those breaks for "global corporations" while cutting property taxes for homeowners instead.
- Fort Worth already hosts four operating data centers totaling roughly 3 million square feet, and developer Black Mountain is pursuing a $10 billion data-center project in the city; the Fort Worth City Council could vote on a 90-day moratorium as early as next year.
- A Red Oak resident told the rally crowd she hears a "constant buzz" from a data center less than three miles from a friend's home — echoing the noise complaints already driving nuisance fights elsewhere in North Texas.

**Today's Signal:** Hinojosa is the first statewide candidate to campaign explicitly on a data-center moratorium rather than just tighter rules, turning what's largely been a county-by-county zoning fight into a top-of-ticket November question.

### 4. 💧 A New Wastewater Filing Surfaces West of Wichita Falls

- Delaware-registered Dynamo Ventures LLC has applied to TCEQ for permission to discharge up to 95,000 gallons per day of treated wastewater into Lake Wichita from a proposed data-center site on 3-Way Road in Archer County, Times Record News reported today.
- The filing sits outside the eight DFW-area counties this briefing tracks most closely, but it's a reminder the same water-permitting fights already playing out in Hood and Somervell counties can surface anywhere developers are scouting land near lake or river access.

**Today's Signal:** It's a small filing next to today's billion-dollar audit numbers, but it's exactly the kind of routine-looking permit that turned into a sustained fight once Hood County residents noticed one just like it.

Quiet today on new TCEQ air-permit filings in the eight tracked DFW-area counties specifically, and on new dedicated on-site gas generation announcements beyond projects already tracked.
