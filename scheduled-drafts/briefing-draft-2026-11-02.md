# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 2, 2026

(Covering Saturday, October 31 – Monday, November 2, 2026)

**Top Story:** PUC staff told commissioners late Friday to approve Comanche Peak's deal to power an Amazon data center straight from the nuclear plant. Staff wants ERCOT's conditions attached, though, and turned down the legal protection Amazon and the plant owner asked for.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚛️ PUC Staff Backs Amazon's Direct Line to Comanche Peak, With ERCOT's Conditions Attached

- PUC Market Analysis Division staff filed a statement of position in Docket 59399 at 4:14 p.m. Friday, Oct. 30, recommending that the commission approve the net metering arrangement between Comanche Peak Power Company LLC and Amazon Data Services "subject to the conditions proposed by ERCOT." Senior counsel R. Floyd Walker signed it.
- The arrangement sits at the two-unit Comanche Peak nuclear plant in Somervell County, next to Hood County. Comanche Peak filed in February, and Amazon joined an amended application on May 19. Vistra's 20-year deal with Amazon covers 1.2 GW of the 2.4 GW plant, with deliveries set to begin in 2027, Utility Dive reported in August.
- ERCOT's Oct. 13 study recommended approval with conditions. The applicants don't dispute what those conditions require, but they want the commission to rely on their own voluntary commitments instead. Staff said that would be "theoretically possible but practically unnecessary." It said this arrangement should carry the same conditions, in the same wording, as the Freestone (Docket 58872, approved May 7) and Crusoe Goodnight (Docket 58881, approved April 23) arrangements, so compliance can be monitored the same way.
- Staff also opposed the applicants' request for a finding that the arrangement is lawful and that any "reasonable cause" objection under PURA § 39.169(c) can't be raised after the final order. Staff said it hasn't "exhaustively studied" every issue, noting that the plant is subject to Nuclear Regulatory Commission rules the PUC hasn't reviewed. United Cooperative Services had called the requested finding "unnecessary and inappropriate at this late stage." Oncor didn't object to ERCOT's conditions but said it may have concerns, and TNMP took no position.
- The case is being decided on written filings. An Oct. 26 order granted the applicants' request and canceled the prehearing conference and hearing. On Sunday, Nov. 1, a United Cooperative Services member-owner filed a formal public comment objecting on transmission-impact and ratepayer-cost grounds.

**Today's Signal:** Amazon and Vistra didn't ask to skip ERCOT's terms. They asked for a ruling that would close the door on future objections. Staff's answer is that this deal should look exactly like the two earlier approvals it cites, Freestone and Crusoe Goodnight, with no extra protection. Because there's no hearing, the next step is a commission vote. Somervell County is next door to Hood County, where commissioners rejected the Comanche Circle settlement last month.

### 2. 🏜️ DCD Reports ServerDome Has Broken Ground in Hudspeth County, Off the Grid

- Data Center Dynamics reported Monday, Nov. 2, that ServerDome Global has started construction on its Hueco Ranch campus in the Desert Haven area of Hudspeth County, east of El Paso. DCD put the price at $10 billion and said phase one reportedly has tenants.
- DCD described the campus as 350 MW across up to ten domed buildings on about 700 acres of a 2,000-acre former ranch and oil-and-gas site. It would run on behind-the-meter gas, with some geothermal generation according to some reports, and closed-loop cooling to cut water use. The company says each prefabricated dome can support about 20 MW and can be delivered in as little as 12 months.
- ServerDome's partner is Kaufman & Company, and its affiliate Lonestar Kaufman is a joint-venture partner on the Texas and Utah projects. DCD said the company has also targeted a site near Wharton, outside Houston.
- Those numbers don't match earlier reports. The company told residents Oct. 26 its campus would have its own 1 GW gas plant. Other coverage has used a $15 billion figure, and CEO David Keegan told reporters last week he was aiming for dirt work in December. DCD didn't say whether the gas generation has a TCEQ air permit, the kind of approval Abbott's Sept. 21 directive froze for data center projects. It also didn't report what Keegan told residents at his Oct. 31 Q&A or what commissioners did with the road use agreement.

**Today's Signal:** A project nobody in Hudspeth County had heard of a month ago is now reported to be under construction. It's skipping the ERCOT queue, so the grid audit doesn't slow it down. That leaves its gas generation permits and its county road deal as the only checks visible from the outside, and the status of neither has been reported.

### 3. 🏛️ Austin Plans to Regulate Data Centers Through Its Business Code First, With a Vote Possible Dec. 3

- An Austin city memo released Oct. 28 lays out a two-phase plan, KXAN reported Saturday, Oct. 31. First, the city would define "data center" and add server facilities to Title 4, the business regulations chapter of city code. Then it would set land-use rules through zoning after reviewing peer cities and doing "targeted engagement." KXAN's headline compared the approach to how the city regulates adult businesses.
- Council member Marc Duchen said the city moved from the Land Development Code to the Business Code so regulation can start sooner. Austin Energy and Austin Water would also send council monthly notices of service requests from large-load customers.
- Council could vote on the Title 4 changes Dec. 3. A broader framework could be released in February or March 2027, with final rules by May 2027.
- Austin Energy had heard from 13 potential data center projects as of late September. Staff said not all are likely to be built and none are close to hyperscale. District 7 council member Mike Siegel said residents want assurance these facilities won't be put near them. Jess Heck, who leads Texas government affairs for the Data Center Coalition, warned against banning large data centers outright or by leaving them out of every zoning district.

**Today's Signal:** San Antonio decided last week to vote on each project. Austin is going the other way, writing rules into its business code before it touches zoning. Either way, two of Texas's largest cities now intend to decide where data centers go, at the same time the state's governor's race is being fought over the industry.

### 4. 🗳️ The Weekend Before Election Day, Both Sides Make Data Centers Part of the Closing Argument

- Gina Hinojosa's campaign released an ad Sunday, Nov. 1, called "Priceless," set to air statewide during the Cowboys game. Among its line items: "Taxpayer-funded handouts for data centers: $40,453,361.81."
- ABC News reported Saturday, Oct. 31, that an October ABC News/Washington Post/Ipsos poll found 69% of Texans oppose new data center construction in their communities. ABC rates the governor's race Lean Republican. Bisnow reported Sunday that Hinojosa trailed by 3 points in recent polling, that both candidates support some form of moratorium, and that Spark AI data show Texas data center approvals fell 5% in the third quarter.
- Developers made the opposite case. At the Bloomberg Invest conference at Dallas's Joule Hotel, Crow Holdings' Michael Hyun called a crackdown on data centers "economic suicide," The Real Deal reported Sunday. Hines co-CEO Laura Hines-Pierce said developers are responsible for how projects fit their communities.
- Hinojosa's suit over Abbott's Elon Musk emails is on hold. On Oct. 27, the 15th Court of Appeals stayed Travis County Judge Catherine Mauzy's Oct. 26 order letting her see the unredacted records, the Austin Chronicle reported. Hinojosa had cited the environmental impact of data centers as one reason she needs them. Earlier editions listed a Nov. 2 hearing on the case. The Chronicle didn't say whether that hearing will still go forward.

**Today's Signal:** Hinojosa's ad puts a dollar figure on data center incentives, down to the cent. Developers in Dallas are warning about what limits would cost. Neither side is talking about the grid audit or the permit freeze, which stay in place whoever wins Tuesday.

### 5. 🪑 Rick Perry Loses His Fermi Board Vote, but Keeps the Seat for Now

- At Fermi's annual shareholder meeting Friday, Oct. 30, former Gov. Rick Perry and director Jeffrey S. Stein each won a majority of votes cast but not a majority of all shares, which the company's bylaws require, the Texas Tribune reported. Fermi says both stay on the board "until their successors are duly elected." Vote tallies are due at the SEC within four business days.
- Fermi blamed co-founder and former CEO Toby Neugebauer, saying he voted against Perry in violation of a founders' agreement. Neugebauer says the agreement didn't apply. Fermi wouldn't say whether it will sue. It removed Neugebauer as CEO for cause in April.
- Perry owns about 16 million shares, worth about $41 million, down from close to half a billion dollars at the peak last fall. Fermi stock is down more than 90% from its high.
- Project Matador is on about 5,000 acres Fermi leases from the Texas Tech University System near Amarillo, with a private "HyperGrid" planned to use gas, nuclear, solar and batteries. Its NRC license application for a large light-water reactor is still pending. TensorWave, an AI cloud startup, was announced in August as the first tenant.

**Today's Signal:** The fight at the Panhandle's biggest proposed campus right now isn't over permits. It's among the founders. A company that needs investors to fund reactors just showed it can't reelect its best-known director.

### 6. 🔇 Taylor Drafts Noise Limits Its Approved Data Centers Won't Have to Meet

- Inside Climate News reported Sunday, Nov. 1, that Taylor's draft zoning amendments would cap data center noise at 50 decibels from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. and 45 decibels overnight. Under state law, the limits wouldn't apply to projects already approved, and the city expects to adopt the code in 2027.
- That leaves out the three projects already moving forward: the 664-acre Taylor Technology Campus approved Oct. 8, KDC's 360 MW Project Comal, which still needs rezoning, and Blueprint's 60 MW facility on the former Bland family parkland. ICN estimates the three would use about 1.42 GW combined.
- Attorney General Ken Paxton's investigation into the Blueprint site, announced Sept. 17, is still pending, as is residents' appeal of the lawsuit dismissed in October 2025 for lack of standing. The city refused to put the HALT Taylor coalition's 1,400-signature moratorium petition on the November ballot, saying state law doesn't allow zoning changes by popular vote.

**Today's Signal:** By the time Taylor's noise rules take effect, the projects residents complained about will already have their approvals. It's what happens to small cities that write rules after the deals are signed.

TCEQ's Nov. 4 commissioners' agenda has no gas generation or data center items, and its November hearing calendar has no air-permit meetings in the tracked counties. PUC filings from Oct. 30 through Monday show no new Batch Zero complaints. The existing cases drew only paperwork, including Skybox's protective orders and a certificate of service from ACS DC Hicks Field Road, and there's no ruling yet on Medina County Power's appeal. ERCOT's news page and the Texas Tribune have nothing newer than Oct. 30.
