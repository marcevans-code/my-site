## 🏗️ 1. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity

**Dallas-Based Mousterian and Samsung Heavy Industries Move Toward the First Floating AI Data Center in the U.S.**

- Dallas-based Mousterian Corporation and South Korea's Samsung Heavy Industries signed an engineering contract advancing their planned moored, floating data center (FDC) — the partnership's first U.S. deployment — with a pilot site set to sit adjacent to an existing combined-cycle gas plant near Houston (Data Center Knowledge, Aug. 4; PR Newswire, Aug. 4; Smart Maritime Network, Aug. 4).
- Each floating unit is designed to deliver 50 megawatts of critical IT capacity using bulk non-evaporative cooling, which the companies say requires no potable water and produces no process-water discharge (The Register, Aug. 5).
- The design leans on locating next to power plants that already have grid and cooling-water access, letting a floating campus bypass both a new interconnection request and a new water permit — the two friction points currently driving Gov. Abbott's audit and DFW cities' zoning fights.
- Mousterian and Samsung say further deployments are planned for other U.S. markets beyond the Houston pilot; no DFW-area site has been named.

**Today's Signal:** A Dallas company just proposed sidestepping both of Texas's biggest 2026 data-center fights — the grid queue and the water permit — by putting the data center on a barge next to a plant that already has both.

## 🏛️ 2. Land-Use & Zoning Developments

**Fort Worth Council Gets Specifics Ahead of Tomorrow's Three-Item Vote**

- Fort Worth's City Council takes up three distinct data-center items at its Aug. 11 meeting: a proposed 90-day moratorium on new development, creation of a standing data-center review committee, and new mandatory filing requirements for operators.
- The filing requirements under consideration would require water-efficient cooling technology, regular reporting of electric and water usage, and proof that a project won't raise electric costs for existing Fort Worth ratepayers.
- Also on the table: repealing local sales-tax exemptions and development incentives currently available to data-center projects — a notable reversal from the abatement packages DFW cities have used to attract these facilities.
- Because the Zoning Commission voted 7-4 in July to recommend denial of the city's original ordinance, any of Tuesday's measures need a three-fourths council supermajority — at least 9 of 11 votes — to pass.

**Today's Signal:** Fort Worth's Tuesday vote isn't just a pause anymore — pairing a moratorium with a proposal to strip data centers' sales-tax breaks would be one of the sharpest incentive reversals any Texas city has floated this year.
