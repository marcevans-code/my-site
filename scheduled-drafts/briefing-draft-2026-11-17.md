## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 💸 Texas Cities and Counties Have Given Data Centers More Than $1 Billion in Local Tax Breaks for 465 Guaranteed Jobs

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: The Texas Tribune's first statewide count of local data center incentives found 53 agreements covering 34 data centers, worth at least $1 billion in forgone property taxes. Together they guarantee 465 jobs, and about half have no job requirement at all (The Texas Tribune, Paul Cobler and Dan Keemahill).
- The Tribune calls it an undercount. The comptroller's database of local agreements doesn't flag data centers, developers often sign through shell companies (Meta was "Wurldwide" in El Paso and "Winner, LLC" in Fort Worth), and many local governments didn't report the financial details state law requires (The Texas Tribune).
- El Paso's deal with Meta is more than half the total: an 80% break over 35 years, projected at $550 million when signed, for 50 required jobs. The project has since grown from $1.5 billion to more than $10 billion (The Texas Tribune).
- In Fort Worth, where Meta has run a data center since 2017, the city is expected to rebate at least $271 million over 20 years for 40 required jobs, after raising property tax rates in September to close a projected $94 million 2027 shortfall. Fort Worth officials declined to comment (The Texas Tribune).
- In Ellis County, Red Oak gives Google up to 85% off its city property taxes through 2031 for 40 jobs and $300 million in investment, with $2.8 million abated so far. Compass's 2.6-million-square-foot Red Oak campus has a rebate of at least 75% from Ellis County. Neighbor Benny Cowart, 70, said buyers won't come near his appraised value: "They don't have to pay, they don't hire anybody" (The Texas Tribune).
- Other DFW deals in the count: Equinix's 2021 Dallas County agreement (25 jobs for about $910,000 over 10 years), Garland's 2015 deal to build a substation and pay RagingWire $1.5 million for fiber, and Lancaster's 2024 promise of up to $1.5 million in infrastructure reimbursements (The Texas Tribune).
- At least 25 data centers get both the state sales tax exemption and a local property tax deal. In July, comptroller officials told lawmakers only 20 data centers had been audited for the sales tax program, and one in five had missed its jobs requirement (The Texas Tribune).
- Sen. Paul Bettencourt (R–Houston) said his staff could find only 10 local data center agreements and that he plans a bill package to standardize reporting: "80% of the problem is just finding out what's out there" (The Texas Tribune).

**Today's Signal:** Red Oak's mayor says the deals are how the town got its parks and roads. Fort Worth's numbers make the other case: the city is rebating at least $271 million to Meta while raising taxes to close a deficit. The bills already filed (Zaffirini's SB 36, Perez's HB 732, Perez's disclosure bill) are written for future deals. Every agreement in the Tribune's count would keep running unless it's renewed or sweetened after Sept. 1, 2027.

### 2. 🏗️ Core Scientific Files a $1.3 Billion Building in Hunt County, Where AMD Plans to Lease 110 MW

- Monday, Nov. 16: Telios Quinlan One LLC filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation for a 341,325-square-foot data center core-and-shell building at 4852 County Road 3507 in Quinlan, about 53 miles northeast of Dallas (DCD).
- The $1.3 billion building is scheduled for construction from November 2026 to September 2028. The mostly vacant site sits near Acciona Energía's 190 MW Cunningham battery storage project (DCD).
- Core Scientific bought Telios Quinlan One and its 265-acre site in March for $232.5 million. It has said the campus could support 285 MW of leasable capacity (430 MW gross), begin energizing in 2027 and reach full power by 2029, with an on-site substation (DCD).
- AMD is set to lease 110 MW there. Core Scientific also said this week it plans to fully exit Bitcoin mining by year-end to focus on AI infrastructure (DCD).

**Today's Signal:** Construction is scheduled to start this month. Without power, though, it's an empty building: 430 MW gross is far above ERCOT's 75 MW threshold, and new energizations for data centers that size are paused until the audit clears. The TDLR filing gets the building started, but the power hookup that decides when it opens is in the queue Hinojosa inherits.

### 3. 🚪 Southlake's Giovanni Capriglione Is Leaving the House 54 Days Early to Keep Working on Data Centers

- Friday, Nov. 13 (reported Monday, Nov. 16): Rep. Giovanni Capriglione (R–Southlake), who represents House District 98 in Tarrant County, told supporters in a letter that he will resign Nov. 19 instead of serving out his term to Jan. 12 as he had promised (Chron).
- "That leadership will depend on the data centers and energy infrastructure we build to support them. As I step away from the legislature I look forward to continuing this work," the letter says (Chron).
- Capriglione is president of the former Texas Blockchain Council, which renamed itself the Digital Infrastructure Network in August, opened an Austin office alongside its Dallas headquarters, and widened its focus to AI and data centers (Chron).

**Today's Signal:** The bill-writing for the 2027 session is already underway, and one of the House's most experienced tech-policy Republicans will be working on it from outside the chamber. On the industry side, he'll arrive as a former lawmaker who knows how the committees work.

### 4. 🌬️ Soluna Lines Up a New 118 MW Tenant for Kati 2 After the First One's Exclusivity Ran Out

- Monday, Nov. 16: In its third-quarter results, Soluna Holdings said the prior prospective tenant's exclusivity period at Kati 2 ended. The company remarketed the Willacy County site and in November signed a non-binding letter of intent with a new prospective tenant for 118 IT MW at the planned 350-plus MW campus (Soluna, PR Newswire).
- Soluna said construction began at Kati 2 and at Dorothy 3 in Briscoe County in October. ERCOT gave both campuses Base Load classifications in September for 266 MW of existing interconnection (166 MW at Kati, 100 MW at Dorothy), keeping that capacity out of the Batch Zero allocation pending verification (Soluna).
- The Briscoe County Commissioners Court approved a Chapter 312 abatement for Dorothy 3's first phase on Nov. 9. Soluna also received state sales tax exemptions for both projects (Soluna).
- Soluna reported a $34.3 million third-quarter net loss, including $3.0 million in unplanned turbine repairs at the Briscoe Wind Farm, and ended September with $109.3 million in cash (Soluna).

**Today's Signal:** Friday's "almost $4 billion" figure for Kati 2 came with a tenant Soluna no longer has. A non-binding letter of intent with a new prospect is a long way from a signed lease. Soluna's strongest card is the ERCOT Base Load classification, which most new projects in the queue don't have.

### 5. 🛑 Lavon Takes Its Final Vote Tonight on a 90-Day Data Center Moratorium

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: The Lavon City Council's 6:30 p.m. agenda includes the second and final reading of Ordinance No. 2026-11-03. It would impose a temporary 90-day moratorium on permits, plats, rezonings, site plans and certificates of occupancy for any development with a data center as a principal or accessory use (City of Lavon agenda; CBS News Texas).
- It's on the consent agenda, so it could pass without separate discussion unless a council member pulls it. The ordinance includes written findings, waivers and limited exceptions, and an expiration date (City of Lavon agenda).
- The same agenda has a public hearing on voluntary annexation of 268.769 acres called Elevon North (City of Lavon agenda). Lavon's 2024 Elevon zoning change already allows a data center on two other parcels of about 79 acres, which the city and its economic development corporation have been marketing (Heard Together).

**Today's Signal:** Lavon zoned for a data center two years ago and is now pausing new ones. It joins Fort Worth and Denton among North Texas cities reaching for a pause to buy time until the Legislature decides what cities can actually regulate.

### 6. 🏘️ Georgetown's Planning Commission Hears the 28-Acre Inner Loop Data Center Tonight

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Georgetown's Planning and Zoning Commission holds a 6 p.m. public hearing on rezoning 28 acres at Northeast Inner Loop and East University Avenue from retail and civic uses for the Inner Loop Exchange planned unit development. City Council would make the final decision Dec. 8 if the commission recommends approval (FOX 7 Austin).
- City documents describe up to 290,000 square feet and about 68 MW, with an Oncor substation on the site's northern edge (FOX 7 Austin; Austin American-Statesman via Yahoo News).
- Residents of Churchill Farms, Riverhill, Carlson Place and Indian Creek met Sunday. Riverhill's Kirk Cunningham cited Oncor outages lasting "14, 15 hours at a time" (FOX 7 Austin). Steve Valadez said he had seen no flyers, signs or meetings: "I'm okay if it's done smartly and the residents are taken into account" (Statesman).
- Mayor Josh Schroeder defended the project on social media, citing closed-loop cooling that uses almost no water. The Statesman noted Rep. Cody Vasut's HB 682, which would let cities and counties prohibit commercial or industrial facilities within 1,000 feet of a home if they produce more than 50 dBA of sound, vibration above one hertz, or brightness above five foot-candles at the property line (Statesman; Texas Legislature Online).

**Today's Signal:** At 68 MW, the project is under ERCOT's 75 MW large-load threshold. That keeps it out of the queue backlog and the Batch Zero audit, but not out of tonight's hearing. HB 682's 1,000-foot, 50-decibel test is the kind of rule neighbors across the street from a data center could use, and it has a Republican author.

### 7. ⏳ Lawyers and Analysts Map the Weeks Between ERCOT's Audit and Hinojosa's Inauguration

- Monday, Nov. 16: Gibson Dunn's client alert and Latitude Media's analysis both put the PUC's review of ERCOT's audit at a Dec. 17 open meeting, a week after ERCOT's Dec. 10 verification and community impact reports. ERCOT's calendar now lists a PUCT meeting and a Large Load Working Group meeting that day (Gibson Dunn; Latitude Media; ERCOT calendar).
- Gibson Dunn said Abbott's TCEQ pause is a directive with no end date, not a rule. If permitting resumes after Dec. 17, about a month would remain before Hinojosa's Jan. 19 inauguration. ERCOT's energization pause doesn't cover 25–75 MW loads, and the April 9 Batch Zero study deadline is expected to slip (Gibson Dunn).
- Latitude counted about 200 projects totaling 66.4 GW provisionally keeping their place, more than 350 projects and 300 GW shut out, and nearly 170 GW still awaiting a decision. Jefferies, it reported, expects a legislative compromise and significant Texas data center construction "in 2028 and beyond," and NRG and Vistra shares each fell 14% after the election (Latitude Media).
- Gibson Dunn also flagged that PUC commissioners Kathleen Jackson's and Patrick Rhode's terms expire Sept. 1, 2027, along with TCEQ Chair Brooke Paup's on Aug. 31, 2027. Commissioner Courtney Hjaltman is serving in holdover, and recess appointees must be renominated within the first 10 days of the session (Gibson Dunn).

**Today's Signal:** A project that passes Batch Zero on Dec. 17 still needs ERCOT to restart energizations and TCEQ to resume permits. Abbott will be in office for those decisions only until Jan. 19. After that, the same pause is Hinojosa's to keep or lift, and her appointments will shape the PUC that enforces it.

### 8. ⚡ NRG Puts a $33.4 Million Price on One Hour Off the Texas Grid

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Reuters reported that big power users can avoid millions in transmission charges by cutting grid use during a few peak hours a year. Houston-based NRG Energy estimates that a large Texas data center can avoid $33.4 million in transmission costs by staying off the grid for one hour, by cutting load or running backup generators (Reuters via KFGO).
- Reuters also cited an August PUC filing in which Google objected to a scenario that would have it pay about $1.4 billion in transmission charges over 20 years. Spring, Texas, resident Bailey Wells said her summer bill neared $600 (Reuters).
- The Data Center Coalition said peak shaving lowers costs for all customers because less capacity and transmission has to be built (Reuters).

**Today's Signal:** Texas sets transmission charges by usage during peak hours, and a data center that can drop its load or switch to on-site generation for that hour can avoid most of its share. That makes behind-the-meter gas a way to cut transmission bills, not just a backup plan. The PUC and Legislature will have to decide who covers the difference.

TCEQ hasn't posted a release since Oct. 21, and there were no new air permit actions in the eight tracked DFW counties. ERCOT's release page still stops at Nov. 10. At the PUC, the only new filings were procedural: issues lists from the Data Center Coalition and Commission Staff in SPS's large-load tariff case, Docket 60332. No new behind-the-meter generation projects were announced.
