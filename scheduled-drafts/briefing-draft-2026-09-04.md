# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through September 4, 2026)*

**Top Story:** Fort Worth's new data-center oversight commission — with an industry seat next to its most vocal critics — began its work today, the same week Wall Street started pricing Texas's statewide grid-connection freeze into power-company stock prices.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🏛️ Fort Worth's Data-Center Referees Take the Field

- Fort Worth's new 11-member Data Center & Infrastructure Commission formally begins its work today, Sept. 4, according to the Fort Worth Star-Telegram — one day after the city confirmed its full roster.
- TCU environmental-studies director Mike Slattery, who is leading the panel, called the assignment "very new territory," adding: "It's laced with politics. There's lots of misinformation and rhetoric out there... My job is to navigate all of these issues from a fact-based evidence perspective."
- The voting roster pairs QTS Data Centers senior vice president Ryan Kuithe with some of the commission's most consistent critics, including Sierra Club Greater Fort Worth's John MacFarlane and 2871 Community Coalition organizer Krista Erbe, plus ten supporting academics from North Texas universities and Cornell.
- The commission's findings on power, water, land use, neighborhood impact and economic development are due to the City Council and Mayor Mattie Parker at a Nov. 3 work session — just before Fort Worth's first possible public hearing on a data-center moratorium.

**Today's Signal:** Slattery's own framing — an evidence-based commission that seats the industry next to its own critics — is the test case for whether North Texas cities can regulate this industry without a blanket moratorium, and it now has a start date on the record.

### 2. 💸 Texas's Grid Freeze Shows Up in Power Stocks

- Shares of Texas power generators moved directly on the state's data-center grid freeze this week: Vistra fell 7% and NRG Energy dropped 16% in a single trading session, according to a Wall Street Journal markets tracker, with NRG's decline compounding a separate earnings disappointment.
- The selloff follows a Reuters analysis finding electricity requests tied to large power users — mostly data centers — now exceed 700 gigawatts nationally, more than ten times current U.S. data-center power use, with utilities like Chicago-based Exelon already cutting "high-probability" demand forecasts by 40% once they required upfront collateral from developers.
- Data-center demand uncertainty has already cost real money elsewhere: growth in existing and forecast demand drove a $29.4 billion increase in capacity costs across the 13-state PJM grid over roughly the past four capacity auctions, according to grid monitor Monitoring Analytics.
- Texas Public Utility Commission Chairman Thomas Gleeson's framing of the state's own audit — regulators can't plan the grid without knowing "what is real" — is now the same question investors are asking about the generators that would supply that demand.

**Today's Signal:** The audit Texas ordered to sort real demand from "ghost" demand has jumped from a regulatory filing to a line item on the state's power-generator stock prices — proof this isn't just an Austin policy fight anymore.

### 3. 🏙️ Dallas Starts Drafting Its Own Rulebook

- Dallas city leaders are drafting new rules governing where and how data centers can be sited, the Dallas Morning News reports, as projects push closer to the urban core — including a planned facility at the current site of the Dallas Market Center and another at the former Dallas Morning News building downtown.
- The city's own figures underline the stakes: Dallas currently tracks 23 data centers with a combined property value of nearly $1.5 billion, which generated more than $10 million in property tax revenue last year.
- Dallas's move follows a North Central Texas Council of Governments survey of member cities and counties in which 96% flagged strain on water resources and 81% flagged strain on the power grid as data-center concerns — with the group's environment and development director noting local governments are "having to play catch-up" once a site purchase is already final.
- No draft ordinance has been made public yet; council discussion remains at an early stage.

**Today's Signal:** Dallas is borrowing the Fort Worth-and-Denton playbook — write real rules instead of a moratorium — but leading with its own tax numbers first, a sign the city wants to regulate the industry's footprint without talking down its revenue.

### 4. 🧭 Two More Fort Worth-Area Projects Hit Real Roadblocks

- Black Mountain's 450-acre, $10 billion data-center campus in southeast Fort Worth remains stalled: the City Council tabled a vote in February on rezoning an additional 80 acres from agricultural to industrial use, and at Black Mountain's own request, that vote is now pushed to a December council meeting, according to a joint KERA News/Fort Worth Report/Denton Record-Chronicle investigation published this week.
- In southwest Fort Worth, Edged Data Centers has rescinded its application for a 50% property-tax abatement on its $1 billion, 186-acre Veale Ranch project, after council members stalled a vote on the exemption back in February.
- Denton's City Council has moved to pause new data-center development for 90 to potentially 180 days, with a first public hearing expected this month; any resulting rules would take effect in early December if approved. Denton's two existing projects, run by Core Scientific and QumulusAI, have together paid more than $3.1 million in combined property taxes and $475,000 in sales taxes to date.
- Both stalled Fort Worth projects predate the city's new Data Center & Infrastructure Commission, meaning the panel that begins its work today inherits two live rezoning fights already in progress.

**Today's Signal:** A tabled rezoning, a withdrawn tax break and a development pause are three different tools three different governments reached for this year — exactly the inconsistency Fort Worth's new commission, and eventually some statewide standard, would have to reconcile.

Quiet today on new TCEQ air-permit filings in the eight tracked counties and on behind-the-meter generation announcements, and no freshly filed Texas noise or nuisance litigation beyond the Westlake and Prosper fights this briefing has already tracked.
