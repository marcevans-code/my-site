# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through September 2, 2026)*

**Top Story:** More than half of Texas's known data centers ignored state water-use inquiries entirely, a Texas Senate committee heard Tuesday — with officials warning some companies could face criminal investigation and lawmakers previewing a slate of new restrictions for next session.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ Texas Data Centers Face Possible Criminal Charges Over Water-Use Silence, Senate Told

- The Texas Water Development Board contacted 324 known data center facilities about their water use; only 97 responded — fewer than 30% — water supply planning director Temple McKinnon told a Texas Senate committee Tuesday. Companies that never respond could face criminal charges, with enforcement left to local district attorneys and law enforcement; McKinnon said it was unclear whether any investigations were currently underway.
- Public Utility Commission Director Connie Corona told the committee that fewer than half of the data centers her agency separately contacted responded either, and acknowledged the state doesn't maintain a complete inventory of Texas data centers — meaning an unknown number of operators may never have been contacted at all.
- Rick Thompson, program director for the County Judges and Commissioners Association of Texas, told the hearing "there's not a bigger issue going on right now in Texas," adding that much of the information driving local debates "is misleading, and in many cases not true."
- Lawmakers previewed a slate of legislation for the 2027 session: repealing the sales-tax exemption that has saved data centers billions of dollars, new water-use limits, and noise restrictions. State Sen. Adam Hinojosa, R-Corpus Christi, extracted tentative pledges from officials representing Google, Lancium, and Blackstone subsidiary QTS Data Centers to support some of the new water-use restrictions.
- University of Texas at Austin researchers testified that roughly 60% of a data center's true water footprint actually occurs at the power plants generating its electricity, not on-site — and that data centers could account for about 5% of the state's total water use by 2030.

**Today's Signal:** The transparency gap cuts both ways — regulators can't credibly size the crisis, and the industry can't credibly rebut it, without the response rate neither side actually has, which is exactly the vacuum Thompson says is filling with claims that are "misleading, and in many cases not true."

### 2. 🗳️ DFW's Data-Center Fights Collide on the Same Night: Prosper Commission Rejects Project Tomahawk, Dallas Pushes Ahead on New Rules

- Prosper's Planning & Zoning Commission voted 7-0 Tuesday night to recommend denying the rezoning for White Rose Partners' $580 million Project Tomahawk data center — reversing town staff's own recommendation to approve it, which this briefing flagged in yesterday's edition. The commission's vote is only a recommendation; the rezoning now heads to the Prosper Town Council for a final vote.
- The 37.3-acre site sits along FM 1385 near Parvin Road, on land inside Prosper's town limits but adjacent to the neighboring city of Aubrey — which has said it has no legal jurisdiction over the project despite its own residents raising some of the loudest objections to noise, lighting, and traffic.
- The same day, five Dallas City Council members pushed ahead with their effort to write data-center-specific land-use standards into the city's development code, a process they opened in mid-August over resident concerns about water, power, noise, and land use as the industry reaches Dallas's own city limits, according to WFAA, KERA News, and The Dallas Morning News.

**Today's Signal:** A staff recommendation didn't survive contact with an actual commission vote — proof that yesterday's "staff recommends approval" framing was never the last word, and Prosper's elected Town Council still has the final say either way.

### 3. 🔧 ERCOT Misses Its Own Batch Zero Deadline — for the Second Time

- ERCOT failed to meet its own (already-extended) deadline to notify transmission and distribution providers of large-load customers' conditional Batch Zero classification status — missing the Aug. 31 date this briefing flagged yesterday as the one to watch, according to RTO Insider and Yahoo Finance reporting.
- It's the second missed deadline for these classification notices; the Public Utility Commission of Texas had already granted ERCOT a "good cause" exception in an Aug. 20 order that pushed the original schedule back once before.
- ERCOT has not announced a new target date for issuing the conditional classifications, leaving large-load projects statewide — including Batch Zero-eligible loads in DFW-area counties — without word on where they stand in the queue that's meant to separate financed, real projects from speculative ones.

**Today's Signal:** A second missed deadline on the process built to sort real Texas megaprojects from speculative ones raises the obvious question of whether the audit itself has become the bottleneck now, not the projects it's supposed to be screening.

### 4. 🔥 SpaceX Is Building Its Own Gas-Turbine Foundry in Bastrop to Speed Up Texas AI Power Plants

- SpaceX is bringing casting of natural-gas turbine blades and vanes in-house at a new foundry in Bastrop, Texas, aiming to cut up to 18 months off the timeline for getting gas turbines online to power AI data centers, Elon Musk said this week, as reported by the Wall Street Journal, TechCrunch, and Simply Wall St.
- The foundry draws on manufacturing techniques from SpaceX's Raptor rocket-engine program and is meant to relieve a supply-chain bottleneck that has become one of the biggest constraints on how fast gas-fired, behind-the-meter power can be built for AI campuses statewide.
- The move comes as SpaceX reported roughly $18.4 billion in capital expenditures in the second quarter of 2026 alone, with about $15.8 billion of that directed to AI compute infrastructure.

**Today's Signal:** Turbine lead times, not permitting or land, have been the quiet chokepoint behind nearly every "gas plant next to the data center" plan this briefing has tracked — an in-house foundry is a bet that owning that bottleneck outright is worth more than any single site.

### 5. 💧 Google Cuts Vernon a $4.5M Check for Water Pipes — the First Payout From Its Texas Water Fund

- Google announced the first grant recipient under the $10 million Texas community water-infrastructure commitment it launched in June: $4.5 million to the City of Vernon to replace a water pipeline more than 50 years old, tied to Google's data center water use in the area.
- Vernon Mayor Pam Gosline called the investment "more than an investment in our city," saying it will help the town "attract new businesses, support local industry, and create a stronger foundation for generations to come."
- The announcement lands the same week the Texas Senate hearing above found most data-center operators statewide haven't even responded to state water-use inquiries — leaving Google's itemized, publicized water spending as something of an outlier in the industry's response so far.

**Today's Signal:** A single well-publicized water grant is easy goodwill against a backdrop where regulators can't get basic response rates out of the rest of the industry — expect more operators to point to gestures like this one now that Tuesday's hearing put the transparency gap on the public record.

TCEQ's air-permit dockets for the eight tracked DFW-area counties stayed quiet today, and no new noise or nuisance litigation turned up in today's search either.
