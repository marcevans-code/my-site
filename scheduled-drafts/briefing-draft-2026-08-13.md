⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use
Wednesday, August 13, 2026

## 1. ⚡ ERCOT & Grid Signals — Freeze Widens, Politics Escalate

- Natural Gas Intelligence reported Aug. 12 that Texas's pause on new data center grid connections now affects roughly 474 GW of pending interconnection requests statewide — more than five times ERCOT's all-time peak demand — as the audit Gov. Abbott ordered Aug. 3 continues.
- The political fight over that pause kept escalating this week: President Trump called Abbott's moratorium "a mistake" in comments that aired Aug. 10-11, and Abbott has been publicly defending the pause since, with Fox 4 Dallas-Fort Worth and CBS Austin both carrying dueling statements from the two.
- ERCOT's Aug. 3 market notice (M-A080326-01) confirmed it suspended the Batch Zero Large Load classification notices originally due Aug. 7 and said it will report back to the Public Utility Commission's Aug. 20 open meeting.

**Today's Signal:** A paperwork pause that started Aug. 3 has become the biggest flashpoint in Texas energy politics, with the White House now publicly needling the governor over it.

## 2. 🏗️ Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity

- Amazon has bought roughly 8,400 acres in Pecos County and is actively clearing land for a data center campus near Fort Stockton; three separate construction permits for the buildings were filed in Texas in early August, per Yahoo Finance and the San Antonio Current.
- USA Today and Houston Public Media reported Aug. 10-11 that eight major tech companies — including Meta, OpenAI, Google and Amazon — have now agreed to Gov. Abbott's new data center energy and water standards; Meta said in its statement it already pays the full cost of its facilities' energy and water use.

**Today's Signal:** Amazon's Pecos County build is now the clearest example of the land rush and the corporate-compliance push happening at the same time, in the same county.

## 3. 🏭 Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)

- TCEQ's pending-permits list shows a new source review air permit application from Westline TX Holdings for a Beacon Data Centers project near San Angelo (Tom Green County); a local official publicly pressed for clarity on the filing this week, per ConchoValleyHomepage.
- A KSAT investigation published Aug. 11 detailed how data center operators are increasingly seeking TCEQ "standard permits" for their gas-fired backup and primary power plants — a permit category that skips the public-comment step required for larger air permits.
- Today's search turned up no new permit filings specifically in the eight DFW-area counties this briefing tracks (Collin, Ellis, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, Hood); the dated TCEQ activity found today centers on West Texas, near San Angelo.

**Today's Signal:** TCEQ's own permit categories are becoming part of the story — "standard permits" let gas plants serving data centers skip the public input that larger air permits require.

## 4. 🗺️ Land-Use & Zoning Developments — Fort Worth Moratorium Advances, Keller vs. Westlake Erupts

- Fort Worth City Council voted unanimously Aug. 11, formalized Aug. 12, to begin the process for a 90-day moratorium on new data center construction; the earliest the pause could take effect is Feb. 16, 2027, per Community Impact.
- Fort Worth also stood up an 11-member Data Center & Infrastructure Commission to evaluate future developments, with its first report due at a Nov. 3 work session, per KERA News.
- Just north of Fort Worth, Keller's city council called an emergency meeting Aug. 12 that ran past midnight into today. Residents packed the room to oppose a roughly 88-acre data center that Westlake's Planning and Zoning Commission had already approved near the Marshall Ridge neighborhood. The council voted unanimously to retain outside legal counsel and sent Westlake a resolution asking it to pause the project, with Mayor Ross McMullin telling the crowd he'll pursue "every legal option available."

**Today's Signal:** The Keller-Westlake dispute shows the zoning fight moving past single-city moratoriums into neighboring cities threatening legal action over projects just across their shared border.

## 5. 🔥 Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation

- The New York Times and Ars Technica reported this week that Amazon is backing a large natural-gas plant — reported at up to 7.65 GW across 35 turbines — to power its Pecos County AI campus, with critics warning it could become one of the largest single sources of power-plant pollution in the country.
- Forbes cited a Cleanview analysis (July 2026) finding roughly 90 GW of behind-the-meter gas generation capacity for U.S. data centers now in some stage of development, with xAI currently accounting for most of the roughly 2 GW already running.

**Today's Signal:** Amazon's Pecos County gas plant is shaping up as the test case for how far "power-first," build-your-own-generation strategies can go before drawing national environmental scrutiny.
