## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**PUCT Affirms Its Curtailment Authority Over Co-Located Data Centers**

- The Public Utility Commission of Texas has affirmed it retains curtailment authority over data centers that are co-located with generation resources (i.e., self-supplying power on the same site rather than drawing solely from the grid), closing off a potential argument that co-located load could sidestep grid-reliability directives (White & Case, Aug. 5).
- The ruling lands squarely inside the freeze Gov. Greg Abbott ordered Monday, Aug. 3, reinforcing that Texas regulators intend to treat co-located and self-generating projects as still subject to state oversight even though Abbott's audit technically targets only ERCOT-interconnected projects.
- ERCOT's Batch Zero transmission planning study — paused since Monday under PUCT Docket 59142 — remains on hold; no new order narrowing or expanding the audit's scope to the roughly 1,800-project, 474-gigawatt interconnection queue posted today.
- No update on whether the PUCT has ruled on ERCOT's expected "good cause" request to push back the missed Aug. 7 Batch Zero notification deadline.

> **Today's Signal:** Regulators are using this week's pause to lock down authority over every version of a data center's grid relationship — connected, co-located, or hybrid — not just the ones sitting in the interconnection queue.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Four New Texas Projects Surface in a Single Industry Roundup**

- Galaxy Digital acquired 500 acres in McGregor, Texas (McLennan County, about 90 miles north of Austin) for "Project Merlin," an AI and high-performance-computing campus expected to reach 74 MW in its first phase (Data Center Knowledge, Aug. 4).
- CyrusOne is reportedly pursuing a 460,000-square-foot, $500 million data center in Medina County, Texas (Data Center Knowledge, Aug. 4).
- Amazon's Q2 2026 earnings disclosures came amid reports the company is planning "Project Eagle," a four-building data center campus in Wharton County, Texas (Data Center Knowledge, Aug. 4).
- NRG Energy disclosed new details on a $3.2 billion, 1.2-gigawatt Texas data center power project during its Aug. 4 Q2 earnings call, describing it as a partnership with a "leading" data center operator (The Business Journals, Aug. 5).

> **Today's Signal:** Even with the state's interconnection queue frozen, project announcements haven't slowed — developers are still locking down land and power deals, betting the audit resolves in their favor.

## 🌿 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**No New DFW-County Filing Today, but National Reporting Flags the Same Loophole**

- No new Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood County TCEQ draft-permit or public-notice filing tied to a data center or gas plant was confirmed as newly dated today.
- WIRED's air-permit document review, published this morning, found that Chevron and Williams are both expanding gas-fired power plant and pipeline capacity specifically to serve AI data centers — Williams alone is building six behind-the-meter gas plants nationally, four of them serving Meta data centers (WIRED, Aug. 6).
- That reporting reinforces the Texas Tribune's July 9 finding that at least 15 Texas data centers are pursuing their own behind-the-meter gas plants, part of a broader 32-plant pipeline researchers project could emit roughly 287 million tons of greenhouse gases annually.
- No confirmation yet on whether TCEQ will be formally folded into the PUCT/ERCOT audit process Abbott ordered Monday, or how the long-underused PUCT/Texas Water Development Board water-use survey will factor into permit review going forward.

> **Today's Signal:** TCEQ's own filings are quiet, but national energy reporting keeps confirming the same pattern local regulators have been slow to formalize — gas-fired capacity going up behind the meter, largely outside standard permitting scrutiny.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Keller-Westlake Border Dispute Escalates After Tuesday's Approval**

- Two days after Westlake's Planning & Zoning Commission approved an 87.9-acre, 1.1-million-square-foot data center along Highway 377, the fight over the project has shifted to its literal border: Keller residents whose homes sit as close as 506 feet from the site are pressing their own city to intervene, even though the project sits entirely within Westlake's jurisdiction (CandysDirt.com, Aug. 6; Dallas Morning News, Aug. 5).
- The approved site plan — which added conditions on sound walls, landscaping, and cooling equipment — next heads to the Westlake Town Council for a final vote; no date has been confirmed.
- Fort Worth remains paused: city staff pulled a planned Aug. 4 council presentation on data-center zoning regulations and a possible moratorium to first determine how Abbott's statewide audit order affects the city's Aug. 11 council vote (Fort Worth Report, Aug. 4).
- No new DFW-area county zoning filing (Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood) was confirmed as newly dated today beyond the ongoing Westlake/Keller dispute.

> **Today's Signal:** Winning a zoning vote isn't the end of the fight — Westlake's approval has just relocated the argument to the neighboring city that has no legal say over the project but is left living next to it.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**Chevron and Williams Emerge as the Fossil-Fuel Winners of the AI Buildout**

- WIRED reports that Chevron and Williams are both positioning as major beneficiaries of the AI data center boom, building out gas-fired power plants and pipeline capacity dedicated to serving new campuses rather than waiting on grid interconnection (WIRED, Aug. 6).
- Williams is building six behind-the-meter gas plants for data centers across the country, including four projects serving Meta facilities — a direct, named example of the "power-first" model this briefing has tracked all week.
- A companion piece from Natural Gas Intelligence, published this morning, describes Williams' "speed-to-market" natural gas power model as designed specifically to outrun ERCOT-style interconnection delays like the one Texas is currently living through (Natural Gas Intelligence, Aug. 6).
- These moves arrive the same week the PUCT reaffirmed curtailment authority over co-located generation (Section 1) — a reminder that going behind the meter reduces interconnection wait times but doesn't remove a project from state regulatory reach entirely.

> **Today's Signal:** With ERCOT's queue still frozen, today's reporting shows the fossil-fuel companies building the power plants, not just the data center operators buying them, are now treating Texas-style grid delays as a business opportunity.
