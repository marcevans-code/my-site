# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through September 17, 2026)*

**Top Story:** Cipher Digital's stock jumped as much as 15% after ERCOT gave two of its Texas data-center projects a conditional "Batch Zero" classification — the first time this week's grid-review process has moved a company's share price — while Cipher, alongside Anthropic and FluidStack, simultaneously pledged $10 million to rebuild the water system in Colorado City, the small West Texas town that hosts one of its data centers.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚡ Cipher's Stock Jumps After an ERCOT Batch Zero Classification — With a $10M Water Pledge Attached

- Cipher Digital (CIFR) shares rose more than 14% Wednesday after the company said ERCOT had given conditional classifications to two of its Texas data-center projects under Batch Zero, the interconnection-review process this briefing covered Monday when ERCOT finished sorting 362 projects (191.8 GW) into base-load and conditional-study tiers. Cipher first disclosed the 3.2 GW figure last week but only released the site-by-site breakdown this week.
- Of that 3.2 GW, 1.1 GW — 100 MW at its Stingray site and 1 GW at Colchis — was classified as base load, the more secure of the two tiers. The remaining 2.1 GW (Mikeska, Apollo, a second phase at Stingray, and McLennan) landed in the less-certain "conditional study" tier. Separately, 807 MW across Cipher's Barber Lake, Black Pearl, and Odessa sites are already operational, and its Reveille and Ulysses sites have been cleared to connect. ERCOT's final designations, following Gov. Abbott's audit, are due in December.
- CIFR had fallen roughly 44% from its July peak amid uncertainty over the audit before Wednesday's jump, illustrating how directly the state's project-by-project review is now moving capital markets, not just permitting timelines.
- In a related move, Cipher, FluidStack, and Anthropic — both tenants at Cipher's Barber Lake data center — pledged $10 million this week to a newly formed Colorado City Water Infrastructure Foundation, funding water-system repairs the town identified after shortages this summer.

**Today's Signal:** This is the first time this briefing has seen a Batch Zero classification move a stock price in real time, and the timing of the water-infrastructure pledge — from the same company, the same week — reads as a data-center operator getting ahead of the water-reporting crackdown this briefing covered Monday, rather than waiting to be named in it.

### 2. 🏗️ $1.2 Billion in New DFW Data Center Projects Surface in Just Weeks

- Four separate filings totaling roughly $1.2 billion have surfaced across the Dallas-Fort Worth area in recent weeks, The Dallas Morning News reported. DataBank filed for a $425 million addition (DFW15) to its 300-acre campus in Red Oak, Ellis County — part of an eight-building buildout — following a $325 million building filed just last month, backed by $1.45 billion in financing closed in June.
- Digital Realty registered a $300 million, 672,000-square-foot expansion in Garland (10 data halls at 8 megawatts each) on Sept. 8, building on a 2024 incentive deal and a Mitsubishi Corp. joint venture. Edged registered a second $300 million, roughly 486,000-square-foot Fort Worth building — "Project Outpost" — in Tarrant County via a Sept. 14 filing, its second such building this year. CoreSite registered a $200 million, 230,000-square-foot Irving data center on Aug. 31, following February's city council approval.
- Cushman & Wakefield's mid-year report puts DFW as Texas's largest data-center market and the fifth-most-active in the Americas, with under-construction capacity climbing from 1.2 gigawatts in late 2025 to 1.7 gigawatts by mid-2026 — with growth increasingly concentrated south of Dallas, in Lancaster, Red Oak, and Midlothian. The report ties that growth directly to Gov. Abbott's self-funded-infrastructure policy and ERCOT's Batch Zero process, noting investment "remains strong" despite "localized opposition and legal challenges."

**Today's Signal:** Every one of these filings lands in a county or city this briefing has already tracked for pushback — Ellis, Tarrant, Garland's own incentive fights — and the pace shows developers moving ahead of, not waiting for, the audit and zoning fights still working through courts and commissioners courts.

### 3. 🏚️ A 940-Square-Foot Loophole: How an Unannounced Data Center Landed in Southeast Dallas

- A small, 940-square-foot data center appeared near the Linfield Motel in Dallas's Joppa neighborhood with no advance notice to residents, The Dallas Morning News reported — because the facility was "permitted by right" as a "communications exchange facility," a classification that doesn't require alerting neighbors or holding a public meeting. A sign identifies it as the "Danik Data Center," and a related company website shows it offering Bitcoin-mining services.
- Joppa residents, including longtime resident Yvette Evans, said they only learned of the facility after it was built; the site's owner did not respond to requests for comment. Dallas is separately considering land-use changes around Joppa that could limit new industrial facilities, but city officials say that process could take years.
- The city is now weighing broader changes: nearly two dozen data centers are already tracked in Dallas, with a combined property value near $1.5 billion and more than $10 million generated in property taxes last year. Council members have said existing rules "fall short of a more modern local regulatory landscape," and the city is considering an advisory task force to study the issue before recommending new regulations.

**Today's Signal:** This is the same zoning-power gap this briefing has tracked in Hood, Ellis, and Collin counties, but playing out inside city limits instead of unincorporated land — proof that a lack of data-center-specific rules can let a project through unnoticed even where a city does have zoning authority.

### 4. 🔧 Granbury Council Turns Down Stricter Data Center Rules

- The Granbury City Council voted against a proposed set of stricter data-center rules, Hood County News reported, with the outlet's headline framing summarizing that "officials question blanket setbacks" while "neighbors still seek stronger protections." The fuller article was not accessible today beyond that headline-level detail.

**Today's Signal:** Coming right after this week's Comanche Circle and Project Patriot fights in the same county, this is the clearest sign yet that Hood County's elected city officials and its data-center-wary residents remain a step apart on how far new rules should go.

Quiet today on data-center-specific developments in Collin, Johnson, Denton, and Wise counties beyond what's already on the record.
