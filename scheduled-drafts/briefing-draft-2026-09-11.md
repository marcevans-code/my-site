# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through September 11, 2026)*

**Top Story:** Texas utility regulators vote today on what data centers must pay and disclose to plug into the grid — the sharpest teeth yet in Gov. Abbott's data-center audit — the same week Amazon defended its $10 billion Boling campus to skeptical neighbors.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ Texas Regulators Set to Vote Today on What Data Centers Must Pay to Plug In

- The Public Utility Commission of Texas meets today, Sept. 11, at 9:30 a.m. in Austin to vote on a rule requiring any customer seeking 75 megawatts or more from the grid — the threshold set by the Legislature in 2025 — to prove it controls the land, disclose permits and construction schedules, and post security before ERCOT will study the request, according to The Texas Dispatch.
- Under the rule as proposed, a customer would pay a study fee starting at $100,000, then $50,000 per megawatt, non-refundable, when it signs the interconnection agreement, and cover the full cost of the wires to its own property line — cost utilities could no longer pass on to other ratepayers. Commissioners cut the per-megawatt figure in half back in March; a 282-page staff adoption order filed Sept. 3 may or may not have kept that lower number, and the Dispatch said it could not confirm which version is up for a vote.
- The rule's other half targets "phantom demand": developers who shop the same project to multiple utilities, inflating the queue. It would require disclosure of substantially similar requests filed elsewhere and bar companies from claiming that information is confidential business information.
- The same meeting includes votes on three contested 765-kilovolt transmission lines. State administrative law judges recommended rejecting both a 223-mile, $1.68 billion Bell County-to-Big Hill line and a Big Hill-to-Sand Lake line proposed by Oncor and the Lower Colorado River Authority, finding neither had been proven necessary and that notice to roughly 1,400 landowners was defective; commission staff disagreed on the Bell County line and urged approval of a route. A third line proposed by AEP Texas and CPS Energy across 14 counties drew a staff recommendation to approve, but commissioners reopened the record after an Aug. 28 hearing instead of voting.

**Today's Signal:** This is the first time the audit Abbott ordered Aug. 3 turns into an actual price tag and paperwork requirement rather than just a pause — whether the $50,000-per-megawatt figure survives today's vote at full strength or at the March-cut half rate will say a lot about how much friction regulators actually want to put between developers and the grid.

### 2. 📦 Amazon Defends Its $10 Billion Boling Campus to Skeptical Neighbors

- Amazon executives met with residents of Boling, in Wharton County, Thursday night to answer questions about the company's data-center campus under construction on nearly 3,000 acres off FM 442 — a project sources told KPRC 2/Click2Houston is worth $10 billion and will include 13 buildings supporting AWS cloud operations for customers ranging from hospitals to first responders.
- Keith Klein, Amazon's director of supply solutions for the Americas, said the project will bring 300 full-time jobs, with 150 filled by the end of 2027, at wages above the county average.
- On the two questions residents pressed hardest — water and power — Klein said the site uses a closed-loop, air-cooled system with no water used for server cooling, putting total water use at about 16,000 gallons a day, "equivalent to about 54 residential homes." Amazon said the facility will draw power through its own dedicated ERCOT transmission connection, with CenterPoint Energy as the local provider, and that it would not affect other customers' bills or usage.
- Some residents raised concerns about construction noise, dust, and flooding; others, including a local realtor, said the project would raise property values and help fund schools and county services.

**Today's Signal:** This is the same Project Eagle expansion this briefing flagged Sept. 9 in an industry filings roundup — now with Amazon's own numbers on the table, the water and power specifics are exactly what Hood County and Prosper residents have been pressing their own developers to commit to in writing.

Quiet today on TCEQ air-permit activity in the eight tracked DFW-area counties, and no new zoning or noise-complaint filings surfaced beyond what's already on the record.
