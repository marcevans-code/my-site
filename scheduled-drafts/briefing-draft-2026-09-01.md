## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 💰 Anthropic's $35B Lambda Deal Puts a Bitcoin Miner's South Texas Campus in the Spotlight

- Anthropic has signed a cloud-capacity agreement worth $35 billion with Lambda, a Nvidia-backed neocloud, covering about 350 MW of capacity at a data center under development in Nueces County, Texas, according to the Wall Street Journal and Reuters. Nvidia will actually hold the lease on the data center itself, per the Journal.
- The site is Hut 8's "Beacon Point" campus — 525 acres near Corpus Christi targeting initial energization in Q1 2027. Hut 8, a crypto-mining company turned HPC/neocloud developer, is targeting a full 1 GW of capacity there.
- Separately, Hut 8 said in July it had doubled a lease with an unnamed hyperscale customer at the same site, adding 352 MW and bringing that customer's total to 704 MW — a reminder that a good chunk of Beacon Point's capacity was already spoken for before today's Anthropic-linked headline.
- The deal lands days after Anthropic was reported to have signed a separate $45 billion capacity agreement with UK neocloud Nscale for a West Virginia campus, part of a compute-buying spree that also includes agreements with TeraWulf, Riot Platforms and Fluidstack.

**Today's Signal:** Nueces County isn't one of the eight DFW-area counties this briefing tracks, but it's the same pattern playing out statewide — the company actually pouring concrete and running transmission lines is a former crypto miner (Hut 8), not the AI lab or the chipmaker whose names make the headline.

### 2. 👻 Texas's "Ghost Demand" Freeze Becomes the National Test Case, as ERCOT's First Batch-Zero Filter Comes Due

- A Reuters review published this morning found electricity requests from large power users — mostly data centers — now exceed 700 GW across the Midwest, Mid-Atlantic and South, more than 10 times industry estimates of current U.S. data center power use. Texas alone has seen requests grow from about 48 GW in 2023 to more than 474 GW today, per ERCOT and Gov. Abbott's own figures, making it the fastest-growing AI/cloud hub in the world by some measures.
- "When you don't know what is real, you really don't know how to build the infrastructure for it," Texas PUC Chairman Thomas Gleeson told an industry conference, quoted in the Reuters piece — the reasoning behind Abbott's freeze and the accompanying ownership-and-usage audit.
- ERCOT's new Batch Zero process, born out of Senate Bill 6 and approved by the PUC in June, replaces one-off project reviews for loads of 75 MW and up with a coordinated systemwide study meant to separate mature, financed projects from speculative ones.
- Abbott's added verification directive pushed ERCOT past its original Aug. 7 classification deadline; the PUCT approved a revised schedule Aug. 20, and ERCOT said it intended to hand utilities conditional Batch Zero classifications by Aug. 31 — yesterday — kicking off a dispute-and-reconciliation process before anything becomes final.
- AEP Texas' own numbers illustrate the scale: 41 GW of SB 6-compliant contracted load through 2030, 88% of it data centers (including crypto customers), out of an active ERCOT pipeline of 78 data center requests totaling 52 GW.

**Today's Signal:** The 474 GW headline number and the Batch Zero classification process are answering two different questions — how much Texas wants to build versus how much it can actually prove is real — and yesterday's conditional-classification deadline is the first real test of whether that filter actually works.

### 3. 🌳 Prosper's Planning & Zoning Commission Takes Up Project Tomahawk Tonight — Staff Recommends Approval

- Prosper's Planning & Zoning Commission meets tonight at 7 p.m. at Town Hall to consider rezoning roughly 37 acres along Parvin Road near FM 1385 for White Rose Partners' Project Tomahawk data center, with town staff recommending approval, according to CBS Texas and WFAA. The facility would occupy about 20% of the lot, sited behind a substation and a planned commercial development.
- The staff agenda item proposes a "living screen" — an expanded tree line with canopy trees and evergreen shrubs — plus an 8-foot rear wall for sound-dampening near the future Prosper Oaks neighborhood, and claims the project would use less than half the water of a single-family home and place all electrical-upgrade costs on the developer.
- Neighboring Aubrey's mayor, Chris Rich, published a statement acknowledging the city has no jurisdiction over the Prosper site but asking Prosper to share impact studies, loop in Aubrey's public-safety staff, and keep the developer talking directly to nearby residents — citing resident concerns over noise, lighting, traffic and proximity to existing Aubrey neighborhoods.
- Notably, the developer's project narrative describes Tomahawk's purpose as internet connectivity, with nothing in the public agenda item tying it specifically to AI workloads.

**Today's Signal:** A staff recommendation isn't a done deal — the rezoning would still need to clear a Town Council vote after tonight — but Prosper spending two weeks reviewing engineering studies rather than rushing a vote, and Aubrey going public with a formal request instead of a legal threat, both suggest the process safeguards this briefing has flagged before are actually shaping how this one plays out.

### 4. 📊 New NCTCOG Survey: 78% of North Texas Cities and Counties Say They Aren't Ready for the Data Center Boom

- The North Central Texas Council of Governments surveyed 31 North Texas city and county leaders and staff; 78% said their community lacks the infrastructure to support new data center development without major upgrades, per KERA News.
- Environmental concerns dominated the responses: 96% flagged water resources as facing the greatest strain, and 81% cited the power grid.
- "A lot of the things that local governments identified in the survey are things that the developers could do to ensure positive outcomes," said Susan Alvarez, NCTCOG's director of environment and development, pointing to water use, energy reliability and sustainability measures.
- Respondents also said the region lacks reliable data to judge whether promised tax-revenue benefits are real, and are pushing for stronger guardrails — water reuse, renewable-energy requirements and efficiency standards — on new projects.

**Today's Signal:** This is the first systematic look at how the region's own city halls and county courthouses — not just residents or judges — rate their own readiness, and a 78% "not ready" number from local governments themselves is harder for the industry to wave away than a resident survey.

### 5. 🗳️ Data Centers Become a 2026 Governor's Race Flashpoint as Hinojosa Demands a Special Session

- Democratic gubernatorial nominee Gina Hinojosa used a Monday El Paso campaign stop to call for an emergency legislative session dedicated to data centers, saying she'd void any contract that "denies Texas residents the water they need to live," per El Paso Matters. She also criticized how Meta's disclosed costs for its El Paso campus grew from $800 million in 2023 to $14 billion today, and said she'd seek to reshape the Public Utility Commission, which has three seats turning over in 2027 regardless of who wins in November.
- Only Abbott can actually call a special session, and he hasn't signaled any intention to before the Legislature's next regular session convenes Jan. 12, 2027.
- The same week, President Trump posted that communities without data centers will "end up being backwards and poor," prompting Vice President Vance to clarify that "99% of the backlash" comes from areas where a project would raise local electricity prices.
- A Texas Politics Project/University of Texas at Austin poll of 1,200 registered voters, cited by CBS Texas, found 57% strongly or somewhat opposed to data center construction in their own community, against 30% in support.

**Today's Signal:** Hinojosa's PUC-appointment threat may matter more in practice than the special session she can't actually force — three commission seats turn over in 2027 no matter who wins in November, and that's the same body currently deciding El Paso Electric's request to build a dedicated 366 MW gas plant for Meta's campus.
