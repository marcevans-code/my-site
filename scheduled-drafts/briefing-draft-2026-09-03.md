# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use
**(Latest updates through September 3, 2026)**

**Top Story:** Texas's Republican leadership has flipped from AI cheerleader to data-center skeptic — Gov. Abbott and Attorney General Paxton are both pushing new restrictions on the industry that made the state its epicenter, with Abbott's grid-connection freeze conveniently expiring the day after November's election.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🐘 Texas Republicans Break With Trump, Turn Against Data Centers Ahead of November

- Reuters found Gov. Greg Abbott and Attorney General Ken Paxton — both facing tight 2026 races — have reversed course on data centers less than a year after Abbott called Texas the "epicenter of AI development." Abbott is now pushing new restrictions, a repeal of data centers' sales-tax exemption, and limits on rural development; his Aug. 3 audit has already frozen new grid-connection approvals, a pause Democratic gubernatorial rival Gina Hinojosa points out "ends November 4, the day after the election."
- Paxton, who voted for the original 2013 tax incentive as a state senator, rolled out his own anti-data-center plan last month. His Senate opponent, James Talarico, called the pivot "a sad attempt to reverse course" bought by tech-industry contributions; Paxton's campaign fired back that Talarico voted for a 2023 sales-tax incentive of his own.
- The shift puts Abbott and Paxton at odds with President Trump, who warned in a Monday Truth Social post that communities fighting data centers risk ending up "backwards and poor" — a split the RNC downplayed, saying candidates will "do what's best for them and their communities."
- Big Tech is scrambling to catch up: super PACs tied to Anthropic have spent more than $2.6 million in Texas, PACs linked to OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman more than $4.8 million, and Meta about $1.4 million to a state-focused PAC — against an August Texas Politics Project poll that found only 30% of respondents would support a data center in their own community.
- Reuters counts Republican candidates in at least five states, including Michigan, Wisconsin and Pennsylvania, hardening their rhetoric on data centers, but says the shift is "most striking" in Texas.

**Today's Signal:** The Nov. 4 timing on Abbott's pause is the detail that turns this from an energy story into a campaign story — every regulator, hearing and zoning vote this briefing has tracked since Aug. 3 is now running on a clock that expires the day the polls close.

### 2. 🔌 ERCOT's Data-Center Audit Has a Deadline Now: December 10

- ERCOT plans to complete its audit of pending data center interconnection requests by Dec. 10, University of Houston Gutierrez Energy Management Institute executive director Greg Bean told Axios — the clearest public timeline yet for when Abbott's Aug. 3 grid-connection freeze might actually lift.
- A new UH report projects U.S. electricity use could grow 32% by 2030, with data centers driving roughly 55% of the nation's peak-demand growth over the next five years, and says Texas — especially West Texas, where annual demand growth is projected above 7% through 2035 — is "at the leading edge" of that curve.
- Bean noted ERCOT's grid runs at only about half capacity on average, since it's built to handle peak demand — real headroom exists, he said, if regulators can ever get a reliable count of which of the hundreds of pending data center proposals will actually get built.
- The Dec. 10 date sits on top of, not instead of, ERCOT's separate Batch Zero classification process, which this briefing has already tracked missing its own deadline twice.

**Today's Signal:** A hard December date gives Abbott's freeze something it's lacked since Aug. 3 — an actual expiration mechanism for the audit itself, distinct from the Nov. 4 political deadline on the pause.

### 3. 🏛️ Fort Worth Names the 11 Members Who'll Write Its Data-Center Rulebook

- Fort Worth and TCU are set to publicly announce the 11 members of the city's new Data Center & Infrastructure Commission Thursday, TCU environmental-studies director Michael Slattery told the Fort Worth Report — the advisory body created Aug. 11, the same council meeting that opened the process toward a data-center moratorium.
- Confirmed members include Sierra Club Greater Fort Worth conservation chair John MacFarlane; Krista Erbe of the 2871 Community Coalition, formed to oppose Edged Data Center's project in southwest Fort Worth; and Ambika Sharma, a Tarrant Appraisal District board candidate who has repeatedly raised data-center concerns at city meetings.
- The commission's charge spans electricity and water impact and economic development, plus — per the Fort Worth Report — setbacks, noise, lighting and other standards meant to keep data centers out of historically underserved or industrialized neighborhoods. Recommendations are due to City Council by the Nov. 3 work session.
- Seats are also reserved for the Tarrant Regional Water District, a local electricity provider, and an environmental advocacy group, alongside industry and economic-development representatives.

**Today's Signal:** Naming an outspoken Sierra Club organizer and two residents from an active anti-data-center coalition to the panel that writes the rules is Fort Worth choosing to put its critics inside the room rather than just taking their public comment.

### 4. ⛏️ A Bitcoin Miner Just Paid $100 Million to Clear Its Last Land Hurdle in Milam County

- Bitdeer, the Singapore-based crypto miner turned AI/HPC developer, paid roughly $100 million in cash this week for about 200 acres of greenfield land adjacent to its existing Rockdale, Texas facility in Milam County, according to the company and Yahoo Finance — a fee-simple purchase that KBW analysts said removes a primary land constraint on the site.
- The deal brings Bitdeer's Milam County footprint to roughly 255 acres and up to 742 MW of existing and pipeline power capacity, positioning the company to build dedicated AI and HPC data centers rather than just cryptocurrency mining at the site.
- KBW kept its "Market Perform" rating on Bitdeer (NASDAQ: BTDR) despite the deal, noting that power rights and water access — not land — remain the bigger open questions for the expansion.
- Milam County, about 70 miles from Austin, sits outside this briefing's core eight-county DFW tracking area, but the deal fits a pattern this briefing keeps finding statewide: the company actually pouring concrete is a former crypto miner, not a hyperscaler.

**Today's Signal:** Bitdeer clearing its land constraint with cash, the same week Texas politicians spend their time talking about slowing this industry down, is a reminder that the audit and the moratoriums target new interconnection approvals — not operators who already control their site and are just waiting on power.
