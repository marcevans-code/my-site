## 🔌 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals

**A Compliance Wave Breaks Out While Trump and Abbott Spar Over the Pause**

- President Trump publicly called Gov. Greg Abbott's Aug. 3 data-center interconnection pause "a mistake," saying communities that want data centers should be able to have them; Abbott has defended the audit as necessary to protect grid reliability, water supplies, and ratepayers (CBS Austin/KEYE, Aug. 9; Houston Public Media, Aug. 10; FOX, Aug. 8).
- In the days since, a string of major operators has publicly agreed to comply with Abbott's Aug. 3 standards — full-cost payment for grid infrastructure, water-efficient cooling, and usage disclosure — including Meta (Aug. 10-11), OpenAI (Office of the Texas Governor release, Aug. 10), Equinix (Aug. 11, citing more than $1.6 billion in Texas investment), Compass Datacenters (Aug. 10), QTS Data Centers (Aug. 6), and Montera Infrastructure (Aug. 7).
- ERCOT's request for a good-cause exception on Batch Zero classification timelines remains queued for the Public Utility Commission of Texas's Aug. 20 open meeting; no new large-load classification notices have posted since the Aug. 3 suspension (ERCOT Market Notice M-A080326-01).

**Today's Signal:** The Abbott standards are turning into a checklist operators race to publicly sign onto, even though the underlying interconnection queue they're supposedly cleared for stays frozen until Aug. 20 at the earliest.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity

**Amazon Confirms Its 8,000-Acre West Texas Mega-Campus**

- Amazon confirmed it has acquired the roughly 8,000-acre GW Ranch site in Pecos County from developer Pacifico Energy, following construction-permit filings earlier this month for three data-center buildings, with land-clearing already underway (Data Center Dynamics, Aug. 10; Yahoo Finance, Aug. 10).
- In a statement, Amazon said the campus is "powered by new on-site generation that won't raise electricity costs for Texas families" and is "designed to transition to grid-connected service as interconnection timelines allow" — pairing the announcement with the compliance language now expected under Abbott's standards.

**Today's Signal:** Amazon just closed the year's largest confirmed Texas data-center land deal in the same week the state paused new grid connections — a reminder that off-grid, self-generated campuses aren't waiting on ERCOT's timeline.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments

**Fort Worth Council Votes Tonight on a 90-Day Moratorium**

- Fort Worth's City Council meets tonight, Aug. 11 — its first session since summer break — to vote on a 90-day moratorium on new data-center development applications (extendable for another 90 days), creation of a standing data-center review committee, and new mandatory filing requirements covering water-efficient cooling and usage reporting (Fort Worth Report, WFAA, City of Fort Worth council agenda).
- Because the Zoning Commission voted 7-4 in July to recommend denial of the city's original data-center ordinance, tonight's measures need a three-fourths council supermajority — at least 9 of 11 votes — to pass.
- The vote follows Denton City Council's Aug. 5 4-3 decision to direct staff toward its own data-center moratorium and zoning rewrite, with public hearings tentatively set for Sept. 22 and Oct. 27 and final adoption targeted for Dec. 2 (KERA News, WFAA, Community Impact, Aug. 5).

**Today's Signal:** Two of DFW's largest cities are now moving toward outright pauses on new data-center development within the same two weeks, even as the state's own audit process is still working out what companies have to disclose.

## ⚡ 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation

**A 7.65-Gigawatt Gas Plant Anchors the Pecos County Campus**

- The GW Ranch project centers on a 35-turbine, 7.65 GW natural-gas plant developed by Pacifico Energy under a TCEQ air permit — behind-the-meter capacity that, according to permit filings, could authorize up to 33 million tons of annual greenhouse gas emissions, potentially making it one of the largest natural-gas plants and single CO2 sources in the US (Tom's Hardware, Ars Technica, Data Center Dynamics, Aug. 10).
- Amazon says the site will combine the gas plant with solar and battery storage, following a similar behind-the-meter playbook Microsoft has already announced nearby with a 2.67 GW gas plant backing its own 2 GW Pecos-area development.

**Today's Signal:** Behind-the-meter gas is now the default answer for hyperscalers who don't want their multi-gigawatt Texas campuses waiting in ERCOT's paused queue.
