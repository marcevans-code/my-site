# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 18, 2026

(Wednesday, November 18, 2026)

**Top Story:** Texas regulators restarted data center permitting more than three weeks before ERCOT's audit report is due, and the first permit out the door lets SpaceX burn gas in 35 turbines at Starbase.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🏭 Texas Restarts Data Center Permits Early, and SpaceX's 35-Turbine Starbase Plant Gets the First Air Permit

- Monday, Nov. 16 (reported Tuesday, Nov. 17): The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality issued an air permit for SpaceX's planned data center and gas-fired power plant on its 444-acre site in Starbase, near Brownsville. The permit covers 35 natural gas turbines producing 750 MW, for a plant that won't connect to the grid. TCEQ spokesperson Laura Lopez confirmed it was issued Monday (Politico, Ariel Wittenberg).
- The permit allows up to 248 tons a year of nitrogen oxides, just under the 250-ton line that would trigger tougher review, including a public hearing (Politico).
- TCEQ said it "has resumed its normal permit review process." The Texas Tribune reported that the agency got notice Friday from Gov. Greg Abbott's office that ERCOT had finished gathering information on data centers' community impacts. That ends the permitting pause Abbott ordered in a Sept. 21 letter (The Texas Tribune, Emily Foxhall; Politico).
- ERCOT spokesperson Trudi Webster said Monday night that ERCOT is still "working to complete" the audit and will report its findings Dec. 10. The PUC is set to discuss them Dec. 17. Abbott's office didn't respond to either outlet (Politico; The Texas Tribune).
- The permit came three days after FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen filed a complaint with TCEQ alleging SpaceX had begun building the plant without a permit. They cited satellite and aerial images showing cranes and turbine work between Oct. 9 and Nov. 12. TCEQ didn't answer questions about the complaint, and SpaceX didn't respond (Politico).
- Public Citizen called on TCEQ's commissioners to resign. FracTracker's Sam Law called for "a true moratorium on data center development" (The Texas Tribune).

**Today's Signal:** Abbott tied this pause to ERCOT finishing its audit, and ERCOT says it hasn't finished. The restart leaves TCEQ about two months of permitting before Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa, who promised to keep approvals paused, takes office. A 248-ton permit for a 750 MW off-grid plant also shows how a project can be designed to stay just under the threshold that triggers a public hearing.

### 2. 🚫 Tarrant County Votes to Stop Giving Tax Abatements to Data Centers

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: The Tarrant County Commissioners Court unanimously approved Commissioner Matt Krause's resolution directing county administrator Chandler Merritt to write abatement guidelines that disqualify data center developers (Fort Worth Report, Cecilia Lenzen).
- The exclusion covers data centers over 1,000 square feet, battery storage and power generation over 5 MW, and substations, cooling systems, transmission and other support facilities directly tied to data centers. Projects already under construction aren't affected (Fort Worth Report).
- Krause called it a "guardrail" and a signal to lawmakers before the Legislature convenes in January. He said it answers residents at a September town hall in his northeast Tarrant precinct who wanted local rules. No other commissioner spoke before the vote (Fort Worth Report).

**Today's Signal:** The vote came the same day the Texas Tribune counted at least $1 billion in local data center tax breaks, including at least $271 million Fort Worth expects to rebate Meta. Tarrant County can't undo the city's deal, but it has ruled out offering its own. The 5 MW cutoff for generation and storage matters too: on-site gas plants and batteries that serve data centers won't get county abatements either.

### 3. 🏗️ Red Oak Expands Compass's Campus to 886 Acres While the May Approval Is Still in Court

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: At a joint 7 p.m. meeting with the Planning and Zoning Commission, the Red Oak City Council voted 4-1 to approve a zoning request that grows Compass Datacenters' campus off FM 2377 to about 886 acres (WFAA; FOX 4).
- The city said the request adds parcels Compass owns to the project approved in May. A lawsuit challenging how the city handled that May rezoning is still pending, and the city declined to comment, citing the litigation (WFAA).
- According to the agenda packet, the new zoning includes landscaping, lighting and sound rules and a public park (WFAA).
- Opponents rallied at Watkins Park across from City Hall before the meeting. "Nobody's asking us, do we want them here?" said neighbor Heidi Yuhanna (WFAA). Compass said it has invested more than $100 million in Red Oak over six years (WFAA).

**Today's Signal:** The council approved more acreage while the original approval is still being challenged. If the court throws out May's rezoning, the city will have to decide what happens to the parcels it added on top of it. Either way, residents who say they were never asked now have a second vote to point to.

### 4. ❌ Cameron County Commissioners Refuse to Advance SpaceX's $24 Billion Tax Break Request

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Cameron County commissioners voted against moving SpaceX's tax break request for the Starbase data center and gas plant to a second meeting, where the company would have presented final numbers. Only County Judge Eddie Treviño Jr. and Precinct 2 Commissioner Joey Lopez voted to advance it (KRGV).
- SpaceX's application listed a $24 billion investment, a first-year taxable value of $13.8 billion and 500 new jobs (KRGV).
- "As of right now, there won't be a second meeting. So the [request] is dead," Lopez said (KRGV).

**Today's Signal:** SpaceX got its state air permit and lost the county's tax break on consecutive days. The city of Starbase, SpaceX's company town, still holds its own abatement hearing Dec. 9. The county's no doesn't stop the plant. It means Cameron County taxpayers won't be helping pay for it.

### 5. 🏘️ Georgetown's Planning Commission Unanimously Recommends Denying the Inner Loop Data Center

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: After more than 100 people spoke against it, the Georgetown Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended denying the rezoning of 28 acres at Northeast Inner Loop and East University Avenue for the single-building Inner Loop Exchange data center. Developers can still take it to City Council in December (KXAN).
- "I want to see people that are working for us and not for developers," said Riverhill HOA president Boz Sabeti. Churchill Farms HOA president Gus Avila cited light and chiller noise (KXAN).
- The developers said the plan has 100- to 150-foot setbacks, a 50-foot landscaped buffer with a six-foot berm, generators used only for outages and testing, and a required pre-construction sound study against the city's 72-decibel daytime and 65-decibel nighttime limits. They said Oncor's new substation next to the site is going ahead regardless (KXAN).
- In nearby Kyle, the City Council took up Councilmember Claudia Zapata's request to start the legal process of banning data centers in every zoning district, though no data center is proposed there. Any ban would need a separate formal vote (KVUE).

**Today's Signal:** The developers' case is that their zoning comes with more protections than the site's current General Commercial zoning. A unanimous no from the commission makes the council's Dec. 8 vote harder to win. Kyle is trying to rule out data centers before anyone applies, as San Marcos did in June, when lawyers were already predicting a legal challenge to that ban.

### 6. 💧 Giga Energy Signs a $1 Billion, 10-Year Lease for a 60 MW Hunt County Data Center

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Houston-based Giga Energy said it signed a 10-year lease worth about $1 billion with an unnamed U.S. AI cloud provider for Project Clay, a 60 MW data center on about 30 acres in Hunt County near Royse City (Royse City Herald-Banner, Kent Miller; Interesting Engineering).
- Construction began in July using factory-built modules, and service is expected next spring. The company projects about 300 construction jobs and 24 permanent jobs paying $116,000 to $180,000 (Royse City Herald-Banner; Interesting Engineering).
- Giga says the closed-loop cooling uses no municipal water or groundwater, and that only sinks and restrooms will use water, about as much as two homes a year. It says the site will use underused capacity on dedicated electrical infrastructure, not capacity that serves homes and businesses (Interesting Engineering; Royse City Herald-Banner).
- The company said local taxing entities will get "millions" a year in tax revenue but gave no estimate. No abatement was mentioned (Royse City Herald-Banner).

**Today's Signal:** At 60 MW, Project Clay is under ERCOT's 75 MW large-load threshold, so the energization pause doesn't apply to it. Giga's announcement also answers the usual objections before anyone raises them: no water, no strain on residential power, no tax break mentioned. Hunt County now has this project and Core Scientific's 430 MW Quinlan filing from Monday, and those two face very different paths to power.

### 7. 🤝 Lt. Gov.-elect Goodwin's Transition Team Includes a Lobbyist Paid by Vantage Data Centers

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin announced a 14-member transition team. Eight members are registered lobbyists, including co-chair Leticia Van de Putte, a former Democratic state senator (Washington Free Beacon, Collin Anderson).
- Van de Putte's lobbying clients include Vantage Data Centers, which is building the $25 billion Frontier campus in Shackelford County and hired her in January on a year-long contract worth up to $114,429.99, and Natura Resources, a Texas nuclear company (Washington Free Beacon).
- Team member Tommy Williams, a former Republican legislator and Abbott fiscal adviser, has an active lobbying contract with NextEra Energy. Executive director Angela Hale is also a registered lobbyist. Goodwin, Van de Putte and Williams didn't respond to requests for comment (Washington Free Beacon).
- Goodwin campaigned for a "pause on new data center construction" and called for a special session on what she called "the data center crisis." Texas ethics rules don't bar lobbyists from transition teams (Washington Free Beacon).

**Today's Signal:** As lieutenant governor, Goodwin will appoint Senate committee chairs and refer bills to committee, so who advises her matters more than who advises most transition teams. A transition team doesn't write bills, but it helps choose the staff who will. Watch whether Van de Putte or Williams lands in her office, which would require them to stop lobbying.

### 8. 📉 Analysts Put Texas at the Center of 191 GW of Data Center Capacity Now at Risk

- Tuesday, Nov. 17: Utility Dive reported an Orennia estimate that 191 GW of planned U.S. data center capacity faces new risk after the midterms, concentrated in Texas, Ohio and Georgia. Orennia said Texas accounts for nearly half of U.S. pre-construction development (Utility Dive, Marlene Wilden).
- In an Orennia scenario where 20% of projected ERCOT data center demand doesn't materialize, average wholesale revenue for solar and wind falls 38% and 37%, and two-hour battery arbitrage spreads could fall below what new projects need to break even (Utility Dive).
- Andrejka Bernatova of Dynamix Capital Partners said investors may favor markets where power is certain, which could cost Texas its advantage (Utility Dive).

**Today's Signal:** The risk isn't limited to data center developers. Solar, wind and battery projects were planned on the expectation that data centers would raise demand and prices. If that demand shrinks, those projects lose revenue too, and the grid buildout the state was counting on gets more expensive to finance.

ERCOT has posted no new release since Nov. 10, and its Technical Advisory Committee meets today. At the PUC, the only new filings were procedural: Commission Staff's list of issues and Targa's protective-order certifications in SPS's large-load tariff case, Docket 60332. Beyond SpaceX's Starbase plant, no new behind-the-meter generation projects were announced, and there were no new TCEQ air permit actions reported in the eight tracked DFW counties.
