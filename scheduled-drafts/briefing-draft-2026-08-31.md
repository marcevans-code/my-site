# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through August 31, 2026)*

**Top Story:** A Tarrant County judge has yet to rule on whether to block Westlake's $1.1 billion Circle T data center, even as national coverage turned Gov. Abbott's statewide pause into a genuine midterm flashpoint today.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ Westlake's Circle T Injunction Fight Is Still Unresolved — TRO Now Runs Through Sept. 8

- Judge Kimberly Fitzpatrick of the 342nd District Court heard arguments Aug. 26 from the Lakes at Marshall Ridge HOA and Westlake residents against the Town of Westlake over the Circle T data center, and rather than ruling that day, extended the temporary restraining order through Sept. 8 — with a decision on converting it to a full temporary injunction still pending as of this morning.
- Plaintiffs argue the project's site plan omits the 150-foot setback and landscaped buffer required by Westlake's own zoning ordinance, on top of noise, heat-pollution, and property-value concerns tied to the 88-acre facility roughly 500 feet from Keller homes.
- The TRO has already blocked one thing outright: Westlake's town council still can't vote on its development agreement with PowerHouse Data Centers, which had included a developer offer of $914,000 toward pump-station upgrades.
- Westlake's mayor has maintained the council never had authority to block the project outright since the land is zoned for the use "by right" — meaning the lawsuit, not the council, is now the only thing standing between the project and construction.

**Today's Signal:** An extended TRO produces the same practical delay as an injunction would — Circle T isn't moving on its own schedule either way — which means the real news is how long a Tarrant County court is willing to let this sit before deciding anything.

### 2. 🗳️ Abbott's Data-Center Pause Becomes a National Midterm Storyline

- CNN's campaign-ad analysis, published today, found more than $31 million spent on data-center-themed political ads this cycle nationwide, with spending spiking to over 8% of all broadcast campaign spending last month — up from under 1% a year ago — and more than 99% of it opposing data-center projects.
- A companion Fast Company piece singled out Texas by name: Gov. Abbott grew the state into a 537-data-center hub, second in the country only to Virginia, before reversing to favor pausing new grid approvals pending an audit of electricity and water use, telling reporters "Simply put, Texans must come first."
- Both pieces describe Texas's freeze as reaching roughly 1,800 projects — the size of ERCOT's full large-load interconnection queue, of which the state has said data centers make up about 90%. That's a different, much larger number than the roughly 300 large-load projects this briefing has reported are actually inside the scope of Abbott's audit ahead of its Dec. 10 deadline.
- One rare ad that engaged with substance rather than avoiding it: a Public First Action-backed spot for Tyler-area GOP Rep. Nathaniel Moran touting his AI-regulation bill.

**Today's Signal:** The national press is treating "1,800 projects paused" as Texas's headline number right as the audit keeps getting reported here as covering a fraction of that — a gap between the national story and the local one that's likely to keep muddying coverage all the way to December.

### 3. 🌾 Prosper's Project Tomahawk Rezoning Still Has No Hearing Date

- The Dallas Business Journal's Monday roundup flagged Prosper as still "considering rezoning" agricultural land for White Rose Partners' $500 million Project Tomahawk — a roughly 37-acre data center site near FM 1385 and Parvin Road, adjacent to a Brazos Electric Power Cooperative substation.
- No hearing has been scheduled since town staff pulled the item from the Aug. 18 Planning & Zoning Commission agenda to review developer-commissioned engineering studies on noise, power, and water impact; Community Impact's most recent coverage still describes it only as "back on" the zoning agenda.
- White Rose has proposed closed-loop water systems and sound-dampening screens for the site — mitigation aimed at the same categories of complaints driving the Westlake litigation.

**Today's Signal:** Prosper is trying to get the engineering answers before a vote instead of after — exactly what Wise County's commissioners asked state lawmakers to require statewide back in March — which makes this one worth watching as a test of whether that approach actually heads off a lawsuit like Westlake's.

### 4. 🏭 Four DFW Counties Now Have Live TCEQ Dockets Open at Once

- TCEQ's pending-permit list, checked this morning, shows air-permit applications tied to data centers or their power supply in four of the eight counties this briefing tracks: Aligned Data Centers REIT's ADC Plano site (permit #131651, Collin County), Amazon.com Services' TXZ5 facility (permit #183703, Tarrant County), Ameresco's Grand Prairie renewable-natural-gas project (permit #181535, Denton County), and Big Hill Energy's gas-fired plant (permit #182795, Ellis County).
- None of the four carry a posted comment deadline in TCEQ's summary table; that detail lives only in each filing's own notice-of-receipt document, which hadn't been posted for all four as of this morning.

**Today's Signal:** Collin, Tarrant, Denton, and Ellis counties all having open dockets at the same time is a cleaner proof than any audit headline that the industry's air-permit pipeline isn't slowing down while Austin sorts out interconnection.

Quiet today on new land purchases by the major hyperscale and neocloud builders in the eight tracked counties, and no fresh noise-nuisance suits beyond the ongoing Westlake fight surfaced in today's search.
