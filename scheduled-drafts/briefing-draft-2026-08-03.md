## 1. ⚡ ERCOT & Grid Signals
**A Transmission Fight Now Threatens the Batch Zero Timeline**

- Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick and state Sen. Charles Schwertner on Friday called for the PUC to reject pending applications for a $33 billion transmission build-out, following a 15-hour Texas Senate Business and Commerce Committee hearing where hundreds of rural landowners pleaded for the process to be paused or stopped (E&E News/POLITICO, published today).
- ERCOT's own outlook assumes that build-out — including the Permian Basin Reliability Plan and the Strategic Transmission Expansion Plan — gets built on schedule; UT-Austin energy researcher Joshua Rhodes warned a pause "could throw a massive wrench into the whole works" for the Batch Zero large-load cohort, whose power allocations depend on that transmission timeline (E&E News/POLITICO).
- EIA's "Today in Energy" confirmed today that ERCOT's hourly peak load hit a record 91.1 gigawatts on July 22, 2026, underscoring the demand growth driving both the transmission push and the data-center interconnection backlog (U.S. Energy Information Administration, published today).
- PUC Chair Thomas Gleeson had already flagged in June that the commission may need to consolidate and decide on Permian Basin transmission projects together rather than case by case — a posture now colliding directly with Patrick and Schwertner's call to halt the process.
- **Today's Signal:** A transmission fight that started as a rural land-use dispute is now, per reporting published today, a direct threat to the Batch Zero data-center interconnection schedule — the two fights are no longer separate.

## 2. 🏗️ Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Red Oak's Data-Center Count Keeps Climbing as Financing Deals Get Bigger**

- Red Oak (Ellis County, just south of Dallas) is on track to host seven data centers — two operational, one under construction, four more planned — after DataBank closed $2 billion in construction financing this spring for the first three of eight planned buildings on a 300-acre campus; a fifth major building was filed with construction reportedly starting in early 2026 at an estimated $880 million (Dallas Morning News, July 22).
- Banks are reportedly in talks to lend Nexus Data Centers $15 billion to build a Texas campus for Anthropic, with financial guarantees from Google backing the loan — one of the largest financing packages reported for a single Texas AI campus to date (Wall Street Journal, this week).
- Meta and BlackRock's $14 billion, roughly 1-gigawatt El Paso data-center venture continues to draw statewide attention, with BlackRock holding an 80% stake in the new 1,000-acre campus; the deal has also drawn public opposition, including recent comments from musician Willie Nelson (Reuters, Los Angeles Times, Forbes; various outlets, past week).
- In Dallas proper, D Magazine reported that the aging Dallas Market Hall exhibition venue — winding down its events business — could become the site of a new data center, a potential brownfield-to-data-center conversion inside Dallas's urban core rather than its exurban ring.
- **Today's Signal:** Red Oak's move toward seven operating/planned facilities shows DFW's data-center growth is increasingly about existing hubs adding buildings, not just new greenfield sites — even as financing for individual campuses (Nexus/Anthropic's reported $15B) keeps getting larger.

## 3. 🌿 Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**No New DFW County Filing Today — the Bigger Story Is Who Gets to Comment**

- No new Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood County TCEQ draft-permit or public-notice filing tied to a data center or gas plant was confirmed as newly dated today.
- The backdrop remains NRG's June 18 withdrawal of its air permit application for the proposed 1,200-megawatt, gas-fired Tolar Power Center in Hood County, after the filing drew more than 11,000 public comments (Oil & Gas Watch; Fort Worth Star-Telegram).
- The Texas Tribune reported July 16 that a proposed rule — tied to reporting by Floodlight — could further limit Texans' ability to weigh in on pollution permits; the same reporting found at least 38 Texas data centers have already received "minor source" permits for on-site power generation since 2024, a faster, lower-scrutiny track than a full major-source review.
- Public Citizen's Texas Data Center Policy Guide, published this week, lays out how TCEQ issues air permits by emissions quantity under two separate programs (New Source Review and Title V Operating permits) — useful background for tracking which track any future DFW filing lands on.
- **Today's Signal:** With no new DFW filing today, the live issue is procedural: the Tribune/Floodlight reporting on shrinking public-comment rights means the next DFW-area filing could clear with less notice than Hood County residents got with Tolar.

## 4. 🏛️ Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Fort Worth's Aug. 4 Work Session Sets Up Next Week's Vote, as Religious Opposition Gets Its Own Headline**

- Fort Worth City Council is scheduled to discuss its proposed data-center moratorium at tomorrow's Aug. 4 work session, then vote Aug. 11 on whether to launch a 90-day pause on new data-center permitting and construction — following the Zoning Commission's July 8 rejection (7-4) of the city's original draft ordinance (Fort Worth Report, July 29; City of Fort Worth).
- Council member Jeanette Martinez has separately proposed a community-led task force to advise the council on data-center regulation, drawing cautious support from the Fort Worth Environmental Coalition of Communities and the Sierra Club's Greater Fort Worth chapter (Fort Worth Report, July 29).
- Four data centers currently operate in Fort Worth, with six more proposed — though major projects from Black Mountain and Edged Data Centers, already in planning or construction, would not be affected by a moratorium (Fort Worth Report).
- A statewide piece published today adds a new angle to local zoning fights: polling cited shows 71% of Texas Democrats and 44% of Texas Republicans oppose data-center development, with religious and moral objections — described by some residents as facilities built on "a deal with the devil" — motivating opposition in communities including North Texas (Dallas Observer/Texas Tribune syndication, published today).
- **Today's Signal:** Fort Worth's Aug. 4 work session is the last formal step before next week's moratorium vote, and today's polling on religious and partisan opposition suggests the council will be voting against a backdrop of broader, deepening public skepticism — not just neighborhood-level complaints.

## 5. 🔌 Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**A New Co-Location Model Emerges Statewide — DFW Still Waiting on Its Own Deal**

- No new dedicated gas-plant, gas-plus-battery, or behind-the-meter generation deal tied specifically to a DFW-area campus was confirmed as newly dated today.
- The Public Utility Commission of Texas last week approved a net-metering arrangement for a 260-megawatt AI data center co-located directly with a wind farm — a notable alternative to the dedicated-gas-plant model that has dominated coverage of Texas power-first strategies (Utility Dive).
- Texas Tribune's July 9 analysis found at least 15 data centers statewide plan to build their own behind-the-meter gas plants, with nine of them combined capable of emitting significant volumes of greenhouse gases; a separate study found Texas accounts for nearly half of 74 proposed BTM gas plants nationwide tied to data centers (KERA News, July 2).
- Roughly 2 gigawatts of behind-the-meter generation capacity is already online across four Texas projects, the majority of it tied to xAI's West Texas operations (Cleanview) — still no comparable DFW-area facility has come online.
- **Today's Signal:** The PUC's approval of a wind-plus-data-center net-metering deal shows Texas regulators are willing to bless power-first models beyond gas — worth watching whether a DFW campus tries a similar renewable co-location play instead of the dedicated-gas-plant route favored further west.
