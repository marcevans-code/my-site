# ⭐ Morning Briefing — October 27, 2026

*(Tuesday, October 27, 2026)*

**Top Story:** ServerDome Global told Hudspeth County residents at its proposed Hueco Ranch site Monday that the campus will run on its own 1-gigawatt gas plant with "no interconnection agreement whatsoever" to the grid.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🏜️ ServerDome Says Its Hudspeth County Campus Will Skip the Grid With Its Own 1-GW Gas Plant

- ServerDome Global hosted a community meeting Monday, Oct. 26, at the Hueco Ranch site in Hudspeth County, east of El Paso. Residents drove several miles out to question CEO David Keegan and co-founder Ryan Wallace (KFOX14, KVIA).
- Wallace said the campus will generate its own power from a 1-gigawatt natural gas plant: "it will be behind the meter. There will be no interconnection agreement whatsoever." The company says it plans closed-loop cooling and that residents' power bills won't be affected (KFOX14, KVIA).
- KFOX14 put the first phase at $15 billion, 700 acres and 10 dome-shaped buildings. Construction Review reported a 2,000-acre campus but gave $1.5 billion in its body text against $15 billion in its headline, so the figures in circulation still don't line up.
- Keegan conceded that "data centers do make noise" and promised noise and transportation studies, without a date. Desert Haven resident Jessica Macias, who helps lead a newly formed community group, called it "devastating" to see a data center come to a rural community. County Judge Joanna MacKenzie estimates 800 construction jobs over four to five years and 200 to 300 permanent jobs (KVIA).
- The project team said it would attend Tuesday's Hudspeth County Commissioners Court meeting, and Keegan plans to take questions at the county's Halloween event Oct. 31 (KVIA, KFOX14).

**Today's Signal:** Staying off the grid is the point. A 1-GW campus with no ERCOT interconnection request doesn't sit in the large-load queue Austin is auditing, so the governor's pause doesn't slow it down. What can slow it is an air permit for that gas plant, which is the piece of this project residents should be asking about first.

### 2. 💰 CenterPoint Adds $4 Billion to Its Houston Grid Plan, and Flags Batch Zero as a Risk

- CenterPoint Energy reported third-quarter results before the market opened Tuesday, Oct. 27, and raised its 10-year capital plan by $4 billion, to $70.7 billion. It also pulled another $1.5 billion into 2026–2030 to keep up with demand growth in Greater Houston (company release).
- CEO Jason Wells said the extra money is meant to "expand system capacity to accommodate growth." The plan covers large-load customers and 765-kV transmission projects, and the company says it still sees at least $10 billion of further opportunities through 2035.
- The release's risk language names the audit directly: large-load connections could slip if customers "fail to obtain ERCOT approval into Batch Zero and future batch frameworks." How many gigawatts actually connect, it says, depends on ERCOT approval.
- CenterPoint raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance to $1.90–$1.92, and claims the growth will produce about $5 billion in savings for Texas residential and commercial customers over the decade. Going in, analysts had pointed to CenterPoint's roughly 10 GW of Batch Zero baseload submissions (Investing.com).

**Today's Signal:** A utility is spending more on its grid while warning, in the same release, that the customers it's building for might not get through ERCOT's audit. Ratepayers should watch the $5 billion savings claim, since it only holds up if the large loads actually arrive and pay their share.

### 3. 📉 The Pause Shows Up in Deal Counts and Credit Ratings

- The Texas Lawbook reported Monday, Oct. 26 (teaser only), that Texas M&A volume and value fell together in the third quarter, the first such drop since 2025. Dealmakers named Abbott's pause on data center grid connections and permits as one factor. Some investors, including ones holding behind-the-meter power assets, are waiting to see whether billion-dollar power investments will pay off.
- S&P Global Ratings warned that data center projects could face credit downgrades as moratoriums and cancellations spread, Bloomberg reported (via Insurance Journal, Oct. 26). The Oct. 23 report studied moratoriums in five states, including Texas, and counted 146 delayed or canceled U.S. projects, about three-quarters tied to local opposition. S&P says the credit impact is minimal so far.
- S&P's concern is contract terms. Delays that "meaningfully delay projects" could expose owners to rent credits and tenant termination rights.

**Today's Signal:** Until now the pause mostly showed up in queue numbers. Now lawyers and credit analysts are counting it too. If tenants start invoking termination rights over delays in Texas, the price of the audit is no longer just slower hookups.

### 4. 🗳️ One Week Out, Data Centers Are a Closing Argument

- Reason (Oct. 27) argued that the data center backlash is undercutting the GOP's pro-growth pitch in Texas. It cited two UT Austin polls: in 2025, nearly 60% of Texans were supportive of or neutral toward a data center in their neighborhood, but by August 2026, 57% said they'd oppose a local project. The piece puts James Talarico ahead of Ken Paxton by two to six points in the Senate race.
- Reason summarized the platforms. Talarico wants data centers to pay the full cost of their power, an end to the sales-tax exemption on equipment and local veto power. Paxton calls for limits on water and power use and an end to the sales-tax exemption. Gina Hinojosa wants a construction moratorium until the Legislature meets, and Abbott has promised new regulations after once calling Texas the "epicenter of AI development."
- WFAA aired one-on-one interviews with Abbott and Hinojosa as their closing arguments (headline/snippet only). The Wall Street Journal named four places where the midterms will "decide the fate of data centers" (headline/snippet only), and The Verge (headline/snippet only) listed Texas among the states where candidates' data center positions could decide close races.
- Houston's KTRH pointed to Politico polling showing about half of Americans oppose a data center within three miles of home. The station framed the opposition as partly "inorganic," without offering evidence for that.

**Today's Signal:** Every major statewide candidate now has a data center plan, and the Senate rivals agree on more than their parties do: Talarico and Paxton both want to end the sales-tax exemption. Whichever of them wins, expect that exemption to come up when the Legislature meets in January.

### 5. 🖥️ Fireworks Books $94.4 Million of GPU Capacity in an Existing Dallas Data Center

- Forum Edge AI, a joint venture of Nasdaq-listed Forum Markets and Edge Node, announced Monday, Oct. 26, a 36-month, roughly $94.4 million contract to supply Nvidia-backed inference provider Fireworks with Nvidia B300 GPU capacity (company release).
- The capacity sits in what the release calls an "existing, powered data center facility in Dallas" that already has permits and power. It doesn't name the building or its megawatts. Fireworks made a substantial prepayment, and deployment is expected in late December or early January.

**Today's Signal:** This is what AI growth looks like during the pause: filling existing, already powered halls in Dallas instead of waiting for new grid hookups. Expect more of these small deals to fill already powered halls while large new campuses sit in the queue.

### 6. 📑 PUC Staff Finds Bimergen's Data Center Power Application Complete

- PUC staff filed a recommendation Monday, Oct. 26, in Docket 60368, finding Emergen Commodities' application for an Option 2 retail electric provider certificate "sufficient for further review." The filing notes that the finding "does not address the merits" (PUC Interchange). Emergen is the Bimergen Energy subsidiary that wants to sell power directly to large data center customers.
- Staff proposed Nov. 19 as the deadline for interventions and comments, and Dec. 3 for staff's request for a hearing or recommendation on final disposition.

**Today's Signal:** The paperwork is moving faster than the business case. Bimergen still hasn't named a customer, and a Nov. 19 intervention deadline gives anyone worried about a thinly capitalized REP serving a few giant loads a little over three weeks to say so.
