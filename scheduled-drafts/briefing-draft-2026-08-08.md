## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Amazon Confirms a Dedicated 7.65-GW Gas Plant to Power a New West Texas AI Campus**

- Amazon is developing a new data center campus in Pecos County, Texas, to be powered by the GW Ranch gas plant — a dedicated, 7.65-gigawatt facility across 35 turbines being developed by Pacifico Energy (Barron's, Aug. 7; Construction Review Online, Aug. 8).
- Amazon has filed three data center construction permits tied to the project this week (Barron's, Aug. 7).
- The plant's permitted emissions run "upward of 30 million metric tons of carbon dioxide annually," with up to 750 megawatts of planned solar and 1.8 gigawatts of battery storage layered alongside the gas turbines.
- Microsoft is building a competing facility roughly 30 miles away with 2 gigawatts of capacity, making Pecos County one of the more contested new build sites in the state.
- Amazon characterized the investment as protecting ratepayers, saying the campus is "powered by new on-site generation that won't raise electricity costs for Texas families."
- No new DFW-area land-acquisition filing (Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood counties) was confirmed as newly dated today; today's activity is centered in West Texas.

> **Today's Signal:** Amazon just showed the post-audit playbook in real time — building a dedicated gas plant large enough that a Pecos County campus never has to touch Abbott's frozen interconnection queue at all.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Fort Worth's Aug. 11 Vote Shifts From Zoning Rules to a Straight Moratorium Question**

- Fort Worth officials canceled a planned Tuesday presentation on data-center zoning regulations, opting instead to "take a step back" and assess how Gov. Abbott's statewide audit affects local projects, according to Assistant City Manager Jesica McEachern (KERA News, Aug. 5).
- That shift follows the city's zoning commission voting 7-4 on July 8 to reject the city's proposed data-center regulations and send the matter back to Council.
- Council members, who indicated majority support for a moratorium in a July 9 letter, are now expected to vote on enforcing that moratorium at the Aug. 11 meeting — not on the regulations originally on the table.
- Separately, Texas Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller said publicly on Aug. 6 that Republicans risk losing statewide elections over the backlash to rapid data-center development, criticizing Gov. Abbott's handling of the issue; the remarks were carried by at least six Texas outlets by Aug. 7, including The Texas Tribune, KSAT, and KERA.

> **Today's Signal:** When a statewide elected Republican is warning his own party could lose races over data centers, Fort Worth's Aug. 11 moratorium vote stops being a local zoning story and starts looking like a preview of a 2026 campaign issue.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**Amazon's Pecos County Plant Becomes the New Largest Dedicated Build Yet Reported**

- Amazon's 7.65-GW GW Ranch plant (Section 2) is now the largest dedicated, off-queue generation project tied to a Texas AI campus reported in this briefing, surpassing NRG's 3.2-billion-dollar "Bring Your Own Power" plant and Williams' six-plant national behind-the-meter fleet covered in prior editions.
- Pairing gas turbines with up to 750 MW of solar and 1.8 GW of battery storage suggests operators are increasingly building hybrid gas-plus-renewables-plus-storage sites rather than gas-only plants, likely to help meet grid ride-through requirements.
- Barron's places the move inside a roughly 90-gigawatt industry-wide pipeline of similar dedicated generation projects announced since early 2025 — Amazon's plant is one data point in an accelerating national pattern, not an outlier.

> **Today's Signal:** Every operator facing Abbott's frozen interconnection queue is reaching for the same "bring your own power" playbook, and the scale keeps climbing — Amazon's 7.65-GW plant is this week's new high-water mark.
