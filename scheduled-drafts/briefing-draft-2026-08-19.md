## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚖️ Keller Sues Westlake Over Circle T

- Keller's city council voted unanimously Tuesday night to authorize legal action against Westlake over the proposed Circle T data center, escalating a fight a Tarrant County judge had already paused with a temporary restraining order, according to WFAA and CandysDirt.
- The underlying claim, per Audacy's reporting, is that Westlake failed to give proper public notice for planning-and-zoning and council actions taken in late 2024 and early 2025 — meaning the legal fight could reach back to the project's original approvals, not just Monday's blocked vote.

**Today's Signal:** Keller's vote turns a judge's temporary block into a full legal campaign — the fight has now fully left the council chamber for the courtroom, and the Aug. 26 injunction hearing is the next real checkpoint.

### 2. 🏗️ White Rose Unveils $500M Prosper Project

- Dallas-based White Rose Partners is proposing "Project Tomahawk," a data center on roughly 37 acres in northwest Prosper in Denton County, near FM 1385 and Parvin Road, in a deal valued at more than $500 million, according to WFAA and The Dallas Morning News, both of which reported new details today.
- The firm told WFAA that Prosper residents should expect "minimal impact" from the project, though neither outlet reported what power source the site would use or whether it has filed for ERCOT interconnection.

**Today's Signal:** Project Tomahawk lands in Denton County days after Keller and Westlake made headlines over a nearly identical fight — proof the interconnection freeze and political backlash haven't slowed the pace of new proposals landing in DFW-area counties, just the pace at which ERCOT can act on them.

### 3. 📢 Red Oak Pushback, Dallas News Calls for a Plan

- In Ellis County, residents in and around Red Oak are pushing back on the pace of the area's data-center buildout — now home to multiple DataBank and Google campuses — a tension a Dallas Morning News editorial published today cited directly.
- The editorial calls on the state to adopt a coherent data-center policy rather than leave it to individual cities and counties to negotiate case by case, noting Red Oak alone is on track to host seven data centers between operational, under-construction, and planned facilities.

**Today's Signal:** Pairing Red Oak's pushback with Keller's lawsuit shows the same friction spreading to a second DFW county that's already years into hosting data centers, not just the newest proposals.

### 4. 🏛️ Data Centers Get Their Day at the Capitol

- The Texas House Committee on State Affairs convened at 8 a.m. today for a hearing combining two interim charges — the statewide development of data centers and a review of the proposed 765-kilovolt transmission lines meant to relieve grid congestion from that buildout — with invited and public testimony scheduled through the morning, per Community Impact's coverage and the committee's own posted agenda.
- Industrial Info Resources reported today that Texas's pause on new data centers is "mirroring growing U.S. skepticism" toward the industry, framing today's hearing as coming just two weeks after Gov. Abbott's Aug. 3 audit-and-pause directive.
- Data-center and transmission-line opponents organized a rally on the Capitol's south steps from 8 to 11 a.m., timed to coincide with the hearing, while separate reporting from Politico and KUT today described growing divisions among state leaders — and among rural voters ahead of next year's governor's race — over how far the state should go in reining in the industry.

**Today's Signal:** For the first time, the data-center fight is playing out in a legislative hearing room instead of just PUCT dockets and county council chambers — and today's wider coverage shows it's becoming a statewide political fault line, not just a regulatory dispute.

### 5. 📊 New Estimate: Gas Plants Could Add 20% to U.S. Emissions

- A Bloomberg analysis reported today found that the wave of dedicated gas plants built to power AI data centers could add roughly 20% to total U.S. power-sector emissions, with Texas — home to a large share of the behind-the-meter gas capacity already under development — positioned as a major driver of that increase.

**Today's Signal:** This is the first nationwide estimate to put a single percentage on the power-first strategy's emissions cost, and given how much of the behind-the-meter pipeline traced in this briefing sits in Texas, the state's share of that 20% figure is likely outsized.
