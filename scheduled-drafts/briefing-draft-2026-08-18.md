## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**Wharton County Project Hits Grid Friction**

- Amazon's "Project Eagle" campus — a roughly 3,000-acre AWS data center site near Boling in Wharton County, southwest of Houston — is now facing its own regulatory headwinds as the statewide interconnection pause leaves the project's grid-connection plans in limbo, The Real Deal reported today. Few details are yet public on how AWS intends to power the site if ERCOT service is delayed.

**Today's Signal:** Even a hyperscaler's lower-profile build isn't shielded from the interconnection freeze — Project Eagle's grid uncertainty shows the pause is now touching projects statewide, not just the ones already tangled up in political fights.

## 🌎 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**Vantage Hearings Officially Pulled, No Reason Given**

- TCEQ has confirmed it canceled both the Aug. 17 and Aug. 24 public comment hearings for Vantage Data Centers' Omicron Drive campus in San Antonio, according to Public Citizen Texas, closing out the uncertainty left over from Monday's briefing. The agency has not stated why either hearing was pulled.
- No new air-permit applications or public-notice postings turned up today in the eight DFW-area counties this briefing tracks (Collin, Ellis, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, Hood).

**Today's Signal:** TCEQ pulling both Vantage hearings without explanation raises the same transparency questions already dogging the state's audit and interconnection freeze — for developers, no hearing means no public timeline either.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**Judge Halts Westlake Vote Over Circle T Data Center**

- A Tarrant County judge signed a temporary restraining order roughly an hour before Monday's Westlake Town Council meeting, halting any council action on the development agreement for the Circle T data center after Keller residents sued to block it, according to WFAA and the Fort Worth Star-Telegram.
- Westlake council members agreed to push the vote until after Aug. 26, when a judge will decide whether to grant a full temporary injunction extending the block, the Fort Worth Report reported — meaning the town is legally barred from acting on the project for at least two more weeks.
- The council chamber needed extra security for Monday's meeting as Keller and Westlake residents packed the room; homeowners have pointed to the roughly 500-foot distance between the data center site and their backyards as central to the dispute, per NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth.

**Today's Signal:** The fight over Circle T just left the council chamber for the courtroom — a judge, not a vote, now controls whether this data center moves forward, and the Aug. 26 injunction hearing is the one to watch.
