# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 10, 2026

(Covering Monday, November 9 – Tuesday, November 10, 2026)

**Top Story:** On the first day of bill filing, Senate Finance Chair Joan Huffman filed SB 293 to end Texas' sales tax exemption for data centers, and 22 other senators from both parties signed on as joint authors. The Comptroller puts the break at about $3.3 billion for the next budget.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 💸 Huffman's Bill to End the Data Center Sales Tax Break Starts With 23 of 31 Senators

- Monday, Nov. 9: Sen. Joan Huffman, R-Houston, who chairs the Senate Finance Committee, filed Senate Bill 293. It repeals the sales and use tax exemptions for property used in data centers and large data center projects (Texas Legislature Online). Exemptions already granted would end when the bill takes effect, and applications pending with the Comptroller would be denied (Fox 26 Houston; Miami Herald via Yahoo News).
- TLO lists 22 joint authors besides Huffman, including Republicans Paul Bettencourt, Lois Kolkhorst, Tan Parker and Charles Schwertner and Democrats Carol Alvarado, Judith Zaffirini and Royce West. Community Impact counted 23 of the 31 senators and senators-elect behind it.
- Huffman said the 2013 exemption cost about $14.6 million in its first two years, while the Comptroller now estimates about $3.3 billion in forgone sales tax for the coming biennium (Fox 26). "No tax exemption should run on autopilot," she said. She also said "Texas should welcome investment" but "should not write a blank check" (Fox 26; Miami Herald).
- House members filed their own repeal bills Monday: HB 167 and HB 408, which would let facilities already getting the break keep it until it expires (Houston Public Media), and El Paso Democrat Vince Perez's HB 851, which also bars data centers from enterprise project tax refunds (TLO; KVIA). The Tribune counted three repeal bills filed by Democrats and Republicans by Monday afternoon.

**Today's Signal:** The sales tax break is the one data center policy the GOP Senate and the incoming Democratic House can agree on from day one. Twenty-three senators is more than enough to pass SB 293 in that chamber, so the real fight will be over Huffman's plan to end exemptions already granted. That's where companies with projects underway will push for the grandfather clause the House bills already include.

### 2. 📑 Day-One Filings Also Target PUC Permits, Grid Cost Recovery, County Authority and Water Reporting

- Sen. Judith Zaffirini, D-Laredo, filed five data center bills Monday (TLO; Community Impact; Texas Tribune):
    - SB 32 would require a Public Utility Commission permit to build a "large computational load site," with administrative penalties.
    - SB 33 covers how electricity costs for those sites are recovered, so other customers don't pay them.
    - SB 34 would give counties authority to regulate the development of certain large electricity users.
    - SB 35 would have ERCOT share large-load customer information with the Texas Water Development Board.
    - SB 36 would make data centers ineligible for state and local incentive programs, including Chapter 312 abatements and 380/381 agreements, and stop cities and counties from renewing existing ones.
- HB 529 would require large data centers to report their water use, write conservation plans and assess their effect on local supplies (Houston Public Media).
- Perez filed four more bills aimed at deals like Meta's in El Paso, which the city and county approved in December 2023. HB 729 would cap a data center's tax break at 10 years and require a public vote before each new phase gets one. HB 732 would bar abatements, local cash grants, tax increment financing, Enterprise Zone benefits and Texas Enterprise Fund money for these projects. HB 734 would require unredacted agreements to be filed with the state. HB 730 would bar regulators from shifting gas plant costs onto other customers unless residential bills won't rise. El Paso Electric is seeking approval for a gas plant to serve Meta's site (KVIA).
- Agriculture Commissioner-elect Clayton Tucker, a Democrat, told KETK in Tyler on Monday that he will bring a statewide moratorium proposal to the Capitol in January, so the state can study the facilities' power and water demands itself instead of relying on company-reported data (KETK via AOL). Public Citizen's Kaiba White told the Austin Free Press she is helping draft a moratorium bill.
- Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa repeated her case in a CNN interview with Jake Tapper, recapped by TechRadar Tuesday: "I hate the way they are taking advantage of Texas."

**Today's Signal:** Zaffirini's package covers the issues Abbott's pause is meant to address: who permits these sites, who pays for their grid hookups, who gets water data, and whether counties can say no. If SB 32 passes, the PUC would issue permits itself, not just respond to ERCOT's interconnection queue.

### 3. 🔌 NRG Sells Three ERCOT Gas Plants, Including Johnson County's, but Keeps Its 1.2 GW Data Center Project

- Monday, Nov. 9: NRG Energy agreed to sell six gas-fired plants totaling just under 4.9 GW to LS Power for $4 billion in cash. Closing is targeted for the second quarter of 2027, pending federal and New York approvals (NRG; Power Engineering).
- Three of the plants are on the ERCOT grid: the Jack County Generation Plant near Jacksboro, the Johnson County plant and the R.W. Miller plant. The other three are in New York and New England (Power Engineering; Benzinga via TradingView).
- NRG said in its third-quarter release the same day that its previously disclosed 1.2 GW "Bring Your Own Power" data center project "remains on track." It gave no location or customer. NRG's Texas segment earned $1.02 billion in adjusted EBITDA for the quarter, up $211 million from a year earlier (NRG).
- Power Engineering reported that NRG still plans to bring 1.1 GW of new ERCOT generation online by 2028 through two Texas Energy Fund projects and is developing 1.5 GW of new gas generation in Texas.

**Today's Signal:** NRG is selling existing gas plants in North Texas but keeping new generation and its data center deal. It wants to supply data centers with new power, not just sell into the grid from older plants. LS Power will own plants in Johnson and Jack counties as DFW-area demand rises.

### 4. 🗂️ The Data Center Coalition Joins the Panhandle Large-Load Tariff Case, and a Weatherford Firm Asks the PUC to Measure Data Centers Itself

- Monday, Nov. 9: The Data Center Coalition and the Federal Executive Agencies moved to intervene in Docket 60332, Southwestern Public Service Co.'s application for large-load customer tariff provisions, and the PUC issued an order requesting lists of issues. SPS also filed its answers to Texas Industrial Energy Consumers' first questions (PUC Interchange).
- Friday, Nov. 6, after Friday's edition: the Office of Public Utility Counsel requested a hearing in the same docket. TIEC requested one Oct. 28, and the PUC suspended the tariff's effective date Oct. 30. Targa Resources and Golden Spread Electric Cooperative are also parties (PUC Interchange).
- Also Monday, Green Recursive Utility Service LLC of Weatherford filed a 73-page petition in Project 59999, the standard large-load interconnection agreement rulemaking. It asks the PUC to adopt a new rule on "Measurement and Reporting of Resource Consumption by Large Computational Loads" and requests a public hearing. The petition argues that self-reporting isn't enough (Project 59999, Item 8).
- Two Batch Zero complainants filed routine paperwork Monday: Medina County Power NR I added counsel in Docket 60356, and South Texas Electric Cooperative filed protective order certifications in Docket 60410. Both cases stay paused until Nov. 30 (PUC Interchange).

**Today's Signal:** SPS serves the Panhandle on the SPP grid, outside ERCOT, so its tariff is a separate test of what data centers will have to pay up front. With the industry's main trade group, federal agencies, consumer advocates and big industrial users all now in the case, a contested hearing is likely.

### 5. 🏭 Skybox Says Wichita Falls Is Still a 2027 Project

- Monday, Nov. 9: Skybox Datacenters chief development officer Haynes Strader told the Wichita Falls Times Record News the company still expects its Wichita Falls project to "kick off in 2027, it's just going to depend on when."
- Strader said the site is "zoned properly," has community support, won't use much water and won't strain the grid. He said data centers are effectively under a moratorium already because of Abbott's August orders, and that tougher legislation could cause delays.
- He said the project is going through ERCOT's review, which won't produce approvals until after December. The city sold Skybox 225 acres in its business park in October 2025 for $14.6 million, and Skybox plans six buildings, a number Strader said could change (Times Record News).
- Skybox is also one of the five Batch Zero complainants. Its Docket 60431 complaint against ERCOT is paused until Nov. 30 (PUC Interchange).

**Today's Signal:** Skybox is saying publicly that it can wait out the pause, while its complaint against ERCOT is still pending. Its pitch, already zoned with little water use, is the case every developer will make to the Hinojosa administration.

### 6. 🗺️ Central Texas Local Officials Now Back Limits on Data Centers

- Monday, Nov. 9: The Austin Free Press reported that Democrats won four of five Hays County Commissioners Court seats, including county judge, and two Williamson County seats, also including county judge. Data center opposition was strong in both counties.
- San Marcos Mayor-elect Amanda Rodriguez, who led the city's zoning-based data center ban as a council member, called the issue a "litmus test" for voters. She noted that Texas counties still can't ban data centers or set binding water or energy rules, the gap Zaffirini's SB 34 is aimed at.
- In Austin, city staff are drafting a data center framework at the council's direction. Both District 1 runoff candidates, Amber Goodwin and Misael Ramos, support a citywide ban on large-scale data centers, and the runoffs are Dec. 12 (Austin Free Press).

**Today's Signal:** The new county officials in Hays and Williamson ran on data centers but don't yet have the power to regulate them. Whether SB 34 or a similar bill passes will decide if those votes change anything.

The Texas Tribune's data center series has added nothing since Nov. 4, TCEQ has posted no news releases since Oct. 21, and ERCOT's release page still stops at Sept. 21. No air permit actions or zoning votes turned up in the eight tracked DFW counties, and no report has appeared yet on Cottle County's Project Matter hearing or a Westlake vote on the Circle T settlement.
