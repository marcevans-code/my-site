## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 📏 New Braunfels Adopts Half-Mile Setbacks and a 35-Decibel Limit for Data Centers, but Its Rules Stop at the City Line

- Monday, Nov. 16 (reported Thursday): The New Braunfels City Council unanimously gave final approval to Ordinance 26-1266. Data centers, including cryptocurrency mining, are allowed only in the city's two heavy-industrial districts and only with a council-approved special use permit, and no building permit can be sought until that permit is granted. The Planning Commission had recommended it 7-0 on Nov. 4 (The Texas Dispatch, citing the New Braunfels Herald-Zeitung and the posted ordinance text).
- Siting: no data center over the Edwards Aquifer recharge or contributing zone, none within 3,000 feet of homes and other sensitive sites (the draft said 2,000), and none in the 100-year floodplain or within 300 feet of it or of a mapped fault line (The Texas Dispatch).
- Operations: a 35-decibel limit at property lines next to homes, schools, churches, parks or trails, including during generator testing; closed-loop cooling that doesn't evaporate water; annual public reporting of water and electricity use; and letters from the electric, water and wastewater providers certifying no impact on, and no costs passed to, existing customers. Converted buildings aren't grandfathered, and a surety bond for site cleanup was added at Mayor Michael French's request (The Texas Dispatch).
- The posted text and the Herald-Zeitung disagree on the minimum lot (50 acres vs. 100) and the height cap (30 feet and two stories vs. 15 feet and one story). The Dispatch says the signed ordinance will settle it. Mayor Pro Tem Michael Capizzi said the permit requirement lets the council "add additional restrictions" project by project (The Texas Dispatch).
- None of it reaches the city's extraterritorial jurisdiction. Under Local Government Code §212.003, a city can extend subdivision rules there but can't regulate land use, and Texas counties have no general zoning power (The Texas Dispatch).

**Today's Signal:** The same Monday New Braunfels voted, TCEQ approved the air permit for CloudBurst's 1,605 MW gas plant (42 turbines plus fuel cells, permitted in Evolve Energy's name) in Guadalupe County, just south of San Marcos (FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen). That's the gap in one day: the city wrote rules for the land it controls, and the big project nearby is on land it doesn't. Unless the Legislature gives counties authority over data centers in 2027, rules like these push projects outward instead of stopping them.

### 2. ✉️ Cedar Creek Neighbors Write to Anthropic, Which Says It Hasn't Signed a Lease for the Bastrop Gas-Powered Campus

- Thursday, Nov. 19: Seventy-three neighbors living within a mile of Earl Callahan Road in Cedar Creek signed a letter to Anthropic asking it to support a public hearing on the planned data center and gas plant and to hear their concerns. Residents say the site is "leased by Anthropic," but an Anthropic spokeswoman told KEYE by phone that the company is in talks and hasn't signed a lease (KEYE/CBS Austin, Adela Uchida).
- The power plant and data center are being built by Pacifico CCK Energy and developed by BlackChamber (KEYE). Pacifico's TCEQ standard permit application covers 48 gas turbines and roughly 1,300 MW, a permit type residents note requires no public notice or comment period (CBS Austin, Jahmal Kennedy, Oct. 13). A Pacifico fact sheet says about 600 of the property's 2,842 acres would be developed (CBS Austin, Vincent Martorano, Oct. 12).
- Bastrop County commissioners on Oct. 13 and the Bastrop City Council on Nov. 3 both asked TCEQ to hold a public meeting on the plant. No meeting has been scheduled (CBS Austin; KXAN).
- Neighbors say the companies are racing to win TCEQ approval before Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office, since projects approved before then would be grandfathered under her proposed pause. "There are no guardrails on this at all," said resident Lucy Diese, a mother of two young children (KEYE).
- ABC13 reported Thursday that TCEQ resumed approvals before ERCOT's community impact findings, due Dec. 10. ERCOT's Nov. 5 preliminary update found 63% of medium-load projects couldn't say whether they were power self-sufficient, and 70% couldn't answer the community impact questions. The governor's office hadn't said whether the restarted approvals meet Abbott's September requirements (ABC13, Brianna Willis).

**Today's Signal:** Because a standard permit doesn't require a hearing, the neighbors are pressing the company that would use the power instead of the agency that permits it. Anthropic's answer, that it's in talks but hasn't signed, means the air permit could be granted before the tenant commits. Two local governments have asked TCEQ for a meeting, and the agency doesn't have to hold one. Whether it acts on Pacifico's application before January is the question that matters most here.

### 3. 🎙️ Hinojosa Calls for "a Ban on New Data Center Permits" Until Lawmakers Act, and Says She'd Work With Musk

- Thursday, Nov. 19: Asked in a Fox News interview whether she'd ban data center permits, Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa said, "I don't hate data centers. I just hate the way they're taking advantage of Texans right now." She added that Texas lacks laws to protect residents, so "we need a moratorium, a ban on new data center permits until we can pass laws to protect Texans" (Benzinga).
- She said she hasn't spoken with Elon Musk but looks forward to it, and that she'd work with him as long as the work is about Texans "doing better and thriving." She described herself as an "economic populist" (Benzinga).
- The interview came three days after TCEQ issued the air permit for SpaceX's Starbase data center gas plant, and two days after Cameron County commissioners voted against advancing SpaceX's tax abatement request (Benzinga; covered Wednesday).

**Today's Signal:** "A ban on new data center permits" is narrower than stopping construction. It describes a freeze on new approvals, not revoking ones already granted. That makes the next eight weeks of TCEQ decisions important: a permit issued before she's sworn in may be beyond her reach, and one still pending may not be.

### 4. 🛣️ Data Center Companies Are Paying for $13.5 Million in State Highway Work, Up for a Vote Today

- Today, Friday, Nov. 20, at 10 a.m.: The Texas Transportation Commission meets in Austin. Its draft minute order 8a includes $7.5 million from Borden County DC, LLC for U.S. 180 improvements west of Gail to reach its site, and $6 million from QTS Big Spring, LLC for turn lanes on FM 818 (The Texas Dispatch, Nov. 19).
- Together the two make up about $13.5 million of the $19.57 million in 16 donations on the agenda, more than two-thirds. Most of the rest come from homebuilders (The Texas Dispatch).
- A Borden County data center campus is on the comptroller's register of large projects. The Dispatch found no announced QTS project in Big Spring and none on the register, and the state's paperwork lists the FM 818 work in Howard County in one place and Borden County in another (The Texas Dispatch).
- The same agenda asks whether to start condemnation on 146 tracts in 11 counties, including 58 along U.S. 380 in Collin County and 19 along FM 157 in Johnson County. The notice lists tracts by highway and parcel and doesn't name owners or prices. The Dispatch doesn't tie those tracts to data centers (The Texas Dispatch).

**Today's Signal:** A road donation agreement can be the first public sign of a data center. QTS Big Spring, LLC shows up here with $6 million for turn lanes and no announced project behind it. If the commission accepts the money, a Howard County appraisal record, TDLR filing or abatement request is the next place the name is likely to appear.

ERCOT hasn't posted a release since Nov. 10, and its calendar shows nothing new on large loads before the Dec. 7–8 board meeting. Project 59999 at the PUC has had no filings since Nov. 11, and no data center action from Thursday's open meeting turned up in any search. TCEQ still hasn't posted a release since Oct. 21, and no new air permit actions were reported in the eight tracked DFW counties. Lavon's moratorium vote and Brazos County's RELLIS abatement transfer still haven't been reported.
