# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 16, 2026

(Covering Friday, November 13 – Monday, November 16, 2026)

**Top Story:** Cottle County's commissioners unanimously backed a reinvestment zone and tax abatement for Aetheris's 96 MW data center and 180 MW gas plant on church-owned ranch land. The county gets $1.5 million within 90 days and $2.5 million a year after that.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🐂 Cottle County Approves the Tax Deal for a Gas-Powered Data Center on Church-Owned Ranch Land

- Monday, Nov. 9 (first reported Friday, Nov. 13): Cottle County's five-member commissioners court unanimously endorsed a special reinvestment zone covering 4,252 acres near Paducah and voted for developer Aetheris's multimillion-dollar tax abatement request (The Salt Lake Tribune).
- The project, called Project Matter, pairs a 96-megawatt data center with a proposed 180-megawatt natural gas plant meant to keep it off the grid. Aetheris can lease up to 3,710 acres of Triangle Ranch, about 15 miles east of Paducah, from Farmland Reserve, a subsidiary of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, plus 542 acres from another landowner (The Salt Lake Tribune; Chron).
- County Judge Karl Holloway confirmed the deal brings the county $1.5 million within 90 days and $2.5 million a year after that. The agreement is meant to guarantee at least 40 well-paying jobs. The votes start a 90-day clock for RS 70 Land Holdings LLC, Aetheris's local entity, to begin development and make its first payment (The Salt Lake Tribune).
- James Gilbert, whose family ranches 900 acres next to Triangle Ranch, said the project would "change everything we've ever been about." Paducah resident Laura Richards asked commissioners to wait until the post-election rules were clear. Others backed it for what it could pay for schools and the library (The Salt Lake Tribune).
- Aetheris says it will use closed-loop cooling and ship in its own water rather than pump groundwater. AgReserves said Nov. 2 that Aetheris is "not affiliated with Farmland Reserve or the church" (Chron, Sunday, Nov. 15; The Salt Lake Tribune).

**Today's Signal:** Cottle County signed six days after the election, and the timing matters. SB 338 (story 2) would only reach abatements signed on or after Sept. 1, 2027, so this deal is grandfathered either way. What Aetheris still needs is a TCEQ air permit for 180 MW of gas generation, and the abatement doesn't help with that.

### 2. 🏛️ Sixteen Senators Behind Charles Perry's SB 338 Want Data Center Abatements Capped at 50% and Grid Hookups Tied to Water-Saving Cooling

- Thursday, Nov. 12 (reported after Friday's edition): Sen. Charles Perry (R–Lubbock) filed SB 338, "relating to data centers," with 15 joint authors, among them Finance Chair Joan Huffman, Lois Kolkhorst, Bryan Hughes, Angela Paxton and Democrat Juan "Chuy" Hinojosa (Texas Legislature Online; Texas Scorecard).
- The bill would bar cities from exempting more than 50% of a data center's abatable value, and it extends the same data center terms to county and school-district deals. Those agreements would have to require closed-loop, dry, saline or other water-efficient cooling, water-use estimates and annual reports to the PUC, payment for environmental cleanup, and repayment of all lost tax revenue plus interest if the agreement is terminated (SB 338 text).
- At the grid level, the PUC would have to bar utilities from connecting any data center of 100,000 square feet or more to ERCOT unless it uses one of those cooling systems and has filed its water plans, and to disconnect sites that later fall out of compliance. The PUC could also set noise, light and setback standards (SB 338 text).
- TCEQ would have to issue emergency orders stopping construction of a data center that doesn't meet those standards under its construction stormwater permit. The bill would take effect Sept. 1, 2027, and apply only to agreements, interconnections and permit filings after that date (SB 338 text).
- Texas Scorecard also tallied House companions to Huffman's SB 293 sales tax repeal: HB 167 (Trey Martinez Fischer), HB 408 (Carrie Isaac) and HB 681 (Cody Vasut). It also noted Sen. Roland Gutierrez's SB 296, filed Nov. 9, which the Legislature's caption says would impose a temporary moratorium, require local option elections and set annual payments to county residents (Texas Scorecard; Texas Legislature Online).

**Today's Signal:** SB 293 goes after the sales tax break. SB 338 goes after the abatement deals counties keep signing, and it uses the grid connection itself to enforce water rules. Sixteen of 31 senators is a majority, and the list includes the Finance chair and a Democrat. That makes SB 338 a likelier GOP-Senate starting point for negotiating with Hinojosa than Gutierrez's moratorium.

### 3. 🏢 Equinix Files for a $500 Million, Four-Story Data Center Near Love Field

- Monday, Nov. 16: DA12 LLC, which shares Equinix's address and lists Equinix Americas president Arquelle Shaw as its contact, filed with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation for DA13-1, a four-story, 372,445-square-foot data center at 1520 West Mockingbird Lane in Dallas (DCD).
- The $500 million project is scheduled for construction from February 2027 to December 2029 on the vacant site of the former Mockingbird Office Park, demolished around 2022, in an industrial area near Love Field (DCD).
- It sits across the street from 1550 West Mockingbird, where Equinix filed in November 2025 for DA12-1 ($542.8 million, 372,515 square feet) and DA12-2 ($293.7 million). Equinix lists eight data centers in the Dallas area (DCD).

**Today's Signal:** Three buildings on two facing lots add up to more than $1.3 billion in filed construction value on one stretch of Mockingbird Lane. It's an infill build inside Dallas city limits, where the fights so far have been about setbacks and zoning, not about turbines or a ranch community's water.

### 4. 🌬️ Soluna Puts a Nearly $4 Billion Price Tag on Kati 2, Its Wind-Fed Expansion in Willacy County

- Friday, Nov. 13: Soluna Holdings CEO John Belizaire told the RGG Business Journal that Project Kati 2, across the street from its 83 MW Kati 1 near Raymondville, would cost "almost $4 billion" for the buildings and computers (Rio Grande Guardian).
- Kati 2 would draw 166 MW, mostly curtailed power from the neighboring Las Majadas Wind Farm. It would have about 40 permanent staff and need about 1,200 construction workers for an estimated 14-month build (Rio Grande Guardian).
- Belizaire said the investment is more than three times Willacy County's roughly $1.2 billion tax base. Kati 1 pays the county $160,000 a year under its abatement, and Kati 2's payment in lieu of taxes is still being negotiated ahead of a formal Chapter 312 application (Rio Grande Guardian).
- Cooling would be closed-loop direct liquid cooling with water trucked in, and Soluna is targeting groundbreaking in early 2027 and operation in the second quarter of 2028 (Rio Grande Guardian).

**Today's Signal:** Soluna's pitch is power the grid can't take: Las Majadas wind that would otherwise be curtailed because the lines are full. A load sited behind a transmission bottleneck is the opposite of what ERCOT's large-load queue is struggling with. That's a better argument to the next PUC than the size of the check to the county.

### 5. 📐 Oakwell and Cawley Take a 68 MW, $1 Billion Georgetown Data Center to the Planning Commission

- Friday, Nov. 13: Austin-based Oakwell Capital Partners and Dallas-based Cawley Partners are proposing a 68-megawatt, 145,000-square-foot data center in Georgetown, with a capital investment approaching $1 billion (Connect CRE, citing the Austin Business Journal).
- The zoning change going to Georgetown's planning commission this week would allow a building of up to 290,000 square feet for future growth. The developers point to Oncor's construction of a large substation at the site (Connect CRE).
- "Our project sets in place real enforceable guardrails around sound and water and light," said Oakwell's Phillip Niels (Connect CRE). The developers have said it could become the city's largest taxpayer (Hoodline).

**Today's Signal:** The rezoning would allow twice the building being proposed. That's the number for Georgetown's planning commissioners to weigh Tuesday, because the guardrails Niels describes cover sound, water and light, not a second phase's power draw.

### 6. ⚡ A Seoul Lawmaker Says Hinojosa's Win Clouds Korea's First U.S. Investment, a Texas Gas Plant

- Monday, Nov. 16: Rep. Hong Kee-won, vice chairman of the South Korean National Assembly's foreign affairs committee, told The Korea Times that uncertainty has grown over the Texas gas-fired power plant chosen as the first project under Korea's $350 billion U.S. investment commitment. The plant is meant to sell electricity to data centers (The Korea Times).
- "Now, with a Democratic governor-elect in Texas taking a cautious stance on data center expansion, uncertainty has grown over whether even the first project can be commercially viable and attract buyers for its electricity once the plant is built," Hong said, adding that the deal was made without guaranteed offtake (The Korea Times).

**Today's Signal:** A plant built to serve data centers needs data centers to buy from it. Seoul is now asking the question every Texas gas developer pitching "power for AI" will face: who's the buyer if new large loads slow down?

The PUC's large-load dockets saw only a protective-order filing, and the weekend brought no air permit actions or zoning votes in the eight tracked DFW counties.
