# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through August 7, 2026)*

## ⚡ 1. ERCOT & Grid Signals
**ERCOT Officially Misses Its Own Batch Zero Deadline, Files for Good Cause Exception**

- ERCOT confirmed via Market Notice M-A080326-01 that it will not meet today's Aug. 7 deadline to tell each Transmission and Distribution Service Provider how their Large Load projects are classified under the Batch Zero interconnection study, citing Gov. Abbott's Aug. 3 audit directive (Butler Snow, Aug. 5; K&L Gates, Aug. 4).
- ERCOT says it will instead file a formal "good cause exception" request covering the Batch Zero timeline under Planning Guide Sections 5 and 9, to be taken up at the PUCT's Aug. 20 open meeting (POWER Magazine, Aug. 6).
- A BloombergNEF report out this week estimates the audit-driven pause could delay 49.8 GW of Texas data-center load — nearly 20% of the entire U.S. development pipeline — with cumulative revenue at risk ranging from roughly $8 billion to $15 billion depending on how much of the delayed capacity is AI-related (POWER Magazine, Aug. 6, citing BNEF's Aug. 5 report).
- QTS Data Centers, a Blackstone portfolio company, became the first major operator to publicly back the audit, saying in an Aug. 6 statement that its facilities already meet the transparency standards Abbott is asking for.

> **Today's Signal:** ERCOT's own paperwork just caught up with Monday's political order — the state's largest grid operator can no longer treat the audit as a footnote to its interconnection calendar.

## 🏗️ 2. Data Center Construction & Corporate Activity
**NRG Details Its First "Bring Your Own Power" Plant as Hyperscalers Keep Building Around the Freeze**

- NRG Energy is nearing a roughly 1.2-GW hyperscaler power deal tied to its newly unveiled "Bring Your Own Power" (BYOP) model — a $3.2 billion combined-cycle gas plant serving a Texas data center client directly, with room to expand to 2.4 GW in a later phase (Utility Dive, Aug. 6-7).
- The company is targeting commercial operation in late 2029 and told investors on its Aug. 4 earnings call the deal is the first of "several" similar BYOP projects it plans to develop in Texas.
- Meta and BlackRock's $14 billion, 1-gigawatt El Paso data center venture — announced July 28, with BlackRock-managed funds owning 80% and Meta leasing back the entire campus — continues to be cited by law firms and analysts this week as evidence that hyperscaler land and power deals haven't slowed despite the state-level freeze.
- No new Texas land-acquisition filing surfaced today beyond continued reporting on already-announced projects (Meta/BlackRock, NRG); watch for fresh disclosures as more Q2 earnings calls wrap this month.

> **Today's Signal:** NRG isn't waiting on ERCOT's queue at all — its BYOP plant is a direct bet that dedicated, off-queue generation is now the fastest way to serve a hyperscaler client in Texas.

## 🌿 3. Environmental & Air-Permit Activity (TCEQ)
**No New DFW-County Filing Today, But the Behind-the-Meter Pattern Keeps Widening**

- No new Ellis, Collin, Johnson, Denton, Wise, Tarrant, Parker, or Hood County TCEQ draft permit or public notice tied to a data center or gas plant was confirmed as newly dated today.
- TCEQ's Title V Air Operating Permits current-applications list was last refreshed Aug. 2, 2026, with no new large-load-linked filing added since (TCEQ, Aug. 2).
- The withdrawal of NRG's air permit for the 1,200-MW Tolar Power Center in Hood County — filed June 18 after the project drew more than 11,000 public comments — remains the most recent DFW-area permit action tied to a data center's on-site generation plans (Oil & Gas Watch; Fort Worth Star-Telegram).
- The Texas Tribune's July 9 finding that at least 15 Texas data centers are pursuing their own behind-the-meter gas plants continues to be cited this week by national outlets covering the audit fallout, underscoring that much of that activity sits outside standard TCEQ review timelines.

> **Today's Signal:** TCEQ's public dockets are quiet today, but that's part of the pattern — the behind-the-meter plants drawing the most scrutiny nationally are largely permitted through faster, less visible tracks than a Title V filing.

## 🏛️ 4. Land-Use & Zoning Developments
**A Fourth DFW-Area City Moves to Pump the Brakes**

- The City of Lavon (Collin County) approved a 90-day moratorium on new data center projects this morning, giving council and residents time to study a proposed roughly 79-acre hyperscale facility before it can advance; the pause takes effect in November and can be extended another 90 days (WFAA, Aug. 7).
- Denton's City Council voted Aug. 5 to move forward with its own potential moratorium on new data center facilities, joining Fort Worth, Westlake/Keller, and now Lavon as North Texas jurisdictions actively slowing or contesting projects this month (Dallas Business Journal, Aug. 5).
- Fort Worth remains paused heading into its Aug. 11 council vote, with staff still evaluating how Abbott's statewide audit order affects the city's own zoning and moratorium decision (Fort Worth Report, Aug. 4).
- Westlake's approved 87.9-acre site plan near Highway 377 still awaits a final Town Council vote, with the neighboring Keller dispute over the project's proximity to homes unresolved.

> **Today's Signal:** Lavon makes it four DFW-area governments now moving on data-center zoning inside a single week — local pushback has become the default response, not the exception.

## 🔌 5. Power-First Strategies & On-Site Generation
**BYOP and Behind-the-Meter Both Frame Themselves as the Way Around the State's Own Freeze**

- NRG's $3.2 billion Bring Your Own Power plant (Section 2) is the clearest example yet of a hyperscaler-aligned developer building dedicated, non-ERCOT generation specifically to avoid the interconnection queue Abbott just froze.
- Legal analysts note that Abbott's Aug. 3 audit technically targets projects in ERCOT's interconnection queue, meaning fully behind-the-meter projects — like the Chevron-Microsoft data center cited by Forbes this week — are, for now, positioned to keep moving while grid-tied projects wait (Forbes, Aug. 6).
- That said, the PUCT's late-July ruling in Docket 59220, which affirmed the commission's curtailment authority over data centers co-located with their own generation, is a reminder that going behind the meter reduces queue exposure but doesn't fully remove a project from state oversight.
- Williams Companies' national build-out of six behind-the-meter gas plants for data centers, four of them serving Meta facilities, continues to be cited as the template other Texas-facing developers are watching (WIRED, Aug. 6).

> **Today's Signal:** With ERCOT's own queue stalled by its own audit, every version of "build your own power" — NRG's BYOP plant, Chevron-Microsoft's co-location, Williams' behind-the-meter fleet — is being pitched as the workaround, even though state regulators have already signaled they intend to keep a hand in all of them.
