## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🏛️ Abbott Calls His Data Center Pause a Success as Democrats Demand a Special Session

- During a Fort Worth news conference recapped on CBS Texas Sunday, Gov. Greg Abbott said the guardrails he ordered — no water or grid strain on local communities, lower electricity costs, no disruption to rural neighborhoods — are "now universally adopted across the state of Texas," meaning no new data centers are coming online while every existing one falls in line.
- Abbott defended not calling a special session by pointing to the PUC's own data-collection effort: grid operators seeking to add load returned "less than 10 percent" of the requested water- and power-usage information, which he compared to solving a 100-piece puzzle with only nine pieces.
- State Rep. Gina Hinojosa, D-Austin — Abbott's 2026 gubernatorial opponent — countered during a Dallas campaign stop that residents "are being taken advantage of" absent lawmaker-approved rules, and renewed her call for Abbott to convene a special session before the regular session convenes in January.
- The exchange aired the same weekend ABC News' "This Week" featured Abbott discussing the data center backlash as a live issue in Texas' midterm races.

**Today's Signal:** Abbott's own defense of the pause is now explicitly tied to the incomplete PUC survey — under 10% response — which means the audit's data problem, not new legislation, is what he's using to justify running out the clock until January.

### 2. 📈 Hut 8's $50 Billion Nvidia Bet Lifts Its Stock as Cipher Digital Slides on Rate Jitters

- Hut 8 shares jumped roughly 6% after the company disclosed long-term data center leases with Nvidia covering its entire 1-gigawatt Texas campus, with base terms and renewal options that could total up to $50 billion over 30 years, per a markets recap from TIKR.
- The company also confirmed its Beacon Point campus in Nueces County is now fully commercialized after a second 15-year, 352-megawatt lease with the same investment-grade tenant, bringing that single site's contracted capacity to 704 megawatts.
- Wall Street followed with fresh price-target increases — Morgan Stanley initiated coverage with an Overweight rating and a $263 target, and Benchmark and Piper Sandler both raised theirs — arguing Hut 8's shift from bitcoin miner to "power-first" AI infrastructure company is being underpriced.
- Cipher Digital's stock fell about 8% the same week; the company's Texas-focused commentary pointed to rising Treasury yields pressuring long-duration data center bets sector-wide, plus "fresh uncertainty" as Texas regulators scrutinize how facilities like its Black Pearl site get approved and connected to the grid.

**Today's Signal:** Investors are now pricing Texas data center leases and Texas regulatory risk as the same trade — the same week one Nasdaq miner's stock jumps on a $50 billion Nvidia lease, another is sliding partly because of the state's own audit-driven interconnection uncertainty.

### 3. 🚫 Athens Moves to Ban Data Centers and Battery Storage Outright

- City staff in Athens, about 70 miles southeast of Dallas, are now recommending that data centers and battery energy storage systems be prohibited in every zoning district in the city, according to KETK — a reversal from an earlier draft that would have allowed the facilities in industrial zones under a special-use permit.
- The Athens City Council is expected to decide on the recommendation at Monday's meeting, capping a process that community organizer LeAnn Embry said began after she discovered a proposed facility near her home back in March.
- The city said in a statement it "hears" residents' concerns about electricity costs and property values but must still "proceed according to our ordinances," while Embry said neighbors are prepared to keep fighting regardless of Monday's outcome.
- Athens would join Chandler, which passed an outright data center ban on Aug. 12, in choosing prohibition over the special-use-permit approach several other DFW-area cities have tried first.

**Today's Signal:** Athens would join a small but growing list of Texas cities moving from special-use conditions to outright zoning bans — a harder line than the "guardrails and guidelines" approach Gov. Abbott has been touting statewide.

### 4. ⚡ ERCOT's Solar-and-Battery Bet Paid Off During July's Heat Peak, New Analysis Shows

- When ERCOT's demand hit a record on July 22 amid extreme heat, the grid needed no emergency actions and real-time prices held around $29 per megawatt-hour — a sharp contrast to PJM's early-July peak, where prices spiked above $2,000/MWh, according to E&E News reporting recapped by MarketScale.
- Solar was generating roughly a third of ERCOT's power at the 6 p.m. peak, with the state's battery fleet providing the "peak help" that PJM and the Southwest Power Pool — which lack comparable solar-and-storage buildout — couldn't replicate during their own record-breaking heat events.
- The analysis frames the gap as a live variable for AI data center site selection: large-load developers are now being urged to model their expected demand against 2026 heat-week pricing in whichever grid they're considering, rather than treating heat risk as background weather.

**Today's Signal:** The same grid regulators are auditing for data center interconnection risk is also, on its own performance numbers, becoming one of Texas's selling points to the industry — a tension the audit's December deadline will have to reckon with.

### 5. 🌾 A Wichita Falls Ranchland Corridor Becomes the State's Next Data Center Front

- Times Record News opened a two-part series Sunday documenting how stretches of U.S. Highway 287 west of Wichita Falls — plains once grazed by cattle — are being leveled for data center construction alongside the solar and battery farms built to power them.
- The outlet frames Wichita Falls as the newest entrant in a now-familiar Texas pattern: rural land near transmission corridors converting directly to data-center and power infrastructure, ahead of any local zoning fight reaching the DFW-area courts or commissioner agendas seen elsewhere this summer.

**Today's Signal:** Every rural corridor that gets developed before a zoning fight breaks out is one Athens-style council vote that never gets the chance to happen — worth watching whether Wichita Falls' second installment covers any local pushback at all.
