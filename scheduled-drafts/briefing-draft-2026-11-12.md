# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 12, 2026

(Covering Wednesday, November 11 – Thursday, November 12, 2026)

**Top Story:** ERCOT CEO Pablo Vegas says he'll ask the Legislature to let the grid operator order data centers to cut power at the first stage of a grid emergency, or even before one is declared, instead of waiting until stage two as the rules now require.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. ⚡ ERCOT's CEO Wants to Make Data Centers Cut Back Before the Grid Hits an Emergency

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: Speaking at the BloombergNEF Summit in Houston, Vegas said he will seek legislative approval to require data centers to reduce load at level one of ERCOT's three-level emergency process, or earlier. Today they can't be asked to cut until level two (Quartz and Briefs Finance, citing Bloomberg).
- "The practical realities of curtailing large amounts of customers like data centers... really requires us to think about pulling that up a little bit earlier into the process," Vegas said. "We really don't want to impact residential and everyday businesses if we get into a scarcity event in the state." He said the hyperscalers are "on board" and that ERCOT can work with lawmakers "if the legislature agrees" (Quartz).
- ERCOT has never directed a data center to curtail under SB 6, the 2025 law that lets it order large loads to cut with a day's notice. Vegas said data centers cut back on their own during last winter's severe weather (Quartz; Briefs Finance).
- At the same conference, Microsoft cloud executive Noelle Walsh said the company will "pay our own way" for power rather than push costs onto other customers. She pointed to Project Kilby, the behind-the-meter power-and-data site in West Texas that Microsoft is developing with Chevron and Joulent, which Chevron still targets to start running in 2028 and reach full operations in 2031 (Finimize, citing Bloomberg).
- The cost side came up separately Wednesday night: NBC 5 Investigates reported that the PUC is writing consumer-protection rules due in December and is considering using security that data centers forfeit when they drop out of the queue to offset other customers' costs. PUC Chairman Thomas Gleeson told lawmakers the goal is "being able to show them that they're being protected." The PUC declined an interview (NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth).

**Today's Signal:** Vegas is asking for this from a Legislature where Democrats now hold the House and a Democratic lieutenant governor will preside over the Senate. Earlier curtailment for data centers fits Gov.-elect Hinojosa's promise to protect households from data center costs, so the fight in January may be less over whether to do it than over how early ERCOT gets to make the call.

### 2. 🗂️ Eight Panhandle Cities Voted Down SPS's Data Center Tariff, and the Utility Is Taking It to the PUC

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: Southwestern Public Service Co. asked the PUC to review resolutions from eight cities in its Texas service area that denied its large-load customer tariff: Pampa (Oct. 12), Hale Center (Oct. 13), Abernathy and Dimmitt (Oct. 19), Ralls, Canyon and Borger (Oct. 20), and New Deal (Oct. 26) (PUC Interchange, Docket 60332, Item 33).
- SPS says it filed the same tariff with its original-jurisdiction cities "out of an abundance of caution" when it applied to the PUC on Sept. 25. It is treating cities that haven't acted as having denied it too, and wants the PUC to hear all the appeals de novo, consolidated into Docket 60332 (PUC Interchange).
- Also Wednesday, intervenor AXM sent SPS its first set of information requests, and AXM and Texas Industrial Energy Consumers filed protective-order certifications so they can see confidential material (PUC Interchange, Items 28–32).
- In Project 59999, the rulemaking on a standard large-load interconnection agreement, Weatherford-based Green Recursive Utility Service filed full comments on the PUC's proposed agreement Wednesday, two days after its Nov. 9 rule petition, and asked for a public hearing. Comments aren't due until Dec. 14 (PUC Interchange, Project 59999, Item 10).

**Today's Signal:** City denials are often how a Texas rate fight gets to the PUC rather than the end of it, and SPS's own filing treats them that way. Still, a single docket now has the utility, consumer advocates, industrial customers, the Data Center Coalition and eight Panhandle towns in it, so whatever terms come out will be the template for data center hookups outside ERCOT.

### 3. 🏘️ Georgetown Neighbors Line Up Against a 68 MW Data Center Before Tuesday's Zoning Hearing

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: KVUE reported that a developer is seeking a zoning change for Inner Loop Exchange, a proposed data center on about 28 acres near Northeast Inner Loop and East University Avenue. The change would allow up to 290,000 square feet, where current commercial zoning caps buildings at 5,000 square feet.
- City staff found the proposal meets three of five criteria for both a zoning amendment and a planned unit development, and flagged concerns about its size and fit with the neighborhood (KVUE).
- Flyers the developer has handed out describe a 68 MW facility with closed-loop cooling that would take power from Oncor rather than Georgetown's municipal utility. KVUE said the developer didn't respond to requests for comment (KVUE).
- HOA presidents Gus Avila (Churchill Farms) and Boz Sabeti (Riverhill) said they don't oppose data centers in industrial zones, but not across from homes and schools. The Planning and Zoning Commission hearing is Nov. 17 at 6 p.m. (KVUE).

**Today's Signal:** At 68 MW on 28 acres, this is the kind of in-town project a statewide permit pause doesn't touch. Whether Georgetown approves it comes down to a zoning vote, which is the local-control lever the Mizani bill would make mandatory everywhere.

### 4. 🛢️ New Era Says Its Odessa Campus Gets Power Outside Batch Zero, and Phase 2's Air Permit Is Filed

- Thursday, Nov. 12: New Era Energy & Digital (Nasdaq: NUAI) said in its third-quarter update that Phase 1 of its Texas Critical Data Centers campus in Odessa and Ector County, up to 207 MW of firm power under a 20-year agreement with Vistra's Luminant, uses an existing registered behind-the-meter private-use network outside ERCOT's Batch Zero process (New Era via GlobeNewswire).
- Phase 2 would add about 550 MW of new behind-the-meter generation with Thunderhead Energy Solutions, and its air permit application has been filed. The release doesn't name the fuel (New Era).
- The company owns 493 acres with 99 more under contract, says site preparation is underway, expects power in the third quarter of 2027, and is in advanced talks on a Phase 1 lease with an unnamed tenant under a nonbinding letter of intent. It reported $187 million in cash as of Oct. 31, $138 million of it restricted (New Era).

**Today's Signal:** This is the second off-queue power story in three days, after AZIO's Live Oak County claim. Both rely on TCEQ air authorizations to grow, which puts the next set of decisions with an agency under Abbott's data center permit pause and answering to a new governor in January.

### 5. 📉 Morgan Stanley Cuts the Odds That Galaxy's Next Texas Data Centers Get Power

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: TheStreet reported that Morgan Stanley analyst Stephen Byrd cut his price target on Galaxy Digital from $43 to $27 in a Nov. 9 note, citing Democrat Gina Hinojosa's win in the governor's race.
- The bank lowered the probability that Galaxy's Helios II and Merlin I projects, with 904 MW of gross capacity, connect to power from 95% to 75%, and raised the discount rate on Galaxy's data center business from 9% to 10%. It kept an Overweight rating (TheStreet).
- Helios I has delivered 133 MW to CoreWeave in its first phase, and Galaxy raised $3.5 billion in notes for Phase II, with data hall deliveries starting in the second quarter of 2027 (TheStreet).

**Today's Signal:** A 20-point haircut on "will it get power" is a Wall Street bank pricing the election into a specific Texas project, and it lands the same week CoreWeave said its own Texas capacity is mostly safe. Investors are drawing a line between sites already permitted and connected and those still waiting.

### 6. 🗣️ Residents Near Data Centers, and the Incoming Ag Commissioner, Say What They Want From 2027

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: Houston Public Media checked back with Melissa Burnett, who lives next to a data center in Brazoria County and said she has seen no change since Gov. Abbott's August pause. Megan Atchison, a conservative who opposes a planned data center near Wichita Falls, said she wants the Legislature to pass zoning limits: "They are a massive industrial building. They belong with other industrial buildings" (Houston Public Media).
- Rice political scientist Mark Jones said rural Republican senators "will be under a lot of pressure" to back Democratic data center bills that seem reasonable. The Data Center Coalition's Jess Heck said the industry will keep working with residents and local leaders (Houston Public Media).
- In a Texas Standard interview the same day, Agriculture Commissioner-elect Clayton Tucker said his agency's planned data center impact studies would cover air, water, soil, noise and light pollution, and would be made fully public. He said only about 20% of data centers provide any information and that "a moratorium is the best option" (Texas Standard).

**Today's Signal:** The residents quoted aren't asking for data centers to go away. They want zoning, which is a narrower request than Tucker's moratorium and one rural Republicans can vote for. Those senators are the votes that decide what passes.

### 7. 🏭 Modine Starts Making Data Center Chillers in Grand Prairie

- Wednesday, Nov. 11: Modine (NYSE: MOD) began commercial production at a 682,000-square-foot Airedale chiller plant in Grand Prairie, which it expects to become its largest Airedale plant worldwide. It makes 2 MW and 3 MW free-cooling chillers for data centers (Modine via PR Newswire; Securities.io).
- The first line started this month. Modine is aiming for four 2 MW lines by the end of its fiscal year and plans to go from one shift to three (Securities.io).

**Today's Signal:** This is the data center economy DFW is already getting without hosting the server halls. A factory that ships chillers to data centers in every state isn't waiting on ERCOT's large-load queue or a TCEQ permit, which makes it one of the few data center bets in Texas the current fight doesn't touch.

No new Texas Tribune data center story after Nov. 10, no ERCOT release after its Nov. 10 grid-alert announcement, and still no TCEQ release since Oct. 21. Nothing turned up on air permits or zoning votes in the eight tracked DFW counties, apart from Westlake's meeting tonight.
