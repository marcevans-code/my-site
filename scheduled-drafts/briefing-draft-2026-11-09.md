# ⭐ Morning Briefing — November 9, 2026

(Covering Saturday, November 7 – Monday, November 9, 2026)

**Top Story:** SpaceX is asking its own company town for a tax break on a $24 billion "super-compute facility" at Starbase, powered by a 748-megawatt gas plant whose air permit application has gone to TCEQ. Over the weekend, CoreWeave wrote to Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa promising to pay its own grid costs in Texas.

---

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🚀 SpaceX Seeks a Starbase Tax Break for a $24 Billion Supercomputer and a 748 MW Gas Plant

- SpaceX has asked the City of Starbase for a property tax abatement on a project the city's public hearing notice describes this way: SpaceX "proposes to construct, own, and operate a gas-fired power plant and super-compute facility." The abatement would also let the project use the Cameron County Enterprise Zone (CoinDesk, Monday; RGV Business Journal, Sunday).
- The project is estimated at $24 billion and would be built in phases. The power plant would use 35 natural gas turbines to produce 748 megawatts. Trinity Consultants has filed an application with TCEQ on SpaceX's behalf for a new air pollution source (RGV Business Journal).
- Starbase city commissioners, some of whom are SpaceX employees, will hold the public hearing Dec. 9 at 7 p.m. at city hall. The notice doesn't say why SpaceX wants its own plant (RGV Business Journal, CoinDesk).
- Friday, Nov. 6: the San Antonio Current reported that Hinojosa told reporters Thursday she had asked state officials to hold off on any sale of Boca Chica State Park to SpaceX until she takes office. She said the Texas Parks and Wildlife Commission pulled the item from Thursday's agenda at her request. Texas Monthly reported in October that the department was considering selling about 1,300 acres of the park so SpaceX could expand Starbase.

**Today's Signal:** SpaceX is trying the power-first route: build the gas plant and the compute together, behind its own fence. But the air permit still has to go through TCEQ while Abbott's pause on data center permits is in effect, and Hinojosa has already shown with the Boca Chica item that she'll use her influence on Musk's Texas plans before she's sworn in.

### 2. ✉️ CoreWeave Writes to Hinojosa: It Will Pay for Its Own Substations

- In a letter dated Saturday, Nov. 7, CoreWeave CEO Michael Intrator congratulated Hinojosa and said the company "strongly support[s]" her principle that data center growth "must work for Texas." He offered to work with her transition team on "responsible completion of permitted projects and on clear, durable standards for future development" (Investing.com via Yahoo Finance; Stocktwits).
- CoreWeave pledged to pay for the infrastructure that connects its sites to the grid, including substations and transmission upgrades, so neighboring households don't pay for it. It said new capacity will use closed-loop liquid cooling, and that daily water use is "comparable to, or lower than, a commercial office building of similar size" (Stocktwits).
- The letter says communities that make its operations possible "should be able to share in the benefits of that growth" without shouldering the costs. It names CoreWeave's Texas sites in Austin, Denton, Dickens County, Plano, Richardson and Round Rock. It also welcomes what it calls Hinojosa's clarification that already-permitted projects can proceed (Stocktwits).
- Bloomberg's story on the letter ran Monday in the Taipei Times. It noted Hinojosa told CNN Thursday that the state's relationship with data centers has to be a partnership that benefits Texans.
- CoreWeave stock closed up more than 6% Friday, before the letter, but was still down 35% for the year (Stocktwits). The company reports third-quarter results Tuesday, Nov. 10, with a call at 5 p.m. Eastern (CoreWeave investor relations).

**Today's Signal:** CoreWeave's pledges — paying for its own grid hookups, protecting water, letting communities share the benefits — track the conditions Hinojosa says new projects should meet. That suggests companies would rather sign on to her terms now than wait for the Legislature to write them. Tuesday's earnings call will be the first chance to hear how many of CoreWeave's Texas sites are already permitted.

### 3. 📜 Bill Filing Opens Today as Senfronia Thompson Claims the Speaker's Votes

- The filing window for the 2027 session opens Monday, Nov. 9, and runs through March 12. The 90th Legislature convenes Jan. 12 (Texas Tribune, via the Gilmer Mirror). The Tribune notes that Democrats broadly campaigned on tighter guardrails for data center development.
- Rep. Senfronia Thompson, D-Houston, announced Friday that she has enough votes to become House speaker, replacing Dustin Burrows. Republicans still hold the Senate, 19-12, with Democratic Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin presiding (Texas Tribune; KXAN).
- In a KPRC Click2Houston preview Sunday, Thompson said residents bear data center infrastructure costs they "don't have a choice to say no" to. Rep. Cody Vasut, R-Angleton, said he will bring back a bill giving counties more say over noise, public health and safety at large developments, including data centers, in unincorporated areas. "When you develop in the county, it's been viewed kind of as the Wild West," he said. Sen. Paul Bettencourt, R-Houston, said the rules should be set statewide rather than town by town.
- A CNN report Saturday (via KVIA) said the pro-Talarico super PAC Lone Star Rising sent mail to voters within five miles of proposed or approved data centers in 55 counties. It also reported that James Talarico carried Kaufman County, southeast of Dallas, by about a point. The county has two large data center projects, and Forney, its largest city, is moving to ban new ones.

**Today's Signal:** The likely next speaker is also the House's loudest voice on who pays for data center infrastructure. Vasut's county bill has a Republican author, and Bettencourt wants one statewide rule. That gives the Senate a version of local control it could accept, as long as one set of rules covers the whole state.

### 4. ⏸️ The PUC Pushes All Five Batch Zero Complaints to Nov. 30, as ERCOT's Data Shows Projects Bunched Just Under 75 MW

- Friday, Nov. 6: the PUC issued orders continuing the abatement in all five Batch Zero complaints against ERCOT, from Medina County Power NR I (Docket 60356), Rowan Stampede (60396), Mesquite H2 (60410), ACS DC Hicks Field Road (60425) and Skybox Datacenters (60431) (PUC Interchange).
- In the Skybox order, the administrative law judge wrote that commissioners discussed at the Nov. 5 open meeting "the need for additional time to consider the issues." Because Thanksgiving falls shortly after the next open meeting on Nov. 19, the judge extended the abatement to Nov. 30. The earlier pause ran to Nov. 10 (Docket 60431, Item 18).
- ERCOT's preliminary data center survey summary, presented to commissioners Nov. 5, shows how many smaller projects sit just under the 75 MW large-load line. Of 186 "medium loads" reviewed, 143 (about 77%) are between 50 and 75 MW. That band holds 9.8 GW of the medium loads' 11.3 GW (ERCOT).
- Thirty-two medium loads didn't respond to the survey. Twenty of the 22 facilities that reported crypto mining are medium loads. ERCOT is targeting its final report for Dec. 10 (ERCOT).

**Today's Signal:** Projects below 75 MW go to their local utility, not through ERCOT's large-load review, and almost 10 GW of requests sit in the 50–75 MW band. That's the easiest gap for the next Legislature to close, and nothing in the Batch Zero complaints will be decided before December anyway.

### 5. 🐂 A 96 MW Data Center and 180 MW Gas Plant Proposed on Church-Owned Ranch Land in Cottle County

- Friday, Nov. 6: The Salt Lake Tribune reported that a developer called Aetheris is proposing "Project Matter" on Triangle Ranch in rural Cottle County, about 15 miles east of Paducah. A nonprofit owned by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints owns about 100,000 acres of the ranch.
- Phase one would cover 729 acres leased from Farmland Reserve, the church-owned nonprofit, which would lease up to 3,710 acres. With 542 acres from another landowner, future phases could reach 4,252 acres. Plans call for a 96 MW data center and a 180 MW natural gas plant on the leased land. The end user is confidential.
- Aetheris is seeking a property tax abatement of up to 10 years from the county. The story said a public hearing is set for Monday, Nov. 9, at the Paducah courthouse. County Judge Karl Holloway said residents have "a lot of concerns."
- In October letters, Aetheris said the project would use no local water or electricity, bring in all its water from outside the county, and limit noise, light and heat. It also promised money for county roads, emergency services and schools. The area is in drought or "abnormal" dryness, according to the U.S. Drought Monitor.

**Today's Signal:** A project that supplies its own gas and water can promise a small county almost everything. What it still needs is the tax break, and Cottle County, with about 1,000 people in Paducah, has to decide on that while state permits are paused.

### 6. 🎓 Texas A&M's RELLIS Campus Gets a 73 MW AI Compute Center

- Saturday, Nov. 7: NBC 5 reported that Austin-based ThisWay Global is building a 73-megawatt Research and AI Compute Center on 25 acres of Texas A&M's RELLIS campus in Bryan, focused on "sovereign AI." The company plans to start computing there by June 2027. It will also run an eight-week course for eligible students and community members.
- The same report covered Dallas integrator GDT's new "AI factory" lab, where businesses can test AI applications on NVIDIA GPUs before buying their own systems.
- In a separate NBC 5 interview Saturday, economist Jorge Barro of the Texas Real Estate Research Center said Texas is on track to overtake Northern Virginia as the world's largest data center market by 2030. He said hyperscalers' heavy borrowing for data centers "has driven interest rates up."

**Today's Signal:** At 73 MW, the RELLIS center is just under ERCOT's 75 MW large-load threshold, the same range story 4's data shows so many projects choosing. A university campus is a friendlier host than most rural counties, but next session's rules for that size range will apply here too.

No new TCEQ news releases since Oct. 21, and no data center air permit actions or zoning votes turned up in the eight tracked DFW counties over the weekend. ERCOT's news releases still stop at Sept. 21, Westlake has not yet voted on the Circle T settlement, and there's still no report on Bastrop's Cedar Creek gas plant.
