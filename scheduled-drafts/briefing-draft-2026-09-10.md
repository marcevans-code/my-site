# ⭐ Morning Briefing — Texas & DFW AI Data Centers, Grid, Land Use

*(Latest updates through September 10, 2026)*

**Top Story:** New reporting from KERA shows Hood County conservatives — including a former Republican state Senate candidate — breaking from the GOP over Gov. Abbott's handling of the data-center boom, turning a local land-use fight into a live issue in this year's governor's and U.S. Senate races.

## 📰 Today's News

### 1. 🗳️ The Data Center Backlash Reaches the Ballot Box

- KERA News reported Sept. 10 that a handful of longtime Hood County Republicans — including Granbury farmer Cheryl Shadden, who has spent two years protesting the crypto-mining data center across the road from her property — say they're voting Democrat or sitting out the midterms over the party's handling of the data-center boom. Shadden appeared in a campaign ad for Democratic gubernatorial candidate Gina Hinojosa, who is challenging Gov. Greg Abbott.
- Rena Schroeder, who ran as a Republican for Texas Senate District 22 (which includes Hood County) and says she lost the March primary largely over her opposition to data centers, has now endorsed the Democrat running against David Cook, the Abbott-endorsed winner of that race, according to KERA.
- Abbott, in a Fox News interview cited by KERA, defended "pressing pause on any new data center approval in the state" and setting new "guidelines and guardrails" for the industry — the same audit that, per a Sept. 9 HPCwire report, is now working through a first batch of roughly 250 to 300 projects representing about 200 GW of the state's 474 GW in pending interconnection requests.
- A Texas Politics Project survey found more than half of Texans oppose data-center construction near their home, and pollster Joshua Blank told KERA that if rural Republican voters sit out the election over the issue, "the GOP's firewall in Texas becomes a lot less formidable." The same rift is shaping the Ken Paxton–James Talarico U.S. Senate race and drew same-day commentary from The Dallas Morning News and The New York Times.

**Today's Signal:** This is the clearest sign yet that the data-center fight has moved from zoning boards and TCEQ hearings into a real electoral threat to the party that spent the last year courting the industry — both the governor's race and the Paxton-Talarico Senate race now have data centers as a live wedge issue, not just local news.

### 2. 📣 A Tarrant County Commissioner Floats Writing State Rules Before the 2027 Session

- Tarrant County Commissioner Matt Krause hosted a town hall Tuesday night, Sept. 8, at the Gary Fickes Northeast Courthouse in Hurst, drawing 23 speakers — including residents of the Lakes at Marshall Ridge HOA in Keller, a neighborhood next to Westlake's contested data-center project — according to the Fort Worth Report.
- Krause said the data-center debate "transcends partisan sides" and floated a regulatory split in which the Texas Legislature would set "standard criteria" for projects statewide — on water use, power sourcing, and noise — while leaving enforcement discretion to individual cities; he said Tarrant County could build a 2027 legislative agenda item around it.
- Speakers pressed Krause on whether developers, operators, and officials can be trusted, questioned tax incentives for data-center projects (which Krause said he'd expect the Legislature to reconsider), and argued new developments should have to supply their own power and water rather than draw on public infrastructure.
- Krause noted the Fort Worth City Council voted last month to require data-center applications to comply with Gov. Abbott's statewide audit criteria before advancing.

**Today's Signal:** Krause's idea — state sets the floor, cities fill in the rest — is the same state/local split Abbott's own audit already assumes; whether Austin actually writes it into law next session is the open question, not whether local officials want it.

### 3. 🏗️ New Dallas-Area Deal, and Fresh Amazon and Anthropic Filings Surface

- Northampton Capital Partners and Dallas-based Provident Data Centers announced a joint venture to build a 54-megawatt data center in the North Dallas Corridor of the DFW Metroplex, targeted to come online in late 2027; the exact site hasn't been disclosed, according to Data Center Dynamics. The two firms also agreed to a framework for additional sites in other markets.
- A monthly industry roundup published Sept. 9 by data-center intelligence firm Aterio reports that Amazon Web Services has filed new permits expanding its Project Eagle site in Boling from four to ten planned buildings across 2,700 acres — a reported $3 billion investment, with one building already under construction.
- The same Aterio report identifies Anthropic as the reported end user at Nexus Data Centers' Hubbard Campus in Hill County, backed by Google financial guarantees and chip-supply support, with Morgan Stanley reportedly exploring roughly $15 billion in financing for the site.

**Today's Signal:** None of these three sites is in the DFW-core counties this briefing tracks most closely, but together they show hyperscaler and AI-lab money still moving into Texas even as the politics around the industry get harder.

Quiet today on TCEQ air-permit activity in the eight tracked counties beyond what's already on the record, and no new behind-the-meter generation announcements surfaced.
